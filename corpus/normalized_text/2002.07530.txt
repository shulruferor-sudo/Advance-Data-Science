# improved optimistic algorithms for logistic bandits

louis faury  $^{1,2,*}$  marc abeille  $^{1,*}$  clément calauzénes  $^{1}$  olivier fercoq  $^{2}$

abstract. the generalized linear bandit framework has attracted a lot of attention in recent years by extending the well-understood linear setting and allowing to model richer reward structures. it notably covers the logistic model, widely used when rewards are binary. for logistic bandits, the frequentist regret guarantees of existing algorithms are  $\tilde{\mathcal{o}} (\kappa \sqrt{t})$ , where  $\kappa$  is a problem-dependent constant. unfortunately,  $\kappa$  can be arbitrarily large as it scales exponentially with the size of the decision set. this may lead to significantly loose regret bounds and poor empirical performance. in this work, we study the logistic bandit with a focus on the prohibitive dependencies introduced by  $\kappa$ . we propose a new optimistic algorithm based on a finer examination of the non-linearities of the reward function. we show that it enjoys a  $\tilde{\mathcal{o}} (\sqrt{t})$  regret with no dependency in  $\kappa$ , but for a second order term. our analysis is based on a new tail-inequality for self-normalized martingales, of independent interest.

# introduction

parametric stochastic bandits is a framework for sequential decision making where the reward distributions associated to each arm are assumed to share a structured relationship through a common unknown parameter. it extends the standard multi-armed bandit framework and allows one to address the exploration-exploitation dilemma in settings with large or infinite action space. linear bandits (lbs) are the most famous instance of parametrized bandits, where the value of an arm is given as the inner product between the arm feature vector and the unknown parameter. while the theoretical challenges in lbs are relatively well understood and addressed (see (dani et al., 2008; rusmevichientong and tsitsiklis, 2010; abbasi-yadkori et al., 2011; abeille et al., 2017) and references therein), their practical interest is limited by the linear structure of the reward, which may fail to model real-world problems. as a result, extending lbs to allow for richer reward structures and go beyond linearity has attracted a lot of attention from the bandit community in recent years. to this end, two main approaches have been investigated. following valko et al. (2013), the linearity of the reward structure has been relaxed to hold only in a reproducing kernel hilbert space. another line of research relies on generalized linear models (glms) to encode non-linearity through a link function. we focus in this work on the second approach.

generalized linear bandits. the use of generalized linear models for the bandit setting was first studied by filippi et al. (2010). they introduced glm-ucb, a generic optimistic algorithm that achieves a  $\tilde{o}(d\sqrt{t})$  frequentist regret. in the finite-arm case, li et al. (2017) proposed supcbglm for which they proved a  $\tilde{o}(\sqrt{d\log k}\sqrt{t})$  regret bound. similar regret guarantees were also demonstrated for thompson sampling, both in the frequentist (abeille et al., 2017) and bayesian (russo and van roy, 2013, 2014; dong and van roy, 2018) settings. in parallel, jun et al. (2017) focused on improving the time and memory complexity of generalized linear bandits (glbs) algorithms while dumitrascu et al. (2018) improved posterior sampling for a bayesian version of thompson sampling in the specific logistic bandits setting.

limitations. at a first glance, existing performance guarantees for glbs seem to coincide with the state-of-the-art regret bounds for lb w.r.t. the dimension  $d$  and the horizon  $t$ . however, a

careful examination of the regret bounds shows that they all depend in an "unpleasant manner on the form of the link function of the glm, and it seems there may be significant room for improvement" (lattimore and szepesvári, 2018, §19.4.5). more in detail, the regrets scale with a multiplicative factor  $\kappa$  which characterizes the degree of non-linearity of the link function. as such, for highly non-linear models,  $\kappa$  can be prohibitively large, which drastically worsens the regret guarantees as well as the practical performances of the algorithms.

logistic bandit. the magnitude of the constant  $\kappa$  is particularly significant for one glb of crucial practical interest: the logistic bandit. in this case, the link function of the glb is the sigmoid function, resulting in a highly non-linear reward model. hence, the associated problem-dependent constant  $\kappa$  is large even in typical instances. while this reduces the interest of existing guarantees for the logistic bandit, previous work suggests that there is room for improvement. in the bayesian setting and under a slightly more specific logistic bandit instance, dong et al. (2019) proposed a refined analysis of thompson sampling. their work suggest that in some problem instances, the impact on the regret of the diameter of the decision set (directly linked to  $\kappa$ ) might be reduced. in the frequentist setting, (filippi et al., 2010, §4.2) conjectured that glm-ucb can be slightly modified in the hope of enjoying an improved regret bound, deflated by a factor  $\kappa^{1/2}$ . to the best of our knowledge, this is still an open question.

contributions. in this work, we consider the logistic bandit problem and explicitly study its dependency with respect to  $\kappa$ . we propose a new non-linear study of optimistic algorithms for the logistic bandit. our main contributions are: 1) we answer positively to the conjecture of filippi et al. (2010) showing that a slightly modified version of glm-ucb enjoys a  $\tilde{o}(d\sqrt{\kappa t})$  frequentist regret (theorem 2). 2) further, we propose a new algorithm with yet better dependencies in  $\kappa$ , showing that it can be pushed in a second-order term. this results in a  $\tilde{o}(d\sqrt{t} + \kappa)$  regret bound (theorem 3). 3) a key ingredient of our analysis is a new bernstein-like inequality for self-normalized martingales, of independent interest (theorem 1).

# 1. preliminaries

notations for any vector  $x \in \mathbb{r}^d$  and any positive definite matrix  $\mathbf{m} \in \mathbb{r}^{d \times d}$ , we will note  $\| x \|_{\mathbf{m}} = \sqrt{x^{\top} \mathbf{m}x}$  the  $\ell^2$ -norm of  $x$  weighted by  $\mathbf{m}$ , and  $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{m}) > 0$  the smallest eigenvalue of  $\mathbf{m}$ . for two symmetric matrices  $\mathbf{a}$  and  $\mathbf{b}$ ,  $\mathbf{a} \succ \mathbf{b}$  means that  $\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b}$  is positive semi-definite. we will denote  $\mathcal{b}_p(d) = \{x \in \mathbb{r}^d : \| x \|_p \leq 1\}$  the  $d$ -dimensional ball of radius 1 under the norm  $\ell^p$ . for two real-valued functions  $f$  and  $g$  of a scalar variable  $t$ , we will use the notation  $f_t = \tilde{\mathcal{o}}_t(g_t)$  to indicate that  $f_t = \mathcal{o}(g_t)$  up to logarithmic factor in  $t$ . for an univariate function  $f$  we will denote  $\dot{f}$  its derivative.

# 1.1. setting

we consider the stochastic contextual bandit problem. at each round  $t$ , the agent observes a context and is presented a set of actions  $\mathcal{x}_t$  (dependent on the context, and potentially infinite). the agent then selects an action  $x_t \in \mathcal{x}_t$  and receives a reward  $r_{t+1}$ . her decision is based on the information gathered until time  $t$ , which can be formally encoded in the filtration  $\mathcal{f}_t := (\mathcal{f}_0, \sigma(\{x_s, r_{s+1}\}_{s=1}^{t-1}))$  where  $\mathcal{f}_0$  represents any prior knowledge. in this paper, we assume that conditionally on the filtration  $\mathcal{f}_t$ , the reward  $r_{t+1}$  is binary, and is drawn from a bernoulli distribution with parameter  $\mu(x_t^\top \theta_*)$ . the fixed but unknown parameter  $\theta_*$  belongs to  $\mathbb{r}^d$ , and  $\mu(x) := (1 + \exp(-x))^{-1}$  is the sigmoid function. formally:

$$
\mathbb {p} \left(r _ {t + 1} = 1 \mid x _ {t}, \mathcal {f} _ {t}\right) = \mu \left(x _ {t} ^ {\top} \theta_ {*}\right) \tag {1}
$$

let  $x_{*}^{t} \coloneqq \arg \max_{x \in \mathcal{x}_{t}} \mu(x^{\top} \theta_{*})$  be the optimal arm. when pulling an arm, the agent suffers an instant pseudo-regret equal to the difference in expectation between the reward of the optimal

arm  $x_{*}^{t}$  and the reward of the played arm  $x_{t}$ . the agent's goal is to minimize the cumulative pseudo-regret up to time  $t$ , defined as:

$$
r _ {t} := \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu \left(\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}\right) - \mu \left(\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t}\right).
$$

following filippi et al. (2010), we work under the subsequent assumptions on the problem structure, necessary for the study of glbs<sup>1</sup>.

assumption 1 (bandit parameter).  $\theta_* \in \theta$  where  $\theta$  is a compact subset of  $\mathbb{r}^d$ . further,  $s \coloneqq \max_{\theta \in \theta} \| \theta \|_2$  is known.

assumption 2 (arm set). let  $\mathcal{x} = \bigcup_{t=1}^{\infty} \mathcal{x}_t$ . for all  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ ,  $\|x\|_2 \leq 1$ .

we let  $l = m = 1/4$  be the upper-bounds on the first and second derivative of the sigmoid function respectively. finally, we formally introduce the parameter  $\kappa$  which quantifies the degree of non-linearity of the sigmoid function over the decision set  $(\mathcal{x}, \theta)$ :

$$
\kappa := \sup  _ {x \in \mathcal {x}, \theta \in \theta} 1 / \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\top} \theta\right). \tag {2}
$$

this key quantity and its impact are discussed in section 2.

# 1.2. reminders on optimistic algorithms

at round  $t$ , for a given estimator  $\theta_t$  of  $\theta_*$  and a given exploration bonus  $\epsilon_t(x)$ , we consider optimistic algorithms that play:

$$
x_{t} = \operatorname *{arg  max}_{x\in \mathcal{x}_{t}}\mu (\theta_{t}^{\mathsf{t}}x) + \epsilon_{t}(x)
$$

we will denote  $\delta^{\mathrm{pred}}(x,\theta_t)\coloneqq \left|\mu (x^\top \theta_*) - \mu (x^\top \theta_t)\right|$  the prediction error of  $\theta_{t}$  at  $x$ . it is known that setting the bonus to be an upper-bound on the prediction error naturally gives a control on the regret. informally:

$$
\delta^ {\operatorname {p r e d}} (x, \theta_ {t}) \leq \epsilon_ {t} (x) \rightarrow r _ {t} \leq 2 \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t} (x _ {t}).
$$

this implication is classical and its proof is given in section c.1 in the supplementary materials. as usual in bandit problems, tighter predictions bounds on  $\delta^{\mathrm{pred}}(x,\theta_t)$  lead to smaller exploration bonus and therefore better regret guarantees, as long as the sequence of bonus can be shown to cumulate sub-linearly. reciprocally, using large bonus leads to over-explorative algorithms and consequently large regret.

# 1.3. maximum likelihood estimate

in the logistic setting, a natural way to compute an estimator for  $\theta_{*}$  given  $\mathcal{f}_t$  derives from the maximum-likelihood principle. at round  $t$ , the regularized log-likelihood (or negative cross-entropy loss) can be written as:

$$
\mathcal {l} _ {t} ^ {\lambda} (\theta) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \left[ r _ {s + 1} \log \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta) + (1 - r _ {s + 1}) \log (1 - \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta)) \right] - \frac {\lambda}{2} \| \theta \| _ {2} ^ {2}.
$$

$\mathcal{l}_t^\lambda$  is a strictly concave function of  $\theta$  for  $\lambda > 0$ , and the maximum likelihood estimator is defined as  $\hat{\theta}_t \coloneqq \arg \max_{\theta \in \mathbb{r}^d} \mathcal{l}_t^\lambda(\theta)$ . in what follows, for  $t \geq 1$  and  $\theta \in \mathbb{r}^d$  we define  $g_t(\theta)$  such as:

$$
\nabla_ {\theta} \mathcal {l} _ {t} ^ {\lambda} (\theta) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} r _ {s + 1} x _ {s} - \left(\underbrace {\sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \mu \left(x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta\right) x _ {s} + \lambda \theta} _ {:= g _ {t} (\theta)}\right). \tag {3}
$$

# 2. challenges and contributions

on the scaling of  $\kappa$ . first, we stress the problematic scaling of  $\kappa$  (defined in equation (2)) with respect to the size of the decision set  $\mathcal{x} \times \theta$ . as illustrated in figure 1, the dependency is exponential and hence prohibitive. from the definition of  $\kappa$  and the definition of the sigmoid function, one can easily see that:

$$
\kappa \geq \exp \left(\max  _ {x \in \mathcal {x}} | x ^ {\top} \theta_ {*} |\right). \tag {5}
$$

the quantity  $x^{\mathsf{t}}\theta_{*}$  is directly linked to the probability of receiving a reward when playing  $x$ . as a result, this lower bound stresses that  $\kappa$  will be exponentially large as soon as there exists bad (resp. good) arms  $x$  associated with a low (resp. high) probability of receiving a reward. this is unfortunately the case of most logistic bandit applications. for instance, it stands as the standard for click predictions, since the probability of observing a click is usually low (and hence  $\kappa$  is large). typically, in this setting,  $\mathbb{p}(\mathrm{click}) = 10^{-3}$  and therefore  $\kappa \sim 10^{3}$ . as all existing algorithms display a linear dependency with  $\kappa$  (see table 1), this narrows down the class of problem they can efficiently address. on the theoretical side, this indicates that the current analyses fail to handle the regime where the reward function is significantly non-linear, which was the primary purpose of extending lb to glb. note that (5) is only a lower-bound on  $\kappa$ . in some settings  $\kappa$  can be even larger: for instance when  $\mathcal{x} = \mathcal{b}_2(d)$ , we have  $\kappa \geq \exp(s)$ . even for reasonable values of  $s$ , this has a disastrous impact on the regret bounds.

uniform vs local control over  $\dot{\mu}$ . the presence of  $\kappa$  in existing regret bounds is inherited from the learning difficulties that arise from the logistic regression. namely, when  $\theta_{*}$  is large, repeatedly playing actions that are closely aligned with  $\theta_{*}$  (a region where  $\dot{\mu}$  is close to 0) will almost always lead to the same reward. this makes the estimation of  $\theta_{*}$  in this direction hard. however, this

on filippi et al. (2010)'s conjecture. in their seminal work, filippi et al. (2010) provided a prediction bound scaling as  $\kappa$ , directly impacting the size of the bonus. they however hint, by using an asymptotic argument, that this dependency could be reduced to a  $\sqrt{\kappa}$ . this suggests that a first limitation resides in their concentration tools. to this end, we introduce a novel bernstein-like self-normalized martingale tail-inequality (theorem 1) of potential independent interest. coupled with a generalized self-concordant analysis, we give a formal proof of filippi's asymptotic argument in the finite-time, adaptive-design case (lemma 2). we leverage this refined prediction bound to introduce logistic-ucb-1. we show that it suffers at most a regret in  $\tilde{\mathcal{o}}(d\sqrt{\kappa t})$  (theorem 2), improving previous guarantees by  $\sqrt{\kappa}$ . our novel bernstein inequality, together with the generalized self-concordance property of the log-loss are key ingredients of local analysis, which allows us to compare the derivatives of the sigmoid function at two different points without using  $l$  and  $\kappa^{-1}$ .

dropping the  $\kappa$  dependency. further challenge is to get rid of the remaining  $\sqrt{\kappa}$  factor from the regret. this in turns requires to eliminate it from the bonus of the algorithm. we show that this can be done by pushing  $\kappa$  to a second order term in the prediction bound (lemma 3). coupled with careful algorithmic design, this yields logistic-ucb-2, for which we show a  $\tilde{\mathcal{o}}(d\sqrt{t} + \kappa \log t)$  regret bound (theorem 3), where the dependency in  $\kappa$  is removed from the leading term.

outline of the following sections. section 3 focuses on exhibiting improved upper-bound on prediction errors. we describe our algorithms and their regret bound in section 4. finally, we discuss our results and their implications in section 5.

# 3. improved prediction guarantees

this section focuses on the first challenge of the logistic bandit analysis, and aims to provide tighter prediction bounds for the logistic model. bounding the prediction error relies on building tight confidence sets for  $\theta_{*}$ , and our first contribution is to provide more adapted concentration tools to this end. our new tail-inequality for self-normalized martingale allows to construct such confidence sets with better dependencies with respect to  $\kappa$ .

# 3.1. new tail-inequality for self-normalized martingales

we present here a new, bernstein-like tail inequality for self-normalized vectorial martingales. this inequality extends known results on self-normalized martingales (de la pena et al., 2004; abbasi-yadkori et al., 2011). compared to the concentration inequality from theorem 1 of abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011), its main novelty resides in considering martingale increments that satisfy a bernstein-like condition instead of a sub-gaussian condition. this allows to derive tail-inequalities for martingales "re-normalized" by their quadratic variation.

theorem 1. let  $\{\mathcal{f}_t\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  be a filtration. let  $\{x_t\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  be a stochastic process in  $\mathcal{b}_2(d)$  such that  $x_t$  is  $\mathcal{f}_t$  measurable. let  $\{\varepsilon_t\}_{t=2}^{\infty}$  be a martingale difference sequence such that  $\varepsilon_{t+1}$  is  $\mathcal{f}_{t+1}$  measurable. furthermore, assume that conditionally on  $\mathcal{f}_t$  we have  $|\varepsilon_{t+1}| \leq 1$  almost surely, and note  $\sigma_t^2 \coloneqq \mathbb{e}\left[\varepsilon_{t+1}^2 |\mathcal{f}_t\right]$ . let  $\lambda > 0$  and for any  $t \geq 1$  define:

$$
\mathbf {h} _ {t} := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \sigma_ {s} ^ {2} x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {t} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d}, \qquad s _ {t} := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \varepsilon_ {s + 1} x _ {s}.
$$

then for any  $\delta \in (0,1]$ :

$$
\mathbb {p} \left(\exists t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \geq \frac {\sqrt {\lambda}}{2} + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {\det (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {\frac {1}{2}} \lambda^ {- \frac {d}{2}}}{\delta}\right) + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} d \log (2)\right) \leq \delta .
$$

proof. the proof is deferred to section a in the supplementary materials. it follows the steps of the pseudo-maximization principle introduced in de la pena et al. (2004), used by abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011) for the linear bandit and thoroughly detailed in chapter 20 of lattimore and szepesvári (2018). the main difference in our analysis comes from the fact that we consider another super-martingale, which adds complexity to the analysis.  $\square$

comparison to prior work the closest inequality of this type was derived by abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011) to be used for the linear bandit setting. namely, introducing  $\omega \coloneqq \inf_s\sigma_s^2$ , it can be extracted from their theorem 1 that that with probability at least  $1 - \delta$  for all  $t\geq 1$ :

$$
\left\| s _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \leq \sqrt {2 d \log \left(1 + \frac {\omega t}{\lambda d}\right)}, \tag {6}
$$

where  $\mathbf{v}_t = \sum_{s=1}^{t-1} x_s x_s^t + (\lambda/\omega) \mathbf{i}_d$ . note that this result can be used to derive another high-probability bound on  $\|s_t\|_{\mathbf{h}_t^{-1}}$ . indeed notice that  $\mathbf{h}_t \succeq \omega \mathbf{v}_t$ , which yields that with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
\left\| s _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \leq \frac {1}{\sqrt {\omega}} \sqrt {2 d \log \left(1 + \frac {\omega t}{\lambda d}\right)}. \tag {7}
$$

in contrast the bound given by theorem 1 gives that with high-probability  $\| s_t\|_{\mathbf{h}_t^{-1}} = \mathcal{o}\left(d\log (t)\right)$  which is independent of  $\omega$ . this saves up the multiplicative factor  $1 / \sqrt{\omega}$ , which is potentially very large if some  $\varepsilon_s$  have small conditional variance. however, it is lagging by a  $\sqrt{d\log(t)}$  factor behind the bound provided in (7). this issue can be fixed by simply adjusting the regularization parameter. more precisely, for a given horizon  $t$ , theorem 1 ensures that choosing a regularization parameter  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  yields that on a high-probability event, for all  $t\leq t$ :

$$
\left\| s _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} = \mathcal {o} \left(\sqrt {d \log (t)}\right).
$$

# 3.2. a new confidence set

we now use our new concentration inequality (theorem 1) to derive a confidence set on  $\theta_{*}$  that in turns will lead us to upper bounds on the prediction error. we introduce:

$$
\mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta) := \left\{\theta \in \theta , \left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} (\hat {\theta} _ {t}) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \right\},
$$

with  $g_{t}$  defined in (3),  $\mathbf{h}_t$  in (4), and where

$$
\gamma_ {t} (\delta) := \sqrt {\lambda} (s + \frac {1}{2}) + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d}}{\delta} \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right) ^ {\frac {d}{2}}\right).
$$

a straight-forward application of theorem 1 proves that the sets  $\mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  are confidence sets for  $\theta_*$ .

lemma 1. let  $\delta \in (0,1]$  and

$$
e _ {\delta} := \{\forall t \geq 1, \theta_ {*} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta) \}.
$$

then  $\mathbb{p}(e_{\delta})\geq 1 - \delta$

sketch of proof. we show  $\left\| g_t(\theta_*) - g_t(\hat{\theta}_t)\right\|_{\mathbf{h}_t^{-1}(\theta_*)}\leq \gamma_t(\delta)$  with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ . as  $\nabla \mathcal{l}_t^\lambda (\hat{\theta}_t) = 0$ , we have

$$
g _ {t} (\hat {\theta} _ {t}) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \underbrace {r _ {s + 1} - \mu (\theta_ {*} ^ {\mathsf {t}} x _ {s})} _ {:= \varepsilon_ {s + 1}} x _ {s} - \lambda \theta_ {*}.
$$

this equality is obtained by using the characterization of  $\hat{\theta}_t$  given by the log-loss. by (1),  $\{\varepsilon_{s+1}\}_{s=1}^{\infty}$  are centered bernoulli variables with parameter  $\mu(x_s^\top \theta_*)$ , and variance  $\sigma_s^2 = \mu(x_s^\top \theta_*)(1 - \mu(x_s^\top \theta_*)) = \dot{\mu}(x_s^t \theta_*)$ . theorem 1 leads to the claimed result up to some simple upper bounding. the formal proof is deferred to section b.1 in the supplementary materials.

illustration of confidence sets. we provide here some intuition on how this confidence set helps us improve the prediction error upper-bound. to do so, and for the ease of exposition, we will consider for the remaining of this subsection the case when  $\hat{\theta}_t \in \theta$ . we back our intuition

on a slightly degraded but more comprehensible version of the upper-bound on the prediction error:

$$
\delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq l \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \| \theta - \theta_ {*} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta)}.
$$

the regret guarantees of our algorithms can still be recovered from this cruder upper-bound, up to some multiplicative constants (for the sake of completeness, technical details are deferred to section b.3 in the appendix). the natural counterpart of  $\mathcal{c}_t$  that allows for controlling the second part of this decomposition is a marginally inflated confidence set,

$$
\mathcal {e} _ {t} ^ {\mathrm {n l}} (\delta) := \left\{\theta \in \theta , \left\| \theta - \hat {\theta} _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta)} \leq (1 + 2 s) \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \right\}.
$$

it is important to notice (see figure 2) that  $\mathcal{e}_t^{\mathrm{nl}}(\delta)$  effectively handles the local curvature of the sigmoid function, as the metric  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta)$  is local and depends on  $\theta$ . this results in a confidence set that is not an ellipsoid, and that does not penalize all estimators in the same ways.

using the same tools as for glm-ucb, such as the concentration result reminded in (6), a similar reasoning leads to the confidence set

$$
\mathcal {e} _ {t} ^ {\mathrm {l}} (\delta) := \left\{\theta \in \theta , \left\| \theta - \hat {\theta} _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t}} \leq \kappa \beta_ {t} (\delta) \right\},
$$

where  $\beta_{t}(\delta)$  is a slowly increasing function of  $t$  with similar scaling as  $\gamma_{t}$ . using global bounds on  $\dot{\mu}$  leads to the appearance of  $\kappa$  in  $\mathcal{e}_t^{\mathrm{l}}(\delta)$ , illustrated by the large difference of diameter between the blue and red sets in figure 2. this highlights the fact that the local metric  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta)$  is much better-suited than  $\mathbf{v}_t$  to measure distances between parameters. the intuition laid out in this section underlies the formal improvements on the prediction error bounds we provide in the following.

# 3.3. prediction error bounds

we are now ready to derive our new prediction guarantees, inherited from theorem 1.

we give a first prediction bound obtained by degrading the local information carried by estimators in  $\mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$ . this guarantee is conditioned on the good event  $e_{\delta}$  (introduced in lemma 1), which occurs with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ .

lemma 2. on the event  $e_{\delta}$ , for all  $t \geq 1$ , any  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  and  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq l \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}}.
$$

in term of scaling with  $\kappa$ , note that lemma 2 improves the prediction bounds of filippi et al. (2010) by a  $\sqrt{\kappa}$ . it therefore matches their asymptotic argument, providing its first rigorous proof in finite-time and for the adaptive-design case. the proof is deferred to section b.4 in the supplementary materials.

a more careful treatment of  $\mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  naturally leads to better prediction guarantees, laying the foundations to build logistic-ucb-2. this is detailed by the following lemma.

lemma 3. on the event  $e_{\delta}$ , for all  $t \geq 1$ , any  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  and any  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq (2 + 4 s) \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + (4 + 8 s) m \kappa \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}.
$$

the proof is deferred to section b.5. the strength of this result is that it displays a first-order term that contains only local information about the region of the sigmoid function at hand, through the quantities  $\dot{\mu} (x^{t}\theta)$  and  $\| x\|_{\mathbf{h}_t^{-1}(\theta)}$ . global information (measured through  $m$  and  $\kappa$ ) are pushed into a second order term that vanishes quickly. finally, we anticipate on the fact that the decomposition displayed in lemma 3 is not innocent. in what follows, we will show that both terms cumulate at different rates, the term involving  $\kappa$  becoming an explicit second order term. however, this will require a careful choice of  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t$ , as the bound on  $\delta^{\mathrm{pred}}$  now depends now on  $\theta$  (and therefore so will the associated exploration bonus).

# 4. algorithms and regret bounds

# 4.1. logistic-ucb-1

we introduce an algorithm leveraging lemma 2, henceforth matching the heuristic regret bound conjectured in (filippi et al., 2010, §4.2). we introduce the feasible estimator:

$$
\theta_ {t} ^ {(1)} = \underset {\theta \in \theta} {\arg \min } \left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)}. \tag {8}
$$

this projection step ensures us that  $\theta_t^{(1)}\in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  on the high-probability event  $e_{\delta}$ . further, we define the bonus:

$$
\epsilon_ {t, 1} (x) = l \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}}
$$

we define logistic-ucb-1 as the optimistic algorithm instantiated with  $(\theta_t^{(1)},\epsilon_{t,1}(x))$ , detailed in algorithm 1. its regret guarantees are provided in theorem 2, and improves previous results by  $\sqrt{\kappa}$ .

theorem 2 (regret of logistic-ucb-1). with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(1)} \leq c _ {1} l \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t}
$$

with  $c_1 = \sqrt{32d(1 + 2s)\max(1,1 / (\kappa\lambda))\log\left(1 + \frac{t}{\kappa\lambda d}\right)}$ . furthermore, if  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then:

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(1)} = \mathcal {o} \left(\sqrt {\kappa} \cdot d \cdot \sqrt {t} \log (t)\right).
$$

sketch of proof. note that by lemma 2 the bonus  $\epsilon_{t,1}(x)$  upper-bounds  $\delta^{\mathrm{pred}}(x,\theta_t^{(1)})$  on a high-probability event. this ensures that  $r_t^{(1)}\leq 2\sum_{t = 1}^{\mathsf{t}}\epsilon_{t,1}(x_t)$  with high-probability. a straightforward application of the elliptical lemma (see e.g. (abbasi-yadkori et al., 2011), stated in appendix d) ensures that the bonus cumulates sub-linearly and leads to the regret bound. the formal proof is deferred to section c.2 in the supplementary material.

remark. the projection step presented in equation (8) is very similar to the one employed in filippi et al. (2010), to the difference that we use the metric  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta)$  instead of  $\mathbf{v}_t$ . while both lead to complex optimization programs (i.e. non-convex), neither needs to be carried out when  $\hat{\theta}_t \in \theta$ , which can be easily checked online and happens most frequently in practice.

# 4.2. logistic-ucb-2

to get rid of the last dependency in  $\sqrt{\kappa}$  and improve logistic-ucb-1, we use the improved prediction bound provided in lemma 3. namely, we define the bonus:

$$
\epsilon_ {t, 2} (x, \theta) = (2 + 4 s) \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + (4 + 8 s) m \kappa \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}
$$

however, as this bonus now depends on the chosen estimate  $\theta$ , existing results (such as the elliptical lemma) do not guarantee that it sums sub-linearly. to obtain this property, we need to restrain  $\mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  to a set of admissible estimates that, intuitively, make the most of the past information already

$$

note that  $\mathcal{w}_t$  is made up of  $\max(|\mathcal{x}|, t - 1)$  convex constraints, and is trivially not empty when  $0_d \in \theta$ . thanks to this new feasible set, we now define the estimator:

$$
\theta_ {t} ^ {(2)} := \underset {\theta \in \mathcal {w} _ {t}} {\arg \min } \left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \tag {9}
$$

we define logistic-ucb-2 as the optimistic bandit instantiated with  $(\theta_t^{(2)},\epsilon_{t,2}(x,\theta_t^{(2)}))$  and detailed in algorithm 2. we state its regret upper-bound in theorem 3. this result shows that the dominating term (in  $\sqrt{t}$ ) of the regret is independent of  $\kappa$ . a dependency still exists, but for a second-order term which grows only as  $\log (t)^2$ .

theorem 3 (regret of logistic-ucb-2). with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(2)} \leq c _ {2} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t} + c _ {3} \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \kappa
$$

with

$$
c _ {2} = (4 + 8 s) \sqrt {2 d l \max  (1 , l / \lambda) \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)}
$$

$$
c _ {3} = m d \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa d \lambda}\right) (8 + 1 6 s) (2 + 2 \sqrt {1 + 2 s})
$$

furthermore if  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then:

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(2)} = \mathcal {o} \left(d \cdot \sqrt {t} \log (t) + \kappa \cdot d ^ {2} \cdot \log (t) ^ {2}\right)
$$

the formal proof is deferred to section c.3 in the supplementary materials. it mostly relies on the following lemma, which ensures that the first term of  $\epsilon_{t,2}(x,\theta_t^{(2)})$  cumulates sub-linearly and independently of  $\kappa$  (up to a second order term that grows only as  $\log (t)$ ).

lemma 4. let  $t \geq 1$ . under the event  $e_{\delta}$ :

$$
\sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \dot {\mu} (x _ {t} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {(2)}) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {t} ^ {(2)})} \leq c _ {4} \sqrt {t} + c _ {5} m \kappa \gamma_ {t} (\delta)
$$

where  $c_4$  and  $c_5$  are independent of  $\kappa$ .

sketch of proof. the proof relies on the fact that  $\theta_t^{(2)} \in \mathcal{w}_t$ . intuitively, this allows us to lower-bound  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_t^{(2)})$  by the matrix  $\sum_{s=1}^{t-1} \min_{\theta \in \mathcal{c}_s(\delta) \cap \theta} \dot{\mu}(\theta^\top x_s) x_s x_s^\top + \lambda \mathbf{i}_d$ . note that in this case,  $\min_{\theta \in \mathcal{c}_s(\delta) \cap \theta} \dot{\mu}(\theta^\top x_s)$  is no longer a function of  $t$ . this, coupled with a one-step taylor expansion

of  $\dot{\mu}$  allows us to use the elliptical lemma on a well chosen quantity and obtain the announced rates. the formal proof is deferred to section b.6 in the supplementary materials.

# 5. discussion

in this work, we studied the scaling of optimistic logistic bandit algorithms for a particular glm: the logistic model. we explicitly showed that previous algorithms suffered from prohibitive scaling introduced by the quantity  $\kappa$ , because of their sub-optimal treatment of the non-linearities of the reward signal. thanks to a novel non-linear approach, we proved that they can be improved by deriving tighter prediction bounds. by doing so, we gave a rigorous justification for an algorithm that resembles the heuristic algorithm empirically evaluated in filippi et al. (2010). this algorithm exhibits a regret bound that only suffers from a  $\sqrt{\kappa}$  dependency, compared to  $\kappa$  for previous guarantees. further, we showed that a more careful algorithmic design leads to yet better guarantees, where the leading term of the regret is independent of  $\kappa$ . this result bridges the gap between logistic bandits and linear bandits, up to polynomial terms in constants of interest (e.g.  $s$ ).

the theoretical value of the regret upper-bound of logistic-ucb-2 can be highlighted by comparing it to the bayesian regret lower bound provided by dong et al. (2019). namely, they show that for any logistic bandit algorithm, and for any polynomial form  $p$  and  $\epsilon > 0$ , there exist a problem instance such that the regret is at least  $\omega(p(d)t^{1 - \epsilon})$ . this does not contradict our bound, as for hard problem instance one can have  $\kappa = t$  in which case the second term of logistic-ucb-2 will scale as  $d^2 t$ . note that other corner-cases instances further highlight the theoretical value of our regret bounds. namely, note that  $\kappa = \sqrt{t}$  turns glm-ucb's regret guarantee vacuous as it will scale linearly with  $t$ . on the other hand for this case the regret of logistic-ucb-1 scales as  $t^{3/4}$ , and the regret of logistic-ucb-2 continues to scale as  $\sqrt{t}$ .

extension to other glms. an important avenue for future work consists in extending our results to other generalized linear models. this can be done naturally by extending our work. indeed, the properties of the sigmoid that we leverage are rather weak, and might easily transfer to other inverse link functions. we first used the fact that  $\dot{\mu}$  represents the variance of the reward in order to use theorem 1. this is not a specificity of the logistic model, but is a common relationship observed for all exponential models and their related mean function (filippi et al., 2010, §2). we also used the generalized self-concordance property of the logistic loss, which is a consequence of the fact that  $|\ddot{\mu}| \leq \dot{\mu}$ . this control is quite mild, and other mean functions might display similar properties (with other constants). this is namely the case of another generalized linear model: the poisson regression.

randomized algorithms. the lessons learned here for optimistic algorithms might be transferred to randomized algorithms (such as thompson sampling) that are often preferred in practical applications thanks to their superior empirical performances. extending our approach to such algorithms would therefore be of significant practical importance.

# appendix a. proof of theorem 1

theorem 1. let  $\{\mathcal{f}_t\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  be a filtration. let  $\{x_t\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  be a stochastic process in  $\mathcal{b}_2(d)$  such that  $x_t$  is  $\mathcal{f}_t$  measurable. let  $\{\varepsilon_t\}_{t=2}^{\infty}$  be a martingale difference sequence such that  $\varepsilon_{t+1}$  is  $\mathcal{f}_{t+1}$  measurable. furthermore, assume that conditionally on  $\mathcal{f}_t$  we have  $|\varepsilon_{t+1}| \leq 1$  almost surely, and note  $\sigma_t^2 \coloneqq \mathbb{e}\left[\varepsilon_{t+1}^2 |\mathcal{f}_t\right]$ . let  $\lambda > 0$  and for any  $t \geq 1$  define:

$$
\mathbf {h} _ {t} := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \sigma_ {s} ^ {2} x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {t} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d}, \qquad s _ {t} := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \varepsilon_ {s + 1} x _ {s}.
$$

then for any  $\delta \in (0,1]$ :

$$
\mathbb {p} \left(\exists t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \geq \frac {\sqrt {\lambda}}{2} + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {\det (\mathbf {h} _ {\mathbf {t}}) ^ {\frac {1}{2}} \lambda^ {- \frac {d}{2}}}{\delta}\right) + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} d \log (2)\right) \leq \delta .
$$

for readability concerns, we define  $\beta = \sqrt{2\lambda}$  and rewrite:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbf {h} _ {t} = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \sigma_ {s} ^ {2} x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {t} + \frac {\beta^ {2}}{2} \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ = \bar {\mathbf {h}} _ {t} + \frac {\beta^ {2}}{2} \mathbf {i} _ {d}. \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\bar{\mathbf{h}}_t\coloneqq \sum_{s = 1}^{t - 1}\sigma_s^2 x_s x_s^t$  . for all  $\xi \in \mathbb{r}^d$  let  $m_0(\xi) = 1$  and for  $t\geq 1$  define:

$$
m _ {t} (\xi) := \exp \left(\xi^ {t} s _ {t} - \| \xi \| _ {\bar {\mathbf {h}} _ {t}} ^ {2}\right)
$$

we now claim lemma 5 which will be proven later (section a.1).

lemma 5. for all  $\xi \in \mathcal{b}_2(d)$ ,  $\{m_t(\xi)\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  is a non-negative super-martingale.

our analysis follows the steps of the pseudo-maximization principle introduced in de la pena et al. (2004), used by abbasi-yadtori et al. (2011) for the linear bandit and thoroughly detailed in chapter 20 of lattimore and szepesvári (2018). the main difference in our analysis comes from the restriction  $\xi \in \mathcal{b}_2(d)$  (instead of  $\xi \in \mathbb{r}^d$ ) which calls for some refinements when using the laplace trick to provide a high-probability bound on the maximum of  $\log m_t(\xi)$ .

let  $h(\xi)$  be a probability density function with support on  $\mathcal{b}_2(d)$  (to be defined later). for  $t \geq 0$  let:

$$
\bar {m} _ {t} := \int_ {\xi} m _ {t} (\xi) d h (\xi)
$$

by lemma 20.3 of lattimore and szepesvári (2018)  $\bar{m}_t$  is also a non-negative super-martingale, and  $\mathbb{e}\left[\bar{m}_0\right] = 1$ . let  $\tau$  be a stopping time with respect to the filtration  $\{f_t\}_{t=0}^{\infty}$ . we can follow the proof of lemma 8 in abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011) to justify that  $\bar{m}_{\tau}$  is well-defined (independently

of whether  $\tau < \infty$  holds or not) and that  $\mathbb{e}\left[\bar{m}_{\tau}\right] \leq 1$ . therefore, with  $\delta \in (0,1)$  and thanks to the maximal inequality:

$$
\mathbb {p} \left(\log (\bar {m} _ {\tau}) \geq \log (\frac {1}{\delta})\right) = \mathbb {p} \left(\bar {m} _ {\tau} \geq \frac {1}{\delta}\right) \leq \delta \tag {10}
$$

we now proceed to compute  $\bar{m}_t$  (more precisely a lower bound on  $\bar{m}_t$ ). let  $\beta$  be a strictly positive scalar, and set  $h$  to be the density of an isotropic normal distribution of precision  $\beta^2$  truncated on  $\mathcal{b}_2(d)$ . we will denote  $n(h)$  its normalization constant. simple computations show that:

$$
\bar {m} _ {t} = \frac {1}{n (h)} \int_ {\mathcal {b} _ {2} (d)} \exp \left(\xi^ {t} s _ {t} - \| \xi \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t}} ^ {2}\right) d \xi
$$

to ease notations, let  $f(\xi) \coloneqq \xi^t s_t - \| \xi \|_{\mathbf{h}_t}^2$  and  $\xi_* = \arg \max_{\| \xi \|_2 \leq 1/2} f(\xi)$ . because:

$$

$$

we obtain that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \bar {m} _ {t} = \frac {e ^ {f (\xi_ {*})}}{n (h)} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} _ {\| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1} \exp \left((\boldsymbol {\xi} - \xi_ {*}) ^ {t} \nabla f (\xi_ {*}) - (\boldsymbol {\xi} - \xi_ {*}) ^ {t} \mathbf {h} _ {t} (\boldsymbol {\xi} - \xi_ {*})\right) d \boldsymbol {\xi} \\ = \frac {e ^ {f (\xi_ {*})}}{n (h)} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} _ {\| \xi + \xi_ {*} \| _ {2} \leq 1} \exp \left(\xi^ {t} \nabla f (\xi_ {*}) - \xi^ {t} \mathbf {h} _ {t} \xi\right) d \xi \quad \text {(c h a n g e o f v a r i a b l e} \xi + \xi_ {*}) \\ \geq \frac {e ^ {f (\xi_ {*})}}{n (h)} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} _ {\| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2} \exp \left(\xi^ {t} \nabla f \left(\xi_ {*}\right) - \xi^ {t} \mathbf {h} _ {t} \xi\right) d \xi \quad \text {(a s} \| \xi_ {*} \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2) \\ = \frac {e ^ {f (\xi_ {*})}}{n (h)} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} _ {\| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2} \exp \left(\xi^ {t} \nabla f (\xi_ {*})\right) \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \xi^ {t} (2 \mathbf {h} _ {t}) \xi\right) d \xi \\ \end{array}
$$

by defining  $g(\xi)$  the density of the normal distribution of precision  $2\mathbf{h}_t$  truncated on the ball  $\{\xi \in \mathbb{r}^d, \| \xi \|_2 \leq 1/2\}$  and noting  $n(g)$  its normalizing constant, we can rewrite:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \bar {m} _ {t} \geq \exp (f (\xi_ {*})) \frac {n (g)}{n (h)} \mathbb {e} _ {g} [ \exp (\xi^ {t} \nabla f (\xi_ {*})) ] \\ \geq \exp \left(f (\xi_ {*})\right) \frac {n (g)}{n (h)} \exp \left(\mathbb {e} _ {g} \left[ \boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} \nabla f (\xi_ {*}) \right]\right) \quad \mathrm {(j e n s e n ' s i n e q u a l i t y)} \\ \geq \exp (f (\xi_ {*})) \frac {n (g)}{n (h)} \quad \left(\text {a s} \mathbb {e} _ {g} [ \xi ] = 0\right) \tag {11} \\ \end{array}
$$

unpacking this results and assembling (10) and (11), we obtain that for any  $\xi_0$  such that  $\| \xi_0\| _2\leq 1 / 2$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {p} \left(\bar {m} _ {t} \geq \frac {1}{\delta}\right) \geq \mathbb {p} \left(\exp (f (\xi_ {*})) \frac {n (g)}{n (h)} \geq 1 / \delta\right) \\ = \mathbb {p} \left(\log \left(\exp (f (\xi_ {*})) \frac {n (g)}{n (h)}\right) \geq \log (1 / \delta)\right) \\ = \mathbb {p} \left(f \left(\xi_ {*}\right) \geq \log (1 / \delta) + \log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right)\right) \\ = \mathbb {p} \left(\max  _ {\| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2} \xi^ {t} s _ {t} - \| \xi \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t}} ^ {2} \geq \log (1 / \delta) + \log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right)\right) \\ \geq \mathbb {p} \left(\xi_ {0} ^ {t} s _ {t} - \| \xi_ {0} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t}} ^ {2} \geq \log (1 / \delta) + \log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right)\right) \tag {12} \\ \end{array}
$$

in particular, we can use:

$$
\xi_ {0} := \frac {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} s _ {t}}{\| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}}} \frac {\beta}{2 \sqrt {2}}
$$

since

$$
\| \xi_ {0} \| _ {2} \leq \frac {\beta}{2 \sqrt {2}} \left(\xi_ {\min } (\bar {\mathbf {h}} _ {t}) + \frac {\beta^ {2}}{2}\right) ^ {- 1 / 2} \leq 1 / 2
$$

using this value of  $\xi_0$  in equation (12) yields:

$$
\mathbb {p} \left(\| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \geq \frac {\beta}{2 \sqrt {2}} + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} \log \left(\frac {n (h)}{\delta n (g)}\right)\right) \leq \mathbb {p} \left(\bar {m} _ {t} \geq \frac {1}{\delta}\right)
$$

to finish the proof we have left to explicit the quantities  $n(h)$  and  $n(g)$ . lemma 6 provides an upper-bound for the log of their ratio. its proof is given in section a.2.

lemma 6. the following inequality holds:

$$
\log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right) \leq \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d / 2} \det  (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {1 / 2}}{\beta^ {d}}\right) + d \log (2)
$$

therefore with probability at least  $1 - \delta$  and by using equation (10):

$$
\left\| s _ {\tau} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {\tau} ^ {- 1}} \leq \frac {\beta}{2 \sqrt {2}} + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} \log (1 / \delta) + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d / 2} \det \left(\mathbf {h} _ {\tau}\right) ^ {1 / 2}}{\beta^ {d}}\right) + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} d \log (2) \tag {13}
$$

directly following the stopping time construction argument in the proof of theorem 1 of abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011) we obtain that with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ , for all  $t \in \mathbb{n}$ :

$$
\left\| s _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \leq \frac {\beta}{2 \sqrt {2}} + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d / 2} \det \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t}\right) ^ {1 / 2}}{\beta^ {d} \delta}\right) + \frac {2 \sqrt {2}}{\beta} d \log (2) \tag {14}
$$

finally, recalling that  $\beta = \sqrt{2\lambda}$  provides the announced result.

# a.1. proof of lemma 5

lemma 5. for all  $\xi \in \mathcal{b}_2(d)$ ,  $\{m_t(\xi)\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  is a non-negative super-martingale.

proof. for all  $t \geq 1$  we have that:

$$
\mathbb {e} \left[ \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t}) | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right] = \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t - 1}) \mathbb {e} \left[ \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} x _ {t - 1} \varepsilon_ {t}) | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right].
$$

since  $|\xi^t x_{t - 1}|\leq 1$  all conditions of lemma 7 (stated and proven below) are checked and:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {e} \left[ \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t}) | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right] \leq \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t - 1}) (1 + \sigma_ {t - 1} ^ {2} (x _ {t - 1} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {\xi}) ^ {2}) \\ \leq \exp (\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t - 1} + \sigma_ {t - 1} ^ {2} (x _ {t - 1} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {\xi}) ^ {2}) \qquad \qquad (1 + x \leq e ^ {x}) \\ \end{array}
$$

therefore:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {e} \left[ m _ {t} (\boldsymbol {\xi}) | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right] = \mathbb {e} \left[ \exp \left(\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} s _ {t} - \| \boldsymbol {\xi} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {\mathbf {t}}} ^ {2}\right) \big | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right] \\ = \mathbb {e} \left[ \exp \left(\boldsymbol {\xi} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {s} _ {t}\right) | \mathcal {f} _ {t - 1} \right] \exp \left(- \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \sigma_ {s} ^ {2} (\boldsymbol {x} _ {s} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {\xi}) ^ {2}\right) \\ \leq \exp \left(\xi^ {t} s _ {t - 1} + \sigma_ {t - 1} ^ {2} (x _ {t - 1} ^ {t} \xi) ^ {2} - \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \sigma_ {s} ^ {2} (x _ {s} ^ {t} \xi) ^ {2}\right) \\ \leq m _ {t - 1} (\xi) \\ \end{array}
$$

yielding the announced result.

to prove lemma 5 we needed the following result.

lemma 7. let  $\varepsilon$  be a centered random variable of variance  $\sigma^2$  and such that  $|\varepsilon| \leq 1$  almost surely. then for all  $\lambda \in [-1,1]$ :

$$
\mathbb {e} \left[ \exp (\lambda \epsilon) \right] \leq 1 + \lambda^ {2} \sigma^ {2}.
$$

proof. a decomposition of the exponential gives:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {e} \left[ \exp (\lambda \epsilon) \right] = 1 + \sum_ {k = 2} ^ {\infty} \frac {\lambda^ {k}}{k !} \mathbb {e} \left[ \epsilon^ {k - 2} \epsilon^ {2} \right] \\ \leq 1 + \lambda^ {2} \sigma^ {2} \sum_ {k = 2} ^ {\infty} \frac {| \lambda | ^ {k - 2}}{k !} \quad (| \varepsilon | \leq 1) \\ \leq 1 + \lambda^ {2} \sigma^ {2} (e - 2) \leq 1 + \lambda^ {2} \sigma^ {2} \quad (| \lambda | \leq 1) \\ \end{array}
$$

# a.2. proof of lemma 6

lemma 6. the following inequality holds:

$$
\log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right) \leq \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d / 2} \det  \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t}\right) ^ {1 / 2}}{\beta^ {d}}\right) + d \log (2)
$$

by definition of  $n(h)$  and thanks to a change of variable:

$$
\begin{array}{l} n (h) = \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \beta^ {2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) d \xi \\ = \beta^ {- d} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) d \xi \\ \end{array}
$$

also by a change of variable:

$$
\begin{array}{l} n (g) = \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2 \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \xi^ {t} (2 \mathbf {h} _ {t}) \xi\right) d \xi \\ = \det  (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {- 1 / 2} 2 ^ {- d / 2} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \| 2 ^ {- 1 / 2} \mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1 / 2} \xi \| _ {2} \leq 1 / 2 \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) \\ = \det  (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {- 1 / 2} 2 ^ {- d / 2} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \left\| \left(\bar {\mathbf {h}} _ {t} + \frac {\beta^ {2}}{2} \mathbf {i} _ {d}\right) ^ {- 1 / 2} \xi \right\| _ {2} \leq 1 / \sqrt {2} \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) \\ \geq \det  (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {- 1 / 2} 2 ^ {- d / 2} \int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} \left[ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta / 2 \right] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

we obtain the following upper-bound on the ratio  $n(h) / n(g)$ :

$$
\frac {n (h)}{n (g)} \leq \beta^ {- d} \det  \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t}\right) ^ {1 / 2} 2 ^ {d / 2} \frac {\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) d \xi}{\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta / 2 ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right)} \tag {15}
$$

note that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \frac {\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) d \xi}{\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta / 2 ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right)} = 1 + \frac {\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \beta / 2 \leq \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {2} ^ {2}\right) d \xi}{\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbf {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta / 2 ] \exp \left(- \frac {1}{2} \| \xi \| _ {0} ^ {2}\right)} \\ \leq 1 + \frac {\exp \left(- \frac {1}{8} \beta^ {2}\right)}{\exp \left(- \frac {1}{8} \beta^ {2}\right)} \cdot \frac {\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} [ \beta / 2 \leq \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta ] d \xi}{\int_ {\mathbb {r} ^ {d}} \mathbb {1} [ \| \xi \| _ {2} \leq \beta / 2 ]} \\ = 1 + \frac {\mathcal {v} _ {d} (\beta) - \mathcal {v} _ {d} (\beta / 2)}{\mathcal {v} _ {d} (\beta / 2)} \\ = 2 ^ {d} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\mathcal{v}_d(\beta) \propto \beta^d$  denotes the volume of the  $d$ -dimensional ball of radius  $\beta$ . plugging this result in equation (15) and taking the logarithm yields the announced result:

$$
\log \left(\frac {n (h)}{n (g)}\right) \leq \log \left(\frac {2 ^ {d / 2} \det (\mathbf {h} _ {t}) ^ {1 / 2}}{\beta^ {d}}\right) + d \log (2)
$$

# appendix b. proofs of prediction and concentration results

for all this section, we will use the following notations:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \alpha (x, \theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}) := \int_ {v = 0} ^ {1} \dot {\mu} (v x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {2} + (1 - v) x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}) d v > 0 \\ \mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}\right) := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \alpha \left(x, \theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ \mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta_ {1}) := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ \mathbf {v} _ {t} := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \kappa \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\theta_{1},\theta_{2}$  and  $x$  are vectors in  $\mathbb{r}^d$  and  $\lambda$  is a strictly positive scalar. we will extensively use that fact that  $\forall \theta_1,\theta_2\in \theta$  we have  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_1,\theta_2)\geq \kappa^{-1}\mathbf{v}_t$  and  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_1)\geq \kappa^{-1}\mathbf{v}_t$

the quantities  $\alpha(x, \theta_1, \theta_2)$  and  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_1, \theta_2)$  naturally arise when studying glms. indeed, note that for all  $x \in \mathbb{r}^d$  and  $\theta \in \mathbb{r}^d$ , the following equality holds:

$$
\mu \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}\right) - \mu \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {2}\right) = \alpha \left(x, \theta_ {2}, \theta_ {1}\right) x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \left(\theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2}\right) \tag {16}
$$

this result is classical (see filippi et al. (2010)) and can be obtained by a straight-forward application of the mean-value theorem. it notably allows us to link  $\theta_{1} - \theta_{2}$  with  $g_{t}(\theta_{1}) - g_{t}(\theta_{2})$ . namely, it is straightforward that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} g _ {t} (\theta_ {1}) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {2}) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \alpha (x _ {s}, \theta_ {2}, \theta_ {1}) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} (\theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2}) + \lambda \theta_ {1} - \lambda \theta_ {2} \\ = \mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {2}, \theta_ {1}\right) \left(\theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

because  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_1,\theta_2)\succ \mathbf{0}_{d\times d}$  this yields:

$$
\left\| \theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2} \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {2}, \theta_ {1}\right)} = \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {1}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {2}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {2}, \theta_ {1}\right)} \tag {17}
$$

# b.1. proof of lemma 1

lemma 1. let  $\delta \in (0,1]$  and

$$
e _ {\delta} := \{\forall t \geq 1, \theta_ {*} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta) \}.
$$

then  $\mathbb{p}(e_{\delta})\geq 1 - \delta$

note that, for any  $t \geq 1$ :

$$
\theta_ {*} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} \longleftrightarrow \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \tag {18}
$$

to prove lemma 1 we therefore need to ensure that the r.h.s happens for all  $t \geq 1$  with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ . this is the object of the following lemma, of which lemma 1 is a direct corollary.

lemma 8. let  $\delta \in (0,1]$ . with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
\forall t \geq 1, \quad \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta)
$$

proof. recall that  $\hat{\theta}_t$  is the unique maximizer of the log-likelihood:

$$
\mathcal {l} _ {t} ^ {\lambda} (\theta) := \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \big [ r _ {s + 1} \log \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\intercal} \theta) + (1 - r _ {s + 1}) \log (1 - \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\intercal} \theta)) \big ] - \frac {\lambda}{2} \| \theta \| _ {2} ^ {2}
$$

and therefore  $\hat{\theta}_t$  is a critical point of  $\mathcal{l}_t^\lambda (\theta)$ . solving for  $\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{l}_t^\lambda = 0$  and using the fact that  $\dot{\mu} = \mu (1 - \mu)$  we obtain:

$$
\sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \mu (\hat {\theta} _ {t} ^ {\mathsf {t}} x _ {s}) x _ {s} + \lambda \hat {\theta} _ {t} = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} r _ {s + 1} x _ {s}
$$

this result, combined with the definition of  $g_{t}(\theta_{*}) = \sum_{s = 1}^{t - 1}\mu (x_{s}^{\mathsf{t}}\theta_{*})x_{s} + \lambda \theta_{*}$  yields:

$$
\begin{array}{l} g _ {t} (\hat {\theta} _ {t}) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \varepsilon_ {s + 1} x _ {s} - \lambda \theta_ {*} \\ = s _ {t} - \lambda \theta_ {*} \\ \end{array}
$$

where we denoted  $\varepsilon_{s + 1} \coloneqq r_{s + 1} - \mu(x_s^\top \theta_*)$  for all  $s \geq 1$  and  $s_t \coloneqq \sum_{s = 1}^{t - 1} \varepsilon_{s + 1} x_s$  for all  $t \geq 1$ . simple linear algebra implies that:

$$
\left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \leq \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} + \sqrt {\lambda} s \tag {19}
$$

note that by equation (1),  $\{\varepsilon_t\}_{t=1}^{\infty}$  is a martingale difference sequence adapted to  $\mathcal{f}$  and almost surely bounded by 1. also, note that for all  $s \geq 1$ :

$$
\dot {\mu} (x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {*}) = \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {*}) (1 - \mu (x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {*})) = \mathbb {e} \left[ \varepsilon_ {s + 1} ^ {2} | \mathcal {f} _ {t} \right] =: \sigma_ {s} ^ {2}
$$

and thus  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_*) = \sum_{s=1}^{t-1} \sigma_s^2 x_s x_s^\top + \lambda \mathbf{i}_d$ . all the conditions of theorem 1 are checked and therefore:

$$
\begin{array}{l} 1 - \delta \leq \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} \leq \frac {\sqrt {\lambda}}{2} + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {\det  \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta_ {*})\right) ^ {1 / 2} \lambda^ {- d / 2}}{\delta}\right) + \frac {2 d}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log (2)\right) \\ \leq \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} \leq \frac {\sqrt {\lambda}}{2} + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {(\lambda + l t / d) ^ {d / 2} \lambda^ {- d / 2}}{\delta}\right) + \frac {2 d}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log (2)\right) \\ \leq \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} \leq \frac {\sqrt {\lambda}}{2} + \frac {2}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log \left(\frac {\left(1 + \frac {l t}{\lambda d}\right) ^ {d / 2}}{\delta}\right) + \frac {2 d}{\sqrt {\lambda}} \log (2)\right) \\ = \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \| s _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) - \sqrt {\lambda} s\right) \tag {20} \\ \end{array}
$$

where we used that:

$$
\det  \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta_ {*})\right) \leq l ^ {d} \det  \left(\sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\intercal} + \frac {\lambda}{l} \mathbf {i} _ {d}\right) \leq l ^ {d} \left(\frac {\lambda}{l} + \frac {t}{d}\right) ^ {d} \leq \left(\lambda + \frac {l t}{d}\right) ^ {d}
$$

thanks to lemma 16. assembling equation (19) with equation (20) yields:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \left\| g _ {t} (\hat {\theta} _ {t}) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta)\right) \geq \mathbb {p} \left(\forall t \geq 1, \left\| s _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} + \sqrt {\lambda} s \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta)\right) \\ \geq 1 - \delta \\ \end{array}
$$

hence the announced result.

remark. in the following sections we will often use the rewriting of  $e_{\delta}$  inherited from equation (18):

$$
e _ {\delta} = \left\{\forall t \geq 1, \left\| g _ {t} (\hat {\theta} _ {t}) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {*})} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \right\}
$$

# b.2. key self-concordant results

we start this section by claiming and proving lemma 9, which uses the generalized self-concordance property of the log-loss and will be later used to derive useful lower-bounds on the function  $\alpha(\cdot)$ .

lemma 9 (self-concordance control). for any  $z_{1},z_{2}\in \mathbb{r}$ , we have the following inequality:

$$
\dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {1 - \exp (- | z _ {1} - z _ {2} |)}{| z _ {1} - z _ {2} |} \leq \int_ {0} ^ {1} \dot {\mu} (z _ {1} + v (z _ {2} - z _ {1})) d v \leq \dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {\exp (| z _ {1} - z _ {2} |) - 1}{| z _ {1} - z _ {2} |}
$$

furthermore:

$$
\int_ {0} ^ {1} \dot {\mu} (z _ {1} + v (z _ {2} - z _ {1})) d v \geq \dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) (1 + | z _ {1} - z _ {2} |) ^ {- 1}
$$

proof. the proof is based on the generalized self-concordance property of the logistic loss, which is detailed and exploited in other works on the logistic regression (bach et al., 2010). this part of the analysis relies on similar properties of the logistic function. indeed, a short computation shows that for any  $z \in \mathbb{r}^d$ , one has  $|\dot{\mu}(z)| \leq \dot{\mu}(z)$ . therefore, using the fact that  $\dot{\mu}(z) > 0$  for all  $z$  in any compact of  $\mathbb{r}$ , one has that for all  $z \geq z_1$ :

$$
- (z - z _ {1}) \leq \int_ {z _ {1}} ^ {z} \frac {d}{d v} \log \dot {\mu} (v) d v \leq z - z _ {1}
$$

which in turns gives us that:

$$
\dot {\mu} \left(z _ {1}\right) \exp \left(- \left(z - z _ {1}\right)\right) \leq \dot {\mu} (z) \leq \dot {\mu} \left(z _ {1}\right) \exp \left(z - z _ {1}\right)
$$

assuming that  $z_{2} \geq z_{1}$ , setting  $z = z_{1} + v(z_{2} - z_{1}) > z_{1}$  for  $v \in [0,1]$  and integrating over  $v$  gives:

$$
\dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {1 - \exp (- (z _ {2} - z _ {1}))}{z _ {2} - z _ {1}} \leq \int_ {0} ^ {1} \dot {\mu} (z _ {1} + v (z _ {2} - z _ {1})) d v \leq \dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {\exp (z _ {2} - z _ {1}) - 1}{z _ {2} - z _ {1}}
$$

repeating this operation for  $z \leq z_1$  and  $z_2 \leq z_1$  leads to:

$$
\dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {\exp (z _ {2} - z _ {1}) - 1}{z _ {2} - z _ {1}} \leq \int_ {0} ^ {1} \dot {\mu} (z _ {1} + v (z _ {2} - z _ {1})) d v \leq \dot {\mu} (z _ {1}) \frac {1 - \exp (z _ {1} - z _ {2})}{z _ {1} - z _ {2}}
$$

combining the last two equations gives the first result. note that if  $x \geq 0$  we have  $e^{-x} \leq (1 + x)^{-1}$ , and therefore  $(1 - e^{-x}) / x \geq (1 + x)^{-1}$ . applying this inequality to the l.h.s of the first result provides the second statement of the lemma.

we now state lemma 10 which allows to provide a control of  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_1,\theta_2)$  by  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_1)$  and  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_2)$ .

lemma 10. for all  $\theta_1, \theta_2 \in \theta$  the following inequalities hold:

$$
\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}\right) \geq (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \mathbf {h} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {1}\right)
$$

$$
\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}\right) \geq \left(1 + 2 s\right) ^ {- 1} \mathbf {h} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {2}\right)
$$

proof. the proof relies on the self-concordance property of the log-loss, which comes with the fact  $|\ddot{\mu}| \leq \dot{\mu}$ . as detailed in lemma 9 this allows us to derive an exponential-control lower bound on  $\dot{\mu}$ . let  $x \in \mathcal{b}_2(d)$ . by applying lemma 9 with  $z_1 = x^\top \theta_1$  and  $z_2 = x^\top (\theta_2 - \theta_1)$  we obtain that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \alpha (x, \theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}) \geq \left(1 + \left| x ^ {\top} \left(\theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2}\right) \right|\right) ^ {- 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\top} \theta_ {1}\right) \\ \geq \left(1 + \| x \| _ {2} \| \theta_ {1} - \theta_ {2} \| _ {2}\right) ^ {- 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\top} \theta_ {1}\right) \quad \text {(c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z)} \\ \geq \left(1 + 2 s\right) ^ {- 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\intercal} \theta_ {1}\right) \quad \left(\theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2} \in \theta , x \in \mathcal {b} _ {2} (d)\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

using this lower bound:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbf {g} _ {t} (\theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \alpha (x _ {s}, \theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2}) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ \succeq (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} (x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \quad \left(x _ {s} \in \mathcal {x} \subseteq \mathcal {b} _ {2} (d), \theta_ {1}, \theta_ {2} \in \theta\right) \\ = (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \left(\sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + (1 + 2 s) \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d}\right) \\ \succeq (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \left(\sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {1}\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d}\right) \quad (s > 0) \\ = (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta_ {1}) \\ \end{array}
$$

which yields the first inequality. realizing (through a change of variable for instance) that  $\theta_{1}$  and  $\theta_{2}$  have symmetric roles in the definition of  $\alpha(x, \theta_{1}, \theta_{2})$  directly yields the second inequality.

\left(\mathbf {v} _ {t} \leq \kappa \mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\mathrm {l}}\right)\right)
$$

(mean value theorem)

# b.4. proof of lemma 2

lemma 2. on the event  $e_{\delta}$ , for all  $t \geq 1$ , any  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  and  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq l \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}}.
$$

proof. during this proof we work under the good event  $e_{\delta}$ , which holds with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ .

for all  $t \geq 1$ , for all  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ , for all  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \delta^ {\operatorname {p r e d}} (x, \theta) = | \mu (x ^ {\top} \theta_ {*}) - \mu (x ^ {\top} \theta) | \\ \leq \alpha (x, \theta_ {*}, \theta) \left| x ^ {\mathsf {t}} (\theta_ {*} - \theta) \right| \quad \mathrm {(e q u a t i o n (1 6))} \\ \leq l \left| x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \left(\theta_ {*} - \theta\right) \right| \quad (\dot {\mu} \leq l) \\ \leq l \left| x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1 / 2} (\theta , \theta_ {*}) \mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {1 / 2} (\theta , \theta_ {*}) (\theta_ {*} - \theta) \right| \\ \leq l \left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} \left\| \theta_ {*} - \theta \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} \quad (\text {c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z}) \\ \leq l \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \| \theta_ {*} - \theta \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \quad \left(\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right) \geq \kappa^ {- 1} \mathbf {v} _ {t}\right) \\ = l \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) - g _ {t} (\theta) \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} \tag {equation(17)} \\ = l \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \left(\left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} + \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} (\theta) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)}\right) \\ \leq l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \left(\left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} + \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} (\theta) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)}\right) \quad (\text {l e m m a} 1 0, \theta , \theta_ {*} \in \theta) \\ \leq l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} (\gamma_ {t} (\delta) + \gamma_ {t} (\delta)) \quad (\theta , \theta_ {*} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta)) \\ \leq 2 l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \\ \end{array}
$$

which proves the desired result.

the main device of this proof is the application of lemma 10, itself inherited from the self-concordance of the log-loss. this allows to replace the matrix  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_*,\theta)$  by  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta_*)$  and  $\mathbf{h}_t(\theta)$ , at the price of a  $\sqrt{1 + 2s}$  multiplicative factor (instead of  $\sqrt{\kappa}$  when we lower-bound  $\mathbf{g}_t(\theta_*,\theta)$  with  $\kappa \mathbf{v}_t$ ). however, following this line of proof we loose two times the local information carried by  $\theta$ ; the first time when using the fact that  $\alpha(x,\theta,\theta_*) \leq l$ , the second time when upper-bounding  $\|x\|_{\mathbf{g}_t^{-1}(\theta_*,\theta)}$  by  $\sqrt{\kappa}\|x\|_{\mathbf{v}_t^{-1}}$ . this flaws are corrected in the more careful analysis leading to lemma 3.

# b.5. proof of lemma 3

lemma 3. on the event  $e_{\delta}$ , for all  $t \geq 1$ , any  $\theta \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  and any  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\delta^ {\operatorname {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq (2 + 4 s) \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + (4 + 8 s) m \kappa \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}.
$$

proof. during this proof we work under the good event  $e_{\delta}$ , which holds with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ .

by a first-order taylor expansion one has :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \alpha (x, \theta_ {*}, \theta) \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) + m | x ^ {\mathsf {t}} (\theta - \theta_ {*}) | \quad (| \ddot {\mu} | \leq m) \\ \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) + m \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \| \theta - \theta_ {*} \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \quad \mathrm {(c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z)} \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\top} \theta\right) + m \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} \| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta , \theta_ {*}\right)} \quad \tag {equation(17)} \\ \end{array}
$$

it can be extracted from the proof of lemma 2 in section b.4 that for all  $t \geq 1$ ,  $\theta \in \theta$ ,  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq \alpha (x, \theta_ {*}, \theta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})}
$$

and also that:

$$
\left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} (\theta_ {*}) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \leq 2 \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \gamma_ {t} (\delta)
$$

unpacking these results, we get that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \delta^ {\text {p r e d}} (x, \theta) \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {t} \theta) \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + m (4 + 8 s) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} ^ {2} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) ^ {2} \\ \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {t} \theta) \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta , \theta_ {*})} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + m (4 + 8 s) \kappa \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) ^ {2} \quad \left(\mathbf {g} _ {t} (\theta_ {*}, \theta) \geq \kappa^ {- 1} \mathbf {v} _ {t}\right) \\ \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {t} \theta) (2 + 4 s) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + m (4 + 8 s) \kappa \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) ^ {2} \quad \left(\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}, \theta\right) \geq (1 + 2 s) ^ {- 1} \mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta)\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

which proves the desired result.

# b.6. proof of lemma 4

we here claim a result more general than lemma 4. the latter is actually a direct corollary of the following lemma, once one has checked that  $\theta_t^{(2)} \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  for all  $t \geq 1$  (which will be formally proven in the following section).

lemma 13. let  $t \geq 1$ . under the event  $e_{\delta}$ , for all sequence  $\{\theta_t\}_{t=1}^t$  such that  $\theta_t \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta) \cap \mathcal{w}_t$ :

$$
\sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {t} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t}\right) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \leq c _ {4} \sqrt {t} + c _ {5} m \kappa \gamma_ {t} (\delta),
$$

where the constants

$$
c _ {4} = \sqrt {2 l \max  (1 , l / \lambda)} \sqrt {d \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)},
$$

$$
c _ {5} = 4 d \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \max (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa d \lambda}\right)
$$

show no dependencies in  $\kappa$  (except in logarithmic terms).

proof. during this proof we work under the good event  $e_{\delta}$ , which holds with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ .

we start the proof by making the following remark. note that  $\mathcal{w}_t$  can be rewritten as:

$$
\mathcal {w} _ {t} = \left\{\theta \in \theta \text {s . t} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta\right) \geq \inf  _ {\theta^ {\prime} \in \mathscr {c} _ {s} (\delta) \cap \theta} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta^ {\prime}\right), \forall s \leq t - 1 \right\}. \tag {21}
$$

indeed, using the monotonicity of  $\dot{\mu}$  on  $\mathbb{r}_d^+$  and  $\mathbb{r}_d^-$ , one can show that this re-writing is equivalent with the one provided in the main text:

$$
\mathcal {w} _ {t} = \left\{\theta \in \theta \text {s . t} | x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta | \leq \sup  _ {\theta^ {\prime} \in \mathcal {c} _ {s} (\delta) \cap \theta} | x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta^ {\prime} |, \forall s \leq t - 1 \right\}.
$$

further, note on the high-probability event  $e_{\delta}$  we have  $\theta_{*} \in \mathcal{c}_{t}(\delta) \cap \theta$  for all  $t \geq 1$ . this implies that under  $e_{\delta}$  we have  $\theta_{*} \in \mathcal{w}_{t}$  for all  $t \geq 1$  (as a result, the set  $\{\mathcal{w}_t\}_t$  are therefore not empty).

in the following, we will use the following notation:

$$
\theta^{\prime}_{t}:= \operatorname *{arg  min}_{\theta \in \mathcal{c}_{t}(\delta)\cap \theta}\dot{\mu} (x^{ \top}_{t}\theta).
$$

first, for all  $\theta_t \in \mathcal{w}_t$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbf {h} _ {t} (\theta_ {t}) = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta_ {t}\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\top} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \\ \geq \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \inf  _ {\theta \in \mathcal {c} _ {s} (\delta) \cap \theta} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\top} \theta\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\top} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} \quad \left(\theta_ {t} \in \mathcal {w} _ {t} \text {a n d e q u a t i o n (2 1)}\right) \\ = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {s} ^ {\prime}\right) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} := \mathbf {l} _ {t} \\ \end{array}
$$

therefore for all  $x\in \mathcal{x}$

$$
\left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}\right)} \leq \left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {l} _ {t} ^ {- 1}}. \tag {22}
$$

also, a first-order taylor expansion gives that for all  $x \in \mathcal{x}$  and  $\theta_t \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta) \cap \mathcal{w}_t$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t}\right) \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) + m \left| x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \left(\theta_ {t} - \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \right| \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) + m \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \| \theta_ {t} - \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime} \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \quad (\text {c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z}) \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) + m \| x \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \quad \tag {equation(17)} \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) + m \sqrt {\kappa} \left\| x \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \quad \left(\mathbf {g} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \geq \kappa^ {- 1} \mathbf {v} _ {t}\right) \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) + 2 m \sqrt {\kappa} \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \quad \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}, \theta_ {t} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta)\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

where we used that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \leq \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} + \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {g} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)} \\ \leq \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \left(\left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}\right)} + \left\| g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right)}\right) \quad (\text {l e m m a} 1 0) \\ \leq 2 \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \quad \left(\theta_ {t}, \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime} \in \mathcal {c} _ {t} (\delta)\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

unpacking, we obtain that for all  $x\in \mathcal{x}$ $\theta_t\in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)\cap \mathcal{w}_t$

$$
\begin{array}{l} \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t}) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {t})} \leq \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {t})} + 2 m \sqrt {\kappa} \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta_ {t})} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathbf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t}\right)} + 2 m \kappa \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \quad \left(\mathbf {h} _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t}\right) \geq \kappa^ {- 1} \mathbf {v} _ {t}\right) \\ \leq \dot {\mu} \left(x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {l} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} + 2 m \kappa \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \tag {23} \\ \end{array}
$$

we first study how the first term of the r.h.s cumulates. for the trajectory  $\{x_{t}\}_{t = 1}^{t}$  let us denote  $\tilde{x}_t\coloneqq \sqrt{\dot{\mu}(x_t^\top\theta_t^{\prime})} x_t$  for every  $1\leq t\leq t$ . note that:

$$
\mathbf {l} _ {t} = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \dot {\mu} (x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {s} ^ {\prime}) x _ {s} x _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d} = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t - 1} \tilde {x} _ {s} \tilde {x} _ {s} ^ {\mathsf {t}} + \lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d}
$$

which means that we can apply the elliptical lemma to  $\sum_{t=1}^{t} \| \tilde{x}_t \|^2_{\mathbf{l}_t^{-1}}$ . hence:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \dot {\mu} \left(x _ {t} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta_ {t} ^ {\prime}\right) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {l} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \leq \sqrt {l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {\mathsf {t}} \| \tilde {x} _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {l} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \\ \leq \sqrt {l} \sqrt {t} \sqrt {\sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| \tilde {x} _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {l} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}} \quad \text {(c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z)} \\ \leq \sqrt {l t} \sqrt {2 \max  (1 , l / \lambda)} \sqrt {\log (\det  (\mathbf {l} _ {t + 1}) \lambda^ {- d})} (lemma 15) \\ \leq \sqrt {l t} \sqrt {2 \max  (1 , l / \lambda)} \sqrt {d \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)} (lemma 16) \\ \end{array}
$$

for the second term, a second application of lemma 15 and lemma 16 gives:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \quad (t: \rightarrow \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \text {i n c r e a s i n g}) \\ \leq 2 d \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa d \lambda}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

assembling these last two inequalities and equation (23) yields the announced result.

# appendix c. regret proof

# c.1. regret decomposition

the pseudo-regret at round  $t$  is:

$$
r _ {t} = \sum_ {s = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}) - \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t})
$$

we will consider optimistic algorithms, that is algorithms that at round  $t$ , for a given estimator  $\theta_t$  of  $\theta_*$  and a given exploration bonus  $\epsilon_t(x)$  plays the action

$$
x _ {t} = \underset {x \in \mathcal {x} _ {t}} {\arg \max} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} x) + \epsilon_ {t} (x)
$$

the following lemma characterizes the regret of such an algorithm.

lemma 14. for any  $t \geq 1$ :

$$
r _ {t} \leq \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x _ {t}, \theta_ {t}) + \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t} (x _ {t}) + \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x _ {*} ^ {t}, \theta_ {t}) - \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t} (x _ {*} ^ {t})
$$

proof. by removing and adding  $\sum_{t=1}^{t} \mu(\theta_t^t x_*^t)$  and  $\sum_{t=1}^{t} \mu(\theta_t^t x_t)$  one has that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} r _ {t} = \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}) - \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t}) = \left[ \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}) - \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}) \right] + \left[ \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {*} ^ {t}) - \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t}) \right] + \left[ \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t}) - \mu (\boldsymbol {\theta} _ {*} ^ {t} \boldsymbol {x} _ {t}) \right] \\ \leq \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x _ {t}, \theta_ {t}) + \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x _ {*} ^ {t}, \theta_ {t}) + \left[ \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu (\theta_ {t} ^ {t} x _ {*} ^ {t}) - \mu (\theta_ {t} ^ {t} x _ {t}) \right] \\ \end{array}
$$

note that by definition of  $x_{t}$ :

$$
\mu \left(x _ {t} ^ {t} \theta_ {t}\right) + \epsilon_ {t} \left(x _ {t}\right) \geq \mu \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {t} x _ {*} ^ {t}\right) + \epsilon_ {t} \left(x _ {*} ^ {t}\right)
$$

which yields the announced results.

note that under the assumption that for all  $t \geq 1$  and all  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ , if we have  $\epsilon_t(x) \geq \delta^{\mathrm{pred}}(x, \theta_t)$  lemma 14 directly yields that:

$$
r _ {t} \leq 2 \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t} (x _ {t})

# c.2. proof of theorem 2

theorem 2 (regret of logistic-ucb-1). with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(1)} \leq c _ {1} l \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t}
$$

with  $c_1 = \sqrt{32d(1 + 2s)\max(1,1 / (\kappa\lambda))\log\left(1 + \frac{t}{\kappa\lambda d}\right)}$ . furthermore, if  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then:

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(1)} = \mathcal {o} \left(\sqrt {\kappa} \cdot d \cdot \sqrt {t} \log (t)\right).
$$

proof. during this proof we work under the good event  $e_{\delta}$ , which holds with probability at least  $1 - \delta$  (as shown in lemma 1).

we start by showing that  $\theta_t^{(1)}\in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  for all  $t\geq 1$ . indeed:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {(1)}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {(1)}\right)} = \min  _ {\theta \in \theta} \left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \quad \text {(d e f i n i t i o n o f} \theta_ {t} ^ {(1)} \\ \leq \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \quad \left(\theta_ {*} \in \theta\right) \\ \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \quad \text {(l e m m a 8 , e _ {\delta} h o l d s .)} \\ \end{array}
$$

which proves the desired result.

as  $\theta_t^{(1)} \in \mathcal{c}_t$  we have from lemma 2 that for all  $t \geq 1$  and  $x \in \mathcal{x}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta_ {t} ^ {(1)}) \leq l \sqrt {4 + 8 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \\ = \epsilon_ {t, 1} (x) \\ \end{array}
$$

and therefore by lemma 14 we have that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} r _ {t} \leq 2 \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t, 1} (x _ {t}) \\ = 4 l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \\ \leq 4 l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} \quad \left(\gamma_ {t} (\delta) \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta)\right) \\ \leq 4 l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t} \sqrt {\sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}} \quad \text {(c a u c h y - s c h w a r t z)} \\ \leq 4 l \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \sqrt {\kappa} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t} \sqrt {2 \max  (1 , 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(\det  \left(\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1}\right) (\kappa \lambda) ^ {- d}\right)} \quad (lemma 15) \\ \leq l \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {\kappa} \sqrt {t} \sqrt {3 2 d (1 + 2 s) \max  (1 , (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa \lambda d}\right)} (lemma 16) \\ \end{array}
$$

which concludes the proof of the first statement. the second is immediate when realizing that when  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then  $\gamma_t(\delta) = \mathcal{o}(d^{1 / 2}\cdot \log (t)^{1 / 2})$

# c.3. proof of theorem 3

theorem 3 (regret of logistic-ucb-2). with probability at least  $1 - \delta$ :

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(2)} \leq c _ {2} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {t} + c _ {3} \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \kappa
$$

with

$$
c _ {2} = (4 + 8 s) \sqrt {2 d l \max  (1 , l / \lambda) \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)}
$$

$$
c _ {3} = m d \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa d \lambda}\right) (8 + 1 6 s) (2 + 2 \sqrt {1 + 2 s})
$$

furthermore if  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then:

$$
r _ {t} ^ {(2)} = \mathcal {o} \left(d \cdot \sqrt {t} \log (t) + \kappa \cdot d ^ {2} \cdot \log (t) ^ {2}\right)
$$

proof. during this proof we work under the good event  $e_{\delta}$ , which holds with probability at least  $1 - \delta$  (as shown in lemma 1).

we start this proof by showing that  $\theta_t^{(2)} \in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$ . note that under  $e_{\delta}$ , we have  $\theta_* \in \bigcap_{s=1}^{t-1} \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)$  for all  $t \geq 1$  and therefore  $\theta_* \in \mathcal{w}_t$ . further:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {(2)}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {t} ^ {(2)}\right)} = \min  _ {\theta \in \mathcal {w} _ {t}} \left\| g _ {t} (\theta) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \quad \text {(d e f i n i t i o n o f} \theta_ {t} ^ {(2)} \\ \leq \left\| g _ {t} \left(\theta_ {*}\right) - g _ {t} \left(\hat {\theta} _ {t}\right) \right\| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\theta_ {*}\right)} \quad \left(\theta_ {*} \in \mathcal {w} _ {t}\right) \\ \leq \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \quad \left(\text {l e m m a 8}, e _ {\delta} \text {h o l d s .}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

which proves the desired result.

therefore  $\theta_t^{(2)}\in \mathcal{c}_t(\delta)\cap \mathcal{w}_t$  and we can apply lemma 3. from this we know that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \delta^ {\mathrm {p r e d}} (x, \theta_ {t} ^ {(2)}) \leq (2 + 4 s) \dot {\mu} (x ^ {\mathsf {t}} \theta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\theta)} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + (4 + 8 s) m \kappa \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \\ = \epsilon_ {t, 2} (x) \\ \end{array}
$$

and therefore thanks to lemma 14 we have:

$$
\begin{array}{l} r _ {t} \leq 2 \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \epsilon_ {t, 2} (x _ {t}) \\ \leq (4 + 8 s) \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \dot {\mu} \left(\bar {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} x _ {t}\right) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\bar {\theta} _ {t})} \gamma_ {t} (\delta) + (8 + 1 6 s) m \kappa \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \\ \leq (4 + 8 s) \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \dot {\mu} \left(\bar {\theta} _ {t} ^ {t} x _ {t}\right) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} \left(\bar {\theta} _ {t}\right)} + (8 + 1 6 s) m \kappa \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \tag {24} \\ \end{array}
$$

note that:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \leq 2 \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(\det  \left(\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1}\right) (\lambda \kappa) ^ {- d}\right) (lemma 15) \\ \leq 2 d \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{\kappa \lambda d}\right) (lemma 16) \\ \end{array}
$$

and according to lemma 4 we have:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \mu \left(x _ {t} ^ {t} \bar {\theta} _ {t}\right) \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {h} _ {t} ^ {- 1} (\bar {\theta} _ {t})} \leq \sqrt {l t} \sqrt {2 \max  (1 , l / \lambda)} \sqrt {d \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)} \\ + 4 d m \kappa \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {1 + 2 s} \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{d \kappa \lambda}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

assembling these last two inequalities with equation (24) gives:

$$
\begin{array}{l} r _ {t} \leq (4 + 8 s) \gamma_ {t} (\delta) \sqrt {l t} \sqrt {2 d \max  (1 , l / \lambda) \log \left(1 + \frac {l t}{d \lambda}\right)} \\ + m \kappa d (8 + 1 6 s) \gamma_ {t} ^ {2} (\delta) \max  (1, 1 / (\kappa \lambda)) \log \left(1 + \frac {t}{d \kappa \lambda}\right) (2 + 2 \sqrt {1 + 2 s}) \\ \end{array}
$$

which concludes the proof of the first statement. the second is immediate when realizing that when  $\lambda = d\log (t)$  then  $\gamma_t(\delta) = \mathcal{o}(d^{1 / 2}\cdot \log (t)^{1 / 2})$

# appendix d. useful lemmas

the following lemma is a version of the elliptical potential lemma and can be extracted from lemma 11 in abbasi-yadkori et al. (2011). we remind its statement and its proof here for the sake of completeness.

lemma 15 (elliptical potential). let  $\{x_s\}_{s=1}^{\infty}$  a sequence in  $\mathbb{r}^d$  such that  $\| x_s \|_2 \leq x$  for all  $s \in \mathbb{n}$ , and let  $\lambda$  be a non-negative scalar. for  $t \geq 1$  define  $\mathbf{v}_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t-1} x_s x_s^t + \lambda \mathbf{i}_d$ . the following inequality holds:

$$
\sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \leq 2 \max (1, \frac {x ^ {2}}{\lambda}) \log \frac {\det (\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1})}{\lambda^ {d}}
$$

proof. by definition of  $\mathbf{v}_t$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left| \mathbf {v} _ {t + 1} \right| = \left| \mathbf {v} _ {t} + x _ {t} x _ {t} ^ {t} \right| \\ = | \mathbf {v} _ {t} | \left| \mathbf {i} _ {d} + \mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1 / 2} x _ {t} x _ {t} ^ {t} \mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1 / 2} \right| \\ = \left| \mathbf {v} _ {t} \right| \left(1 + \left\| x _ {t} \right\| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

and therefore by taking the log on both side of the equation and summing from  $t = 1$  to  $t$ :

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \log \left(1 + \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}\right) = \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \log | \mathbf {v} _ {t + 1} | - \log | \mathbf {v} _ {t} | \\ = \log \left(\frac {\operatorname* {d e t} (\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1})}{\operatorname* {d e t} (\lambda \mathbf {i} _ {d})}\right) \quad \text {(t e l e s c o p i c s u m)} \\ = \log \left(\frac {\operatorname* {d e t} \left(\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1}\right)}{\lambda^ {d}}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

remember that for all  $x \in [0,1]$  we have the inequality  $\log(1 + x) \geq x/2$ . also note that  $\|x_t\|_{\mathbf{v}_t^{-1}}^2 \leq x^2/\lambda$ . therefore:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \log \left(\frac {\operatorname* {d e t} (\mathbf {v} _ {t + 1})}{\lambda^ {d}}\right) = \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \log \left(1 + \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}\right) \\ \geq \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \log \left(1 + \frac {1}{\operatorname* {m a x} (1 , x ^ {2} / \lambda)} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2}\right) \\ \geq \frac {1}{2 \max (1 , x ^ {2} / \lambda)} \sum_ {t = 1} ^ {t} \| x _ {t} \| _ {\mathbf {v} _ {t} ^ {- 1}} ^ {2} \\ \end{array}
$$

which yields the announced result.
