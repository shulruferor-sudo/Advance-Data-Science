# Secure MIMO Transmission via Intelligent Reflecting Surface

Limeng Dong, Hui-Ming Wang Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract-In this letter, we consider an intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) assisted Gaussian multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) wiretap channel in which a multi-antenna transmitter communicates with a multi-antenna receiver in the presence of a multi-antenna eavesdropper. To maximize the secrecy rate of this channel, an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm is proposed to jointly optimize the transmit covariance  $\mathbf{R}$  at transmitter and phase shift coefficient  $\mathrm{Q}$  at IRS by fixing the other as a constant. When  $\mathrm{Q}$  is fixed, existing numerical algorithm is used to search for global optimal  $\mathbf{R}$ . When  $\mathbf{R}$  is fixed, three successive approximation to the objective function to surrogate lower bound is applied and minorization-maximization (MM) algorithm is proposed to optimize the local optimal  $\mathbf{Q}$ . Simulation results have been provided to validate the convergence and performance of the proposed AO algorithm.

Index Terms—Intelligent reflecting surface, MIMO, phase shift, alternating optimization, MM.

# I. INTRODUCTION

Intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) has drawn wide attention for its applications in wireless communications. IRS is a low complexity software-controlled passive metasurface which could significantly help enhancing user's transmission rate with very low power consumption [1]. Motivated by these advantages, IRS was recently applied to the study in physical layer security, and several research results about secrecy rate maximization of IRS-assisted multiple-input multiple-output (MISO) wiretap channels wiretap channels were established, including single user case [2]-[4] and downlink multi-user case [5][6]. All these studies indicate that IRS significantly enhance user's secrecy rate.

However, all the aforementioned contributions in the current literatures [2]-[6] are only restricted to MISO settings, i.e., only single antenna at the receiver as well as eavesdropper are considered. When multi-input multi-output (MIMO) is considered, there are two significant differences about the optimization problems compared with that in conventional MISO case. Firstly, in MIMO systems, beamforming is not always optimal solution. Therefore, we need to optimize an transmit covariance instead of beamformer vector in the

Therefore, all these existing solutions for the MISO case fail to the MIMO case. To the best of our knowledge, the study of IRS-assisted MIMO wiretap channel is still an open problem and there is no existing numerical or analytical solutions to maximize its secrecy rate.

Motivated by the aforementioned aspects, the main contribution in this letter is that we consider an IRS-assisted Gaussian MIMO wiretap channel, and aim at maximizing the user's secrecy rate numerically. To solve this non-convex problem, an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm is proposed to jointly optimize the transmit covariance  $\mathbf{R}$  at transmitter as well as phase shift coefficient  $\mathbf{Q}$  at IRS. When  $\mathbf{Q}$  is fixed, the existing algorithm is applied to optimize  $\mathbf{R}$  globally. When  $\mathbf{R}$  is fixed, we approximate the objective function to surrogate lower bound, and the local optimal  $\mathbf{Q}$  is optimized via minorization-maximization (MM) algorithm. In particular, the key difficulty is how to find a proper surrogate function for the complicated objective function. Hence, we apply three successive approximations for the objective function to obtain the proper lower bound so that MM algorithm can be applied, which is significantly different from the existing MM used in the simple MISO case [2][4] in which only one time approximation for the objective is needed due to the simple structure of the objective function. As the convergence is reached, the results returned by the AO algorithm is guaranteed to be a Karush-Khun-Tucker (KKT) solution of the original problem.

# II. CHANNEL MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION

Let us consider an IRS-assisted MIMO wiretap channel model shown as Fig. 1, in which a transmitter Alice, receiver Bob, eavesdropper Eve and an IRS are included. The number of antennas deployed at Alice, Bob and Eve are  $m$ ,  $d$ ,  $e$  respectively, and the number of reflecting elements on the IRS is  $n$ . We assume that Alice, Bob and Eve are located in city's hot spot area, and the direct link between Alice and Bob/Eve is blocked by a building. Then, the IRS is located in a higher

position to help Alice's transmission by passively reflecting the signals to Bob. Due to broadcast nautre of wireless channels, the reflected signal could also be sent to Eve. Therefore, the main task for IRS is to adjust the phase shift for signals by the reflecting elements so as to increase the information rate at Bob but decrease the information leakage to Eve. Based on these settings, the received signals at Bob and Eve are expressed as

$$
\mathbf {y} _ {B} = \mathbf {H} _ {I B} \mathbf {Q} \mathbf {H} _ {A I} \mathbf {x} + \boldsymbol {\xi} _ {B}, \mathbf {y} _ {E} = \mathbf {H} _ {I E} \mathbf {Q} \mathbf {H} _ {A I} \mathbf {x} + \boldsymbol {\xi} _ {E}, \tag {1}
$$

respectively where  $\mathbf{x}$  is the transmitted signal,  $\mathbf{Q}$  is the diagonal phase shift matrix for IRS, in which the diagonal element is  $e^{j\theta_i}$  ( $i = 1,2,\dots,n$ ),  $\theta_{i}$  is the phase shift coefficient at reflecting element  $i$ ,  $\mathbf{H}_{AI}$ ,  $\mathbf{H}_{IB}$  and  $\mathbf{H}_{IE}$  are the channel matrices representing the direct link of Alice-IRS, IRS-Bob and IRS-Eve respectively,  $\xi_B$  and  $\xi_{E}$  represent noise at Bob and Eve respectively with i.i.d. entries distributed as  $\mathcal{CN}(0,1)$ . And we consider that full channel state information (CSI) is available at Alice, which can be achieved by modern adaptive system design, where channels are estimated at Bob and Eve, and send back to Alice. We note that Eve is just other user in the system and it also share its CSI with Alice but is untrusted by Bob. The controller is used to coordinates Alice and IRS for channel acquisition and data transmission tasks [3].

Given (1), the secrecy rate maximization of this model can be expressed as the following problem P1.

$$
\begin{array}{l} P 1: \max  _ {\mathbf {R}, \mathbf {Q}} C _ {s} (\mathbf {R}, \mathbf {Q}) = \log_ {2} \frac {| \mathbf {I} + \mathbf {H} _ {B} \mathbf {R} \mathbf {H} _ {B} ^ {H} |}{| \mathbf {I} + \mathbf {H} _ {E} \mathbf {R} \mathbf {H} _ {E} ^ {H} |} \\ s. t. \quad \mathbf {R} \geq \mathbf {0}, t r (\mathbf {R}) \leq P, | \mathbf {Q} _ {i, i} | = 1, (i = 1, 2, \dots , n) \tag {2} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\mathbf{H}_i = \mathbf{H}_{Ii}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{H}_{AI}, i\in \{B,E\}$ ,  $P$  denotes total transmit power budget for Alice,  $\mathbf{R} = E\{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^H\}$  stands for the transmit covariance for Alice and where the unit modulus constraint  $|\mathbf{Q}_{i,i}| = 1$  ensures that each reflecting element in IRS does not change the amplitude of the signals. Obviously, the determinant part in the objective function of P1 cannot be simplified to scalar formular as for MISO case, thereby significantly increasing the difficulty to solve this problem.

# III. ALTERNATING OPTIMIZATION ALGORITHM

To solve this new non-convex problem, we propose an iterative AO algorithm to optimize  $\mathbf{R}$  and  $\mathbf{Q}$  alternatively by fixing the other as constant.

We firstly fix  $\mathbf{Q}$  as a constant and maximize  $\mathbf{R}$ . Note that when  $\mathbf{Q}$  is fixed,  $\mathbf{H}_B$  and  $\mathbf{H}_E$  are also fixed so that P1 is reduced to a secrecy capacity optimization problem of general Gaussian MIMO wiretap channel. To solve this problem, we apply the key Theorem 1 in [7] so that the original problem is equivalently transformed to a convex-concave max-min optimization problem. Then, we apply the existing algorithm in [7] which is based on barrier method in combination with Newton method and backtracking line search method to globally optimized  $\mathbf{R}$ . Note that in [7], the algorithm was developed only based on real-valued channel matrix case. Therefore, we extend this algorithm to complex-valued channel cases by re-deriving the gradient and Hessians of the barrier objective function. Using the same steps of proof illustrated in [7], the non-singularity of Hessian matrix as well as global convergence of the algorithm still can be proved for the complex-valued channel case. Here we omit the detailed steps of this algorithm due to page limit.

The next step is to optimize  $\mathbf{Q}$  for fixed  $\mathbf{R}$  in the subproblem, which can be express as P2.

$$
\begin{array}{l} P 2: \max  _ {\mathbf {Q}} f (\mathbf {Q}) = f _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) + f _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}) \\ s. t. \quad | \mathbf {Q} _ {i, i} | = 1, i = 1, 2, \dots , n \tag {3} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $f_{B}(\mathbf{Q}) = \log_{2}|\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{H}_{IB}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{Q}^{H}\mathbf{H}_{IB}^{H}|$ ,  $f_{E}(\mathbf{Q}) = -\log_{2}|\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{H}_{IE}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{Q}^{H}\mathbf{H}_{IE}^{H}|$  and where  $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{H}_{AI}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{H}_{AI}^{H}$ . P2 is a complicated non-convex problem with both non-convex objective function and constraints, and the existing solutions (e.g., semi-definite relaxation and fractional programming) for IRS-assisted MISO case [2][3] cannot be directly applied to our problem. To optimize Q, we apply MM algorithm to solve P2, in which the key idea is to firstly obtain an approximately lower bound (i.e., surrogate function) of the objective, and then iteratively compute the optimal value of this bound subject to the constraints. If the bound is constructed properly, any converged point generated by MM is a KKT point (i.e., local optimal point) for the original problem. For detailed explanation of MM, please refer to [8].

Since the objective function  $f(\mathbf{Q})$  in P2 consists of two complicated log determinant functions, the difficulty to directly find its proper lower bound has significantly increased. Therefore, the solution we apply is firstly find the lower bound for  $f_{B}(\mathbf{Q})$  and  $f_{E}(\mathbf{Q})$  respectively, and then formulate the new approximated problem by adding this two bounds together. After that, we make further two successive approximations to this bound to formulate the final surrogate function of  $f(\mathbf{Q})$ , and apply MM algorithm to optimize a local optimal solution of  $\mathbf{Q}$ .

Firstly, consider  $\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}$  is a feasible point satisfying the unit mudulous constraint, a quadratic lower bound of the function  $f_{E}(\mathbf{Q})$  can be expressed as

$$
f _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}) \geq \bar {f} _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}, \tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {1} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) \tag {4}
$$

where

$$
\begin{array}{l} \bar {f} _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}, \tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) = - t r \left(\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {E} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} \mathbf {Q L Q} ^ {H} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} ^ {H}\right), \\ C _ {1} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) = - \log_ {2} | \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {E} | + t r (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {E} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} \mathbf {L} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} ^ {H} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} ^ {H}), \\ \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {E} = \mathbf {I} + \mathbf {H} _ {I E} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} \mathbf {L} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} ^ {H} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} ^ {H}. \\ \end{array}
$$

The inequality in (4) is obtained via the lemma: for any matrix  $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$  and  $\tilde{\mathbf{A}} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ ,

$$
\log_ {2} | \mathbf {A} | \leq \log_ {2} | \tilde {\mathbf {A}} | + t r \left(\tilde {\mathbf {A}} ^ {- 1} (\mathbf {A} - \tilde {\mathbf {A}})\right). \tag {5}
$$

Then, to obtain the lower bound of  $f_{B}(\mathbf{Q})$ , let  $\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{H}_{IB}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}$  according to matrix inversion lemma,  $f_{B}(\mathbf{Q})$  can be further expressed as

$$
f _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) = - l o g _ {2} | \mathbf {I} - \mathbf {T} (\mathbf {I} + \mathbf {T} ^ {H} \mathbf {T}) ^ {- 1} \mathbf {T} ^ {H} |. \tag {6}
$$

Let  $\tilde{\mathbf{T}} = \mathbf{H}_{IB}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ , by applying (5),  $f_{B}(\mathbf{Q})$  is also lower bounded by

$$
\begin{array}{l} f _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) \geq - \log_ {2} | \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} | - t r \left(\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \left(\mathbf {Q} _ {B} - \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B}\right)\right) \\ = C _ {2} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + h _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) \tag {7} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $C_2(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}) = -\log_2|\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}_B| + tr(\mathbf{I}) - tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}_B^{-1}), h_B(\mathbf{Q}) = tr(\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}_B^{-1}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{T}^H\mathbf{T})^{-1}\mathbf{T}^H), \tilde{\mathbf{Q}}_B = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{H}_{IB}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{L}\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}^H\mathbf{H}_{IB}^H.$

Hence, combining (4) and (7), the approximated problem of P2 can be expressed as P3

$$
P 3: \max  _ {\mathbf {Q}} \bar {f} _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}, \tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {1} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {2} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + h _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}), s. t. \tag {3}.
$$

However, we find that it is still difficult to optimize  $\mathbf{Q}$  given  $\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}$  due to the complicated term  $h_B(\mathbf{Q})$  as well as the constraint (3). Therefore, a second approximation of the objective function in P3 is needed. In the following, we apply the key lemma of matrix fractional functions [9]: for any positive semi-definite matrix  $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{m \times m}$  and positive definite matrix  $\mathbf{B}, \tilde{\mathbf{B}} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ , and  $\mathbf{X}, \tilde{\mathbf{X}} \in \mathbb{C}^{m \times n}$ ,

$$
\begin{array}{l} t r (\mathbf {A X B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {X} ^ {H}) \\ \geq t r \left(\mathbf {A} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} \tilde {\mathbf {B}} ^ {- 1} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} ^ {H}\right) - t r \left(\mathbf {A} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} \tilde {\mathbf {B}} ^ {- 1} (\mathbf {B} - \tilde {\mathbf {B}}) \tilde {\mathbf {B}} ^ {- 1} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} ^ {H}\right) \\ + t r (\mathbf {A} (\mathbf {X} - \tilde {\mathbf {X}}) \tilde {\mathbf {B}} ^ {- 1} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} ^ {H}) + t r (\mathbf {A} \tilde {\mathbf {X}} \tilde {\mathbf {B}} ^ {- 1} (\mathbf {X} - \tilde {\mathbf {X}}) ^ {H}). \\ \end{array}
$$

Therefore, by applying this lemma to the term  $h_B(\mathbf{Q})$  via setting  $\mathbf{A} = \tilde{\mathbf{Q}}_B^{-1}$ ,  $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{T}$ ,  $\tilde{\mathbf{X}} = \tilde{\mathbf{T}}$ ,  $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{I} + \mathbf{T}^H\mathbf{T}$  and  $\tilde{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{I} + \tilde{\mathbf{T}}^H\tilde{\mathbf{T}}$  and after some manipulations, the lower bound of  $f(\mathbf{Q})$  can be further expressed as

$$
\begin{array}{l} f (\mathbf {Q}) \geq \bar {f} _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}, \tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {1} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {2} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + h _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) \\ \geq \bar {f} _ {E} (\mathbf {Q}, \tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {1} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + C _ {2} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) + g _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) + C _ {3} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) \tag {8} \\ \end{array}
$$

where

$$
\begin{array}{l} C _ {3} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) = - t r \left(\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1}\right) + t r \left(\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {J} _ {B} \tilde {\mathbf {T}} ^ {H} \tilde {\mathbf {T}} \mathbf {J} _ {B} ^ {H}\right), \\ g _ {B} (\mathbf {Q}) = - t r (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {J} _ {B} \mathbf {T} ^ {H} \mathbf {T} \mathbf {J} _ {B} ^ {H}) + t r (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {J} _ {B} \mathbf {T} ^ {H}) \\ + t r \left(\mathbf {T} \mathbf {J} _ {B} ^ {H} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1}\right) \\ \end{array}
$$

and where  $\mathbf{J}_B = \tilde{\mathbf{T}} (\mathbf{I} + \tilde{\mathbf{T}}^H\tilde{\mathbf{T}})^{-1}$

In the following, we express (8) to a more tractable form. Let  $\mathbf{q} = [e^{j\theta_1}, e^{j\theta_2}, \dots, e^{j\theta_n}]^T$ , and let

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbf {A} _ {1} = \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {J} _ {B} \mathbf {L} ^ {\frac {1}{2}}, \mathbf {A} _ {2} = \mathbf {H} _ {I B} ^ {H} \mathbf {H} _ {I B}, \mathbf {A} _ {3} = \mathbf {L} ^ {\frac {1}{2}} \mathbf {J} _ {B} ^ {H}, \\ \mathbf {A} _ {4} = \mathbf {H} _ {I B} ^ {H} \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {B} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {J} _ {B} \mathbf {L} ^ {\frac {1}{2}}, \mathbf {A} _ {5} = \tilde {\mathbf {Q}} _ {E} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {H} _ {I E}. \\ \end{array}
$$

We firstly apply the lemma of matrix identity in [10]: for any matrix  $\mathbf{A}$ ,  $\mathbf{B}$  and diagonal matrix  $\mathbf{V}$  with proper sizes,  $tr(\mathbf{V}^H\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{B}) = \mathbf{v}^H (\mathbf{A}\odot \mathbf{B}^T)\mathbf{v}$  holds where the entries in

$\mathbf{v}$  are all diagonal elements in  $\mathbf{V}$ . Using this lemma,  $f(\mathbf{Q})$  is lower bounded as

$$
\begin{array}{l} f (\mathbf {Q}) \geq - t r \left(\mathbf {Q} ^ {H} \mathbf {H} _ {I E} ^ {H} \mathbf {A} _ {5} \mathbf {Q L}\right) - t r \left(\mathbf {Q} ^ {H} \mathbf {A} _ {2} \mathbf {Q} \mathbf {A} _ {3} \mathbf {A} _ {1}\right) \\ + t r \left(\mathbf {A} _ {4} \mathbf {Q} ^ {H}\right) + t r \left(\mathbf {Q} \mathbf {A} _ {4} ^ {H}\right) + \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {3} C _ {i} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) \\ = - g (\mathbf {q}) + 2 R e \left\{\mathbf {q} ^ {H} \mathbf {a} \right\} + \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {3} C _ {i} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) \tag {9} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $g(\mathbf{q}) = \mathbf{q}^H\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{A}_2\odot (\mathbf{A}_3\mathbf{A}_1)^T + (\mathbf{H}_{IE}^H\mathbf{A}_5)\odot \mathbf{L}^T$  and where the entries in a are all diagonal entires in  $\mathbf{A}_4$ . It can be known that given fixed feasible  $\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}$ , the bound (9) is a quadratic convex function respect to  $\mathbf{q}$ . However, since  $\mathbf{q}$  needs to satisfy the non-convex unit modulus constraint  $|\mathbf{q}_i| = 1$ , it is still difficult to use MM algorithm to optimize (9). Hence, a third approximation of  $f(\mathbf{Q})$  is needed by finding a surrogate function of  $g(\mathbf{q})$ , which is expressed as follows [8].

$$
\begin{array}{l} g (\mathbf {q}) \leq \mathbf {q} ^ {H} \lambda_ {m a x} (\mathbf {Z}) \mathbf {I q} - 2 R e \{\mathbf {q} ^ {H} (\lambda_ {m a x} (\mathbf {Z}) \mathbf {I} - \mathbf {Z}) \tilde {\mathbf {q}} \} \\ + \tilde {\mathbf {q}} ^ {H} (\lambda_ {m a x} (\mathbf {Z}) \mathbf {I} - \mathbf {Z}) \tilde {\mathbf {q}} \\ = 2 n \lambda_ {m a x} (\mathbf {Z}) - 2 R e \left\{\mathbf {q} ^ {H} \left(\lambda_ {m a x} (\mathbf {Z}) \mathbf {I} - \mathbf {Z}\right) \tilde {\mathbf {q}} \right\} - \tilde {\mathbf {q}} ^ {H} \mathbf {Z} \tilde {\mathbf {q}} \\ = \tilde {g} (\mathbf {q}, \tilde {\mathbf {q}}) \tag {10} \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\tilde{\mathbf{q}}$  is the feasible point, the entries of which are the diagonal entries of  $\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}$ . Hence,  $f(\mathbf{Q})$  can be further approximated by

$$
f (\mathbf {Q}) \geq - \tilde {g} (\mathbf {q}, \tilde {\mathbf {q}}) + 2 R e \left\{\mathbf {q} ^ {H} \mathbf {a} \right\} + \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {3} C _ {i} (\tilde {\mathbf {Q}}) \tag {11}
$$

By dropping the constant terms of this bound, P3 can be further approximated to P4.

$$
P 4: \max  _ {\mathbf {q}} R e \{\mathbf {q} ^ {H} \mathbf {v} \} \quad s. t. | \mathbf {q} _ {i} | = 1, i = 1, 2,..., n
$$

where  $\mathbf{v} = (\lambda_{max}(\mathbf{Z})\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Z})\tilde{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{a}$ . Obviously, the objective is maximized only when the phase of  $\mathbf{q}_i$  and  $\mathbf{v}_i$  are equal. Thus, the closed-form global optimal solution for P4 is expressed as

$$
\mathbf {q} _ {\text {o p t}} = \left[ e ^ {j a r g \left(\mathbf {v} _ {1}\right)}, e ^ {j a r g \left(\mathbf {v} _ {2}\right)}, \dots , e ^ {j a r g \left(\mathbf {v} _ {n}\right)} \right] ^ {T}. \tag {12}
$$

Therefore, by initializing a feasible point  $\tilde{\mathbf{q}}$  and use MM to optimize P4, a KKT solution of P2 given fixed  $\mathbf{R}$  can be obtained.

Based on the above analysis, the overall AO algorithm for maximizing the secrecy rate of IRS-assisted MIMO wiretap channel is summarized as Algorithm 1. Since  $\mathbf{R}$  and  $\mathbf{Q}$  are optimized alternatively, the objective function  $C_s(\mathbf{R}_k,\mathbf{Q}_k)$  is monotonically increasing with number of iterations  $k$ . Moreover, since  $\mathbf{R}$  and  $\mathbf{Q}$  are both bounded by the independent constraints in P1, according to Cauchy's theorem [5], the algorithm is guaranteed to converge.

# V. CONCLUSION

In this letter, the secrecy rate maximization problem of an IRS-assisted Gaussian MIMO wiretap channel is studied. To solve this difficult non-convex problem, an AO algorithm is proposed to jointly optimize the transmit covariance at Alice and phase shift coefficient at IRS. Simulation results have validated the monotonic convergence of the proposed AO algorithm, and it is shown that the performance of AO is significantly better than the other benchmark schemes.
