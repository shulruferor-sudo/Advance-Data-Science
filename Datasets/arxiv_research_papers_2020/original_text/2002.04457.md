# Community Detection on Mixture Multi-layer Networks via Regularized Tensor Decomposition

Bing-Yi Jing, Ting Li, Zhongyuan Lyu and Dong Xia Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (February 12, 2020)

# Abstract

We study the problem of community detection in multi-layer networks, where pairs of nodes can be related in multiple modalities. We introduce a general framework, i.e., mixture multi-layer stochastic block model (MMSBM), which includes many earlier models as special cases. We propose a tensor-based algorithm (TWIST) to reveal both global/local memberships of nodes, and memberships of layers. We show that the TWIST procedure can accurately detect the communities with small misclassification error as the number of nodes and/or number of layers increases. Numerical studies confirm our theoretical findings. To our best knowledge, this is the first systematic study on the mixture multi-layer networks using tensor decomposition. The method is applied to two real datasets: worldwide trading networks and malaria parasite genes networks, yielding new and interesting findings.

# 1 Introduction

Networks arise in many areas of research and applications, which come in all shapes and sizes. The most studied and best understood are static network models. Many other network models are also in existence, but have been less studied. One such example is the multi-layer networks, which are a powerful representation of relational data, and commonly encountered in contemporary data analysis (Kivelä et al. (2014)). The nodes in a multi-layer network represent the entities of interest and the edges in different layers indicate the multiple relations among those entities. Examples include brain connectivity networks, world trading networks, gene-gene interactive networks and so on. In this paper, we focus on the multi-layer networks with the same nodes set of each layer and there are no edges between two different layers.

The study on multi-layer networks has received an increasing interest. Considering the dependency among the different layers, Paul and Chen (2016) derives consistency results for the community assignments from the maximum likelihood estimators in two models. Consistency properties of various methods for community detection under the multi-layer stochastic block model are investigated in Paul and Chen (2017). Three different matrix factorization-based algorithms are employed in Tang et al. (2009), Nickel et al. (2011) and Dong et al. (2012) separately. Common community structures for multiple networks are identified via two spectral clustering algorithms with theoretical guarantee in Bhattacharyya and Chatterjee (2018). In Arroyo et al. (2019), authors introduce the common subspace independent-edge multiple random graph model to describe a heterogeneous collection of networks with a shared latent structure and propose a joint spectral embedding of adjacency matrices to simultaneously and consistently estimate underlying parameters for each graph. Consistency results for a least squares estimation of memberships under the multi-layer stochastic block model framework are derived in Lei et al. (2019). Several literature focus on recovering the network from a collection of networks with edge contamination. The original network is estimated from multiple noisy realizations utilizing community structure in Le et al. (2018) and low-rank expectation in Levin et al. (2019). A weighted latent position

graph model contaminated via an edge weight gross error model is proposed in Tang et al. (2017) with an estimation methodology based on robust  $L_{q}$  estimation followed by low-rank adjacency spectral decomposition.

In applications, a random effects stochastic block model is proposed by Paul and Chen (2018) for the neuroimaging data and a statistical framework with a significance and a robustness test for detecting common modules in the Drosophila melanogaster dynamic gene regulation network is proposed in Zhang and Cao (2017).

Most of the literature about community detection in multi-layer networks is limited to consistent membership setting, which means all the layers carry information about the same community assignment. However, in reality, different layers may have different community structures. For instance, in a social network, layers related with sports (people connected with the same sport hobbies) may have different community structure with layers about movie taste (people connected with similar movie taste). Understanding the large-scale structure of multi-layer networks is made difficult by the fact that the patterns of one type of link may be similar to, uncorrelated with, or different from the patterns of another type of link. These differences from layer to layer may exist at the level of individual links, connectivity patterns among groups of nodes, or even the hidden groups themselves to which each node belongs. In De Bacco et al. (2017), authors pointed out that, in order to do community detection on multi-layer networks, it is crucial to know which layers have related structure and which layer are unrelated, since redundant information across layers may provide stronger evidence for clear communities than each layer would on its own. Such situation is not clearly discussed in the works mentioned above. Although, in Matias and Miele (2017), authors introduced community structure variety as time varying, it is hard to be applied in general multi-layer networks without time ordering.

In this paper, we introduce a general framework, i.e., mixture multi-layer stochastic block model (MMSBM), and propose a tensor-based algorithm (TWIST) to reveal both global/local memberships of nodes, and memberships of layers. To fix ideas, we start with a simple motivating example, illustrated in Figure 1. We have  $L = 3$  layers of networks

First, we propose a very general model to handle the type of problems discussed above. To be more specific, we will introduce the so-called mixture multi-layer stochastic block model (MMSBM), which can characterize the different community structures among different layers of the multi-layer network. In some way, the MMSBM resembles the relatively well studied multi-layer stochastic block model (MLSBM) Paul and Chen (2017); Han et al. (2015); Paul and Chen (2016). However, the MMSBM is more general in that it allows the multi-layer network to contain different block structures. Thus, the MMSBM not only allows each layer to have different community structures, but also can maintain the consistent structure in the network.

Secondly, we propose a tensor-based method to study the MMSBM. The approach is referred to as the Tucker decomposition with integrated SVD transformation (TWIST). Unlike earlier approaches for multi-layer network analysis, TWIST can uncover the clusters of layers, the local and global membership of nodes simultaneously. On the theoretical front, we prove for MMSBM that TWIST can consistently recover the layer labels and global memberships of nodes under near optimal network sparsity conditions. In addition, network

labels can be exactly recovered under a slightly stronger network sparsity condition. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic study on statistical guarantees about community detection in a mixture multi-layer networks using tensor decomposition. Our primary technical tool is a sharp concentration inequality of sparse tensors which might be of independent interest.

Finally, two real-world applications of the proposed methodology demonstrated to be a powerful tool in analysing multi-layer networks. The algorithm is easy to use and can help practitioners quickly uncover interesting findings, which would otherwise be difficult by using other tools.

Moreover, we make comparisons between our main results and the cutting-edge theoretical results. # 2 Model framework

# 2.1 Mixture multi-layer stochastic block model (MMSBM)

The observed data contains  $L$ -layers of networks on the same set of vertices:  $\mathcal{V} = [n] := \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ :

$$
\mathcal {G} = \{\mathcal {G} _ {l}: l = 1, \dots , L \}.
$$

Assume that these networks are generated from a mixture of  $m$  latent networks with probability  $\pi = (\pi_1, \dots, \pi_m)$ . Denoting  $\ell_l \in \{1, \dots, m\}$  as a latent label of  $\mathcal{G}_l$  with  $1 \leq l \leq L$ ,

then

$$
\mathbb {P} (\ell_ {l} = j) = \pi_ {j}, \quad \text {w i t h} \sum_ {j = 1} ^ {m} \pi_ {j} = 1
$$

Assume that each of the  $m$  classes of networks satisfies the stochastic block model (SBM). More specifically, for  $j \in [m]$ , the  $j$ -th class SBM is described by the membership matrix  $Z_{j} \in \{0,1\}^{n \times K_{j}}$  and the probability matrix  $B_{j} \in [0,1]^{K_{j} \times K_{j}}$  where  $K_{j}$  is the number of communities. Each row of  $Z_{j}$  has exactly one entry which is non-zero. For simplicity, we denote

-  $\operatorname{SBM}(Z_j, B_j) = \text{the } j$ -th SBM with parameter  $Z_j$  and  $B_j$ ,  $j = 1, \dots, m$ .  
-  $\mathcal{V}_k^j =$  the  $k$ -th community in the  $j$ -th SBM. So  $\mathcal{V}_k^j \subset \mathcal{V}$  and  $\cup_{k=1}^{K_j} \mathcal{V}_k^j = \mathcal{V}$ .  
-  $L_{j} = \# \{l : \ell_{l} = j, 1 \leq l \leq L\} =$  the number of layers generated by  $\operatorname{SBM}(Z_{j}, B_{j})$ . Clearly,  $L = \sum_{j=1}^{m} L_{j}$ .  
-  $\mathring{K} = K_{1} + \dots + K_{m}$  and  $\mathbb{L} = \{\ell_l\}_{l=1}^{L}$  and  $\mathbb{V}^j := \{\mathcal{V}_k^j\}_{k=1}^{K_j}$ .

The observed adjacency matrix  $A_{l} \in \{0,1\}^{n \times n}$  of  $\mathcal{G}_l$  obeys Bernoulli distribution:

$$
A _ {l} (i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \sim_ {i i d} \operatorname {B e r n} \big (Z _ {\ell_ {l}} (i _ {1},:) B _ {\ell_ {l}} Z _ {\ell_ {l}} (i _ {2},:) ^ {\top} \big) \iff A _ {l} \sim_ {i i d} \operatorname {B e r n} \big (Z _ {\ell_ {l}} B _ {\ell_ {l}} Z _ {\ell_ {l}} ^ {\top} \big) \qquad (2. 1)
$$

for all  $i_1 \leq i_2 \in [n]$ , where  $Z(i,:)$  denotes the  $i$ -th row of  $Z$ .

The resulting model is referred to as "mixture multi-layer stochastic block model" (MMSBM).

# 2.2 Adjacency tensor and its decomposition

Observing the  $L$  layers of networks, we define the adjacency tensor  $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n \times L}$  so that  $\mathbf{A}$ 's  $l$ -th slice

$$
A (:,: l) = A _ {l}, \quad \forall 1 \leq l \leq L.
$$

See Kolda and Bader (2009) for an introduction to tensor and applications. It follows from (2.1) that

$$
\mathbb {E} (A _ {l} | \ell_ {l}) = Z _ {\ell_ {l}} B _ {\ell_ {l}} Z _ {\ell_ {l}} ^ {\top}, \quad \forall 1 \leq l \leq L,
$$

from which we can derive the following tensor representation, whose proof is in the Appendix.

Lemma 1 (Tensor representation). We have

$$
\mathbb {E} (\mathbf {A} | \mathbb {L}) = \mathbf {B} \times_ {1} \bar {Z} \times_ {2} \bar {Z} \times_ {3} W, \tag {2.2}
$$

where  $\mathbb{L} = \{\ell_l\}_{l = 1}^L$  and

-  $\bar{Z} = (Z_1, Z_2, \dots, Z_m) \in \{0, 1\}^{n \times \hat{K}}$  is the global membership matrix, whereas each  $Z_j$  is the local membership matrix,  
-  $W = (e_{\ell_1}, e_{\ell_2}, \dots, e_{\ell_L})^\top \in \{0, 1\}^{L \times m}$  is the network label matrix with each row of  $W$  having exactly one non-zero entry, and  $e_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$  being the  $j$ -th canonical basis vector,  
-  $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{\hat{K} \times \hat{K} \times m}$  is a 3-way probability tensor whose  $j$ -th frontal slide is

$$
B (:,: j) = d i a g (0 _ {K _ {1}}, \dots , 0 _ {K _ {j - 1}}, B _ {j}, 0 _ {K _ {j + 1}}, \dots , 0 _ {K _ {m}}), \quad 1 \leq j \leq m
$$

with  $0_{K}$  being a  $K\times K$  zero matrix.

# 2.3 Local versus global memberships via Tucker decomposition

The matrix  $\bar{Z}$  defined in Lemma 1 suggests the existence of global community structures. We say that two nodes  $i_1$  and  $i_2$  belong to the same global community if and only if they belong to the same local community for all the  $m$  classes of SBM, i.e.,

$$
\bar {Z} (i _ {1},:) = \bar {Z} (i _ {2},:).
$$

Let  $\bar{K}$  denote the number of global communities. Clearly,  $\max_j K_j \leq \bar{K} \leq \prod_j K_j$ . Denote  $\bar{\mathbb{V}} = \{\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k\}_{k=1}^{\bar{K}}$  the global community clusters such that  $\cup_{k=1}^{\bar{K}} \bar{\mathcal{V}}_k = \mathcal{V}$ . Therefore, for two nodes  $i_1 \neq i_2$ ,

$$
\{i _ {1}, i _ {2} \} \in \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \left\{i _ {1}, i _ {2} \right\} \in \mathcal {V} _ {k _ {j}} ^ {j}, \quad k _ {j} \in [ K _ {j} ], \quad \forall j \in [ m ], \tag {2.3}
$$

Let  $r = \mathrm{rank}(\bar{Z})$  denote the rank of  $\bar{Z}$ . We hereby write the thin SVD of  $\bar{Z}$  as

$$
\bar {Z} = \bar {U} \bar {D} \bar {R} ^ {\top} \tag {2.4}
$$

where  $\bar{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ ,  $\bar{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{(\bar{K}) \times r}$  have orthonormal columns, and  $\bar{D}$  is the singular value diagonal matrix

$$
\bar {D} = d i a g (\sigma_ {1} (\bar {D}), \dots , \sigma_ {r} (\bar {D})) \in \mathbb {R} ^ {r \times r}, \qquad \sigma_ {1} (\bar {D}) \geq \dots \geq \sigma_ {r} (\bar {D}) > 0.
$$

The global community structure can be checked by  $\bar{U}$  as in Lemma 2.

Lemma 2. For  $i_1 \in \bar{\mathcal{V}}_{k_1}$  and  $i_2 \in \bar{\mathcal{V}}_{k_2}$  with  $k_1 \neq k_2$ , then,

$$
\| \bar {U} (i _ {1},:) - \bar {U} (i _ {2},:) \| _ {\ell_ {2}} \geq \frac {1}{\sigma_ {1} (\bar {D})}.
$$

By (2.4), the population adjacency tensor  $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})$  admits the Tucker decomposition as

$$
\mathbb {E} (\mathbf {A} | \mathbb {L}) = \bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {1} \bar {U} \times_ {2} \bar {U} \times_ {3} \bar {W} \tag {2.5}
$$

where the core tensor  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}\in \mathbb{R}^{r\times r\times m}$  is defined by

$$
\bar {\mathbf {C}} = \mathbf {B} \times_ {1} (\bar {D} \bar {R} ^ {\top}) \times_ {2} (\bar {D} \bar {R} ^ {\top}) \times_ {3} D _ {L} ^ {1 / 2} \tag {2.6}
$$

and  $\bar{W} = WD_L^{-1 / 2}\in \mathbb{R}^{L\times m}$  so that  $\bar{W}^{\top}\bar{W} = I_{m}$ , and the diagonal matrix

$$
D _ {L} = \mathrm {d i a g} (L _ {1}, L _ {2}, \dots , L _ {m}).
$$

We assume that  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  has Tucker ranks  $(r,r,m)$ . Further assume that  $m\leq r$ , which is reasonable as one new type of network will introduce at least one new global community.

The decomposition (2.5) shows that the singular vectors of  $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})$  contain the latent network information. More exactly, the singular vectors in the 1-st dimension of  $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})$  could identify the global community structures and singular vectors in the 3-rd dimension could identify the latent network labels. After identifying the latent network labels, a post-processing procedure can identify the local community structures.

# 3 Methodology: TWIST

By observing the multi-layer networks  $\{\mathcal{G}_l\}_{l=1}^L$  satisfying model (2.1), our goals are to:

(1) recover the global community structures of vertices  $\{\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k\}_{k = 1}^K$

(2) identify network classes  $\{\ell_l\}_{l = 1}^L$ , and grouping networks with the same class;  
(3) recover the local community structures of the vertices  $\mathbb{V}^j \coloneqq \{\mathcal{V}_k^j : k \in [K_j]\}$  for all  $j \in [m]$ .

Note that in order to efficiently recover the local community structures, it is necessary to first identify the network classes. As a result, task (3) usually follows from task (2).

By the decomposition of oracle tensor (2.5), the singular vectors  $\bar{U}$  contains information of global memberships since its column space comes from  $\bar{Z}$ . Additionally, the singular vectors  $\bar{W}$  contains information of network classes. Therefore, task (1) and task (2) are both related with the Tucker decomposition of oracle tensor  $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})$ . Since the oracle is unavailable, we seek a low-rank approximation of  $\mathbf{A}$ .

# 3.1 Tucker decomposition with integrated SVD transformation (TWIST)

In order to utilize the low rank structure of the tensor and the non-negative property of the elements, we propose a new algorithm called Tucker decomposition with integrated SVD transformation (TWIST). The general procedure is summarized below and illustrated in Figure 2.

- Step 1: Decomposition of adjacency tensor

Apply the regularized tensor power iterations to  $\mathbf{A}$  to obtain its low-rank approximation. The outputs are  $\widehat{U}$  and  $\widehat{W}$ . Details are given in Algorithm 1.

- Step 2: Global memberships

Apply the standard K-means algorithm on the rows of  $\widehat{U}$  to identify the global community memberships and output  $\widehat{\overline{\mathbb{V}}} = \{\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_k\}_{k=1}^K$ .

- Step 3: Network classes

Use the rows of  $\widehat{W}$  to identify the network classes and output the network classes:

$\widehat{\mathbb{L}} = \{\hat{\ell}_l \in [m]\}_{l=1}^L$ . We can use either the standard K-means or the sup-norm related algorithm (Algorithm 2).

# - Step 4: Local memberships

We can find the local membership  $\mathbb{V}^j = \{\mathcal{V}_k^j\}$  by focusing on networks with the same labels (Lei and Rinaldo (2015); Rohe et al. (2011)). More precisely, for each  $j \in \{1, \ldots, m\}$ , we can apply K-means either

- to the sum of those networks with the same label  $\sum_{l:\hat{\ell}_l = j}A_l$ , or  
- to the sub-tensor  $A(:, :, \{l : \hat{\ell}_l = j\})$ ), those slides with the same labels.

Outputs are  $\widehat{\mathbb{V}}^j = \{\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_k^j\}_{k=1}^{K_j}$ .

# Warm starts for  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  and  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  in Algorithm 1

Computing the optimal low-rank approximation of a tensor  $\mathbf{A}$  is NP-hard in general; see Hillar and Lim (2013). Algorithms with random initializations are almost always trapped in non-informative local minimals which can be nearly orthogonal to the truth, see Arous et al. (2019). To avoid these issues, tensor decomposition algorithms usually run from a warm starting point Zhang and Xia (2018); Xia and Yuan (2019a); Xia et al.  $(2020+)$ ; Richard and Montanari (2014); Jain and Oh (2014); Ke et al. (2019); Zhang (2019); Sun et al. (2017); Cai et al. (2019b); Wang and Li (2018).

In Section 5.5, we will introduce a warm initialization algorithm, obtained by applying a spectral method for initializing  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  by summing up all the network layers. Initialization of  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  is easy whenever  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  is available. We show in Lemma 5 that these initializations can indeed improve estimation accuracy.

# Regularized power iterations for sparse tensor decomposition

Adjacency matrices from some layers are often very sparse and the individual layers are even disconnected graphs. For example, in the Malaria parasite genes networks given in Section 7, three out of nine networks are very sparse and and disconnected. Under these circumstances, the popular tensor power iteration algorithm, i.e., high-order orthogonal iterations (HOOI, see Sheehan and Saad (2007)) may not work. In fact, its statistical optimality was proved by Zhang and Xia (2018) only for dense tensors, while its properties on sparse random tensors remain much more challenging.

To handle sparse random tensors, we employ a regularized tensor power iteration algorithm in Algorithm 1, which was used in Ke et al. (2019) to deal with sparse hypergraph networks. Regularizations to singular vectors  $\widehat{U}^{(t)},\widehat{W}^{(t)}$  are applied before each power iteration. We take  $\widehat{U}^{(t)}$  for example as  $\widehat{W}^{(t)}$  can be treated similarly. The regularization  $\mathcal{P}_{\delta}(U)$  is done by

$$
\mathcal {P} _ {\delta} (U) = \operatorname {S V D} _ {r} \left(U _ {\star}\right) \quad \text {w h e r e} \quad U _ {\star} (i,:) := U (i,:) \cdot \frac {\min  \left\{\delta , \| U (i , :) \| \right\}}{\| U (i , :) \|} \quad i \in [ n ], \tag {3.1}
$$

The effect of regularization is to dampen the influence of "large" rows of  $\widehat{U}^{(t)}$ , which is due to the communities of small sizes, besides stochastic errors. Following Lemma 4, the true singular vectors  $\bar{U}$  are incoherent with  $\max_j\| e_j^\top \bar{U}\| = O(\sqrt{r / n})$  if community sizes are balanced. In practice, we suggest

$$
\hat {\delta} _ {1} = 2 \sqrt {r} \cdot \frac {\operatorname* {m a x} _ {1 \leq i \leq n} \deg_ {i}}{\sqrt {\sum_ {i} \deg_ {i} ^ {2}}} \quad \text {a n d} \quad \hat {\delta} _ {2} = 2 \sqrt {m} \cdot \frac {\operatorname* {m a x} _ {1 \leq l \leq L} \operatorname* {n e g} _ {l}}{\sqrt {\sum_ {l} \operatorname* {n e g} _ {l} ^ {2}}} \tag {3.2}
$$

where the node degree  $\deg_i = \sum_{j,l}A(i,j,l)$  and layer degree  $\mathrm{neg}_l = \sum_{i,j}A(i,j,l)$ .

# K-means with sup-norm distance

Clearly, the accuracy of local membership clustering (Step 4) hinges on the reliability of layer labelling. In Algorithm 2, a sup-norm version of K-means is applied to the singular vectors  $\widehat{W}$  obtained from Algorithm 1, and then outputs the network labels  $\widehat{\mathbb{L}} = \{\hat{\ell}_l\}_{l=1}^L$ . The sup-norm K-means has recently been extensively investigated and shown to perform well in network community detection. See, e.g. Chen et al. (2019); Abbe et al. (2017); Kim et al. (2018) and references therein.

The rationale of Algorithm 2 is that when the rows of  $\bar{W}$  are well-separated (similar to Lemma 2), a row-wise screening of  $\widehat{W}$  can immediately recover the true network labels as long as the row-wise perturbation bound of  $\widehat{W} - W$  is small enough. As shown in Section 5, Algorithm 2 guarantees exact clustering of networks under weak conditions.

In Steps 2-4, one could use alternative methods other than K-means clustering, which might improve its performances. For example, we can use DBSCAN, Gaussian mixture model, the SCORE method (Jin (2015); Jin et al. (2017); Ke et al. (2019)).

# 4.1 Notations and definitions

For ease of exposition, we introduce the following notations.

- Denote  $c, c_{j}, C, C_{j}, C_{j}^{\prime}$ ,  $j \geq 1$  as generic constants, which may vary from line to line.

- Denote  $e_k$  as the  $k$ -th canonical basis vector in Euclidean space (i.e., with only the  $k$ -th entry equal to 1 and others 0), whose dimension depends on each context.  
- For a matrix  $M = \{m_{ij}\}$ , let

$\sigma_{i}(M) =$  the  $i$  -th largest singular value of  $M$

$\| M\|_{\max} = \max_{i,j}|m_{ij}|$ , the maximal absolute value of all entries of  $M$

$\| M\| = \max \{\sigma_i(M^T M)\}^{1 / 2}$ , the spectral norm (Euclidean norm for vectors).

- For a  $d_{1} \times d_{2} \times d_{3}$  tensor  $\mathbf{T}$ , let  $\mathcal{M}_j(\mathbf{T})$  be a  $d_{j} \times (d_{1}d_{2}d_{3}/d_{j})$  matrix by unfolding  $\mathbf{T}$  in the  $j$ -th dimension.

# 4.2 Signal Strengths

Recall the low-rank decomposition of  $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})$  in (2.5) with

$$
\mathbb {E} (\mathbf {A} | \mathbb {L}) = \bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {1} \bar {U} \times_ {2} \bar {U} \times_ {3} \bar {W}
$$

where  $\mathbb{L} = \{\ell_l\}_{l=1}^L$  and the core tensor

$$
\begin{array}{l} \bar {\mathbf {C}} = \mathbf {B} \times_ {1} (\bar {D} \bar {R} ^ {\top}) \times_ {2} (\bar {D} \bar {R} ^ {\top}) \times_ {3} D _ {L} ^ {1 / 2} \\ = \bar {\mathbf {B}} \times_ {1} \bar {D} \times_ {2} \bar {D} \times_ {3} D _ {L} ^ {1 / 2} \\ \end{array}
$$

where

$$
\bar {\mathbf {B}} = \mathbf {B} \times_ {1} \bar {R} ^ {\top} \times_ {2} \bar {R} ^ {\top} \in \mathbb {R} ^ {r \times r \times m} \tag {4.1}
$$

Denote the signal strengths of  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  and  $\bar{\mathbf{B}}$  by  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{C}})$  and  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{B}})$ , respectively, where

$$
\sigma_ {\min } (\mathbf {T}) = \min  \left\{\sigma_ {r _ {j}} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {j} (\mathbf {T})\right): j = 1, 2, 3 \right\} \tag {4.2}
$$

if  $\mathbf{T}$  has Tucker ranks  $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ .

We give a lower bound for the signal strength in the population adjacency tensor. The following conditions can sometimes greatly simplify our presentation.

Condition 1. Assume that

$\bullet$  (A1):  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{B}})\geq c_1p_{\mathrm{max}}$  , where  $p_{max} = \max_{i,j,l}[\mathbb{E}A_l]_{ij}$  
$(A2)$ :  $\bar{D}$  is well-conditioned, i.e.  $\sigma_1(\bar{D}) \leq \kappa_0 \sigma_r(\bar{D})$ .  
- (A3): Minimal network balance condition:  $L_{\min} \asymp L / m$ , where  $L_{\min} = \min_{1 \leq j \leq m} L_j$ .  
- (A4): Maximal network balance condition:  $L_{\max} \asymp L / m$ , where  $L_{\max} = \max_{1 \leq j \leq m} L_j$ .

Lemma 3 (signal strength). If conditions (A1) and (A2) hold, then we have

$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \geq r ^ {- 1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {- 2} \cdot m p _ {\mathrm {m a x}} n \sqrt {L _ {\mathrm {m i n}}}.
$$

Further if (A3) holds and  $m, r, \kappa_0$  are fixed, then

$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \geq C \sqrt {L} n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}.
$$

Condition (A1) is mild since we assumed that  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  has Tucker ranks  $(r,r,m)$  implying that  $\bar{\mathbf{B}}$  also has the same Tucker ranks. By Lemma 3, the signal strength of  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  is characterized by the overall network sparsity.

# 4.3 Incoherence Property

Theoretically, the ideal regularization parameters in Algorithm 1 are

$$
\delta_ {1} = \max _ {j} \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \bar {U} \|, \qquad \mathrm {a n d} \qquad \delta_ {2} = \max _ {j} \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \|.
$$

Incoherence property ensures that singular vectors  $\bar{U}$  and  $\bar{W}$  are not too correlated with or incoherent to the standard basis  $e_j$ 's, as stated in the next lemma, which the sharp convergence rates of regularized tensor power iteration algorithm rely crucially on.

Lemma 4 (Incoherence of  $\bar{U}$  and  $\bar{W}$ ). If conditions (A1) and (A2) hold, we have

$$
\delta_ {1} \leq \kappa_ {0} \sqrt {\frac {r}{n}} \quad \mathrm {a n d} \quad \delta_ {2} \leq \frac {\kappa_ {0} r}{m \sqrt {L _ {\mathrm {m i n}}}}.
$$

Denote  $A \asymp B$  iff  $A = O(B)$  and  $B = O(A)$ . Then under conditions (A1)-(A3), it follows from Lemma 4:

$$
\delta_ {2} \leq C \kappa_ {0} r / \sqrt {m L}.
$$

# 4.4 Tensor incoherent norms and a concentration inequality

Given a random tensor  $\mathbf{A}$ , we write

$$
\mathbf {A} = \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} + (\mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) = \mathrm {t h e s i g n a l} + \mathrm {n o i s e p a r t}.
$$

A tensor norm is needed to measure the size of the noise. In order to deal with extremely sparse tensors, we will adopt the following definition, first introduced in Yuan and Zhang (2017).

Definition 1 (Tensor incoherent norm Yuan and Zhang (2017)). For  $\delta \in (0,1]$  and  $k = 1,2,3$ , define

$$
\left\| \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} \right\| _ {k, \delta} := \sup _ {\mathbf {U} \in \mathcal {U} _ {k} (\delta)} \left\langle \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}, \mathbf {U} \right\rangle
$$

where  $\mathcal{U}_k(\delta) := \{u_1 \otimes u_2 \otimes u_3 : \| u_j \|_{\ell_2} \leq 1, \forall j; \| u_k \|_{\ell_\infty} \leq \delta\}$ , and  $\| u \|_{\ell_p}$  is the  $l_p$ -norm of  $u$ .

We now present a concentration inequality for tensor incoherent norms for sparse random tensors, which is essential in proving Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 later.

Theorem 1 (A concentration inequality for tensor incoherent norm). Suppose that  $L \leq n$  and  $Lnp_{max} \geq \log n$ . Denote  $n_1 = n_2 = n$  and  $n_3 = L$ . Then for  $k = 1, 2, 3$ , we have

$$
\mathbb {P} \left\{\| \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} \| _ {k, \delta} \geq 3 t \right\} \leq \frac {2}{n ^ {2}} + 1 0 (\log n) ^ {2} \lceil \log_ {2} \delta^ {2} n _ {k} \rceil \left[ \exp \left(- \frac {t ^ {2}}{C _ {3} p _ {m a x}}\right) + \exp \left(- \frac {3 t}{C _ {4} \delta}\right) \right]
$$

provided

$$
t \geq \max \left\{C _ {1}, C _ {2} \delta \sqrt {n _ {k}} \log (n) \right\} \sqrt {n p _ {m a x}} \log (\delta^ {2} n _ {k}) \log (n).
$$

We make several remarks concerning the inequality.

1. The bound in Theorem 1 is sharper than that in Yuan and Zhang (2017), in order to deal with extremely sparse networks. Analogous results were previously established for sparse hypergraph networks (Ke et al. (2019)), however, the dimension sizes ( $n$  and  $L$ ) in our model can be drastically different from (Ke et al. (2019)), which needs more careful treatments.

2. Clearly, if  $\delta = 1$ ,  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}\|_{k,\delta}$  reduces to the standard tensor operator norm  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}\|$ . From Lemma 3, the signal strength is of the order  $\sqrt{L} np_{\mathrm{max}}$ . By using the standard operator norm to control  $\mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$ , the success of power iterations then requires, with high probability, that

$$
\sqrt {L} n p _ {\max } \geq C _ {1} ^ {\prime} \| \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} \|. \tag {4.3}
$$

Under the condition  $Lnp_{\mathrm{max}} \gg 1$ , it is easy to check (by the maximum number of nonzero entries on the fibers of  $\mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$ ) that  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A} \| \gg 1$  with high probability (see (Lei et al., 2019, Theorem 2)). Consequently, by using the standard tensor operator norm to control the size of noise part, condition (4.3) requires that  $\sqrt{Lnp_{\mathrm{max}}} \gg 1$  rather than the optimal condition  $Lnp_{\mathrm{max}} \gg 1$ . Indeed, if  $\delta_1 = O(1 / \sqrt{n})$  and  $\delta_2 = O(1 / \sqrt{L})$ , Theorem 1 shows that  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A} \|_{1,\delta_1}, \| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A} \|_{3,\delta_2} = O_p(\sqrt{np_{\mathrm{max}}})$  up to some logarithmic factor. It can be much smaller than  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A} \| \gg 1$  (w.h.p.) when  $L$  is large.

3. To apply tensor incoherent norms to analyze the convergence property of power iterations, it is necessary to prove that  $\{\widehat{U}^{(t)}\}$  and  $\{\widehat{W}^{(t)}\}$  are incoherent. It is possible to generalize the methods in Koltchinskii and Xia (2016); Xia and Zhou (2019); Xia and Yuan (2019b); Cai et al. (2019a) for this purpose whose actual proof can be very involved. For simplicity, we adopt an auxiliary regularization step (3.1) to truncate those singular vectors.

# 5 Main results

# 5.1 Error Bound of Regularized Power Iteration

Theorem 2 states that regularized power iteration method (Algorithm 1) works if we have a warm initialization and a strong enough signal-to-noise ratio. These conditions are typically required (see, e.g., Zhang and Xia (2018); Xia et al.  $(2020+)$ ; Ke et al. (2019); Xia and Yuan (2019a)) and generally unavoidable (Zhang and Xia (2018)).

For  $\widehat{V}, V \in \mathbb{O}_{p,r} = \{V \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times r}: V^T V = I_r\}$ , the distance between their column spaces is

$$
\mathrm {d} (\hat {V}, V) := \inf  _ {O \in \mathbb {O} _ {r, r}} \| \hat {V} - V O \|
$$

Define

$$
\mathrm {E r r} (t) = \max \{\mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(t)}, \bar {U}), \mathrm {d} (\widehat {W} ^ {(t)}, \bar {W}) \}.
$$

We have the following result.

Theorem 2 (General convergence results of regularized power iterations). Assume that

- the initializations  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  and  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  are warm, i.e.,  $\mathrm{Err}(0) \leq 1/4$ ,  
- the signal strength of  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  satisfies

$$
\frac {\sigma_ {\mathrm {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}{\sqrt {r \wedge 2 m}} \geq \Big (C _ {1} + C _ {2} \big ((\delta_ {1} \sqrt {n}) \vee (\delta_ {2} \sqrt {L}) \big) \log n \Big) \log \big (\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n \vee \delta_ {2} ^ {2} L \big) \sqrt {n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} \log (n).
$$

Then with probability at least  $1 - 2n^{-2}$

1. for all  $t \leq t_{\max}$ , we have

$$
\operatorname {E r r} (t) \leq \frac {1}{2} \cdot \operatorname {E r r} (t - 1) + C _ {3} \frac {\sqrt {n p _ {\max} \log n} + \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {\min} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}
$$

2. for  $t \geq t_{\max} = C \log \left(\sigma_{\min}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}) / (\sqrt{np_{\max}} + \delta_1 \delta_2)\right)$  iterations, we have

$$
\mathrm {E r r} (t _ {\mathrm {m a x}}) \leq C _ {3} \frac {\sqrt {n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}} \log n} + \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {\mathrm {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}.
$$

Theorem 2 holds true on general tensor structures. Specializing Theorem 2 to the mixture multi-layer network model (Section 2) yields the following corollary.

Corollary 1. Assume that (A1)-(A3) hold. Further assume

- the initializations  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  and  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  are warm, i.e.,  $\mathrm{Err}(0) \leq 1/4$ ,  
- the signal strength of  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}$  satisfies

$$
\sqrt {L n p _ {\max}} \geq \left(C _ {1} + C _ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} (r / \sqrt {m}) \log (n)\right) r \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} \log (\kappa_ {0} r) \log (n). \tag {5.1}
$$

Then with probability at least  $1 - 2n^{-2}$ , after at most  $t_{\mathrm{max}} = O(\log (n))$  iterations,

$$
\mathrm {E r r} (t _ {\mathrm {m a x}}) \leq C _ {3} \frac {\kappa_ {0} ^ {2} r}{\sqrt {m}} \cdot \sqrt {\frac {\log n}{L n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}}.
$$

By Corollary 1, if  $\kappa_0,r,m$  are fixed and the network sparsity satisfies  $Lnp_{\mathrm{max}}\geq C\log^4 n$  then

$$
\operatorname {E r r} \left(t _ {\max }\right) = O _ {p} \left(\sqrt {\log (n) / \left(L n p _ {\max }\right)}\right). \tag {5.2}
$$

The convergence rate (5.2) is optimal up to the logarithmic factor.

# 5.2 Consistency of recovering global memberships

Recall from Section 2 that the global community structure is denoted as  $\mathcal{V} = \cup_{k=1}^{\bar{K}}\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k$  with disjoint communities  $\{\bar{\mathcal{V}}_j\}_{j=1}^{\bar{K}}$  where node  $i_1$  and  $i_2$  belong to the same global community if and only if  $(e_{i_1} - e_{i_1})^\top \bar{Z} = 0$ . In the TWIST algorithm, after applying the K-means to the rows of  $\widehat{U}$ , we get the vertices' global membership  $\widehat{\widehat{\mathbb{V}}} = \{\widehat{\widehat{\mathcal{V}}}_k, k \in [\bar{K}]\}$ .

We measure the performance by the Hamming error of clustering:

$$
\mathcal {L} (\widehat {\bar {\mathbb {V}}}, \bar {\mathbb {V}}) = \min _ {\tau : \mathrm {a p e r m u t a t i o n o n} [ \bar {K} ]} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \mathbf {1} \big (i \in \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k}, i \notin \widehat {\bar {\mathcal {V}}} _ {\tau (k)} \big)
$$

where  $\bar{\mathbb{V}} = \{\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k,k\in [\bar{K} ]\}$

Theorem 3 (Consistency of global clustering). Assume that (A1)-(A4) hold, and that  $\min_k |\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k| \asymp n / \bar{K}$ . Then with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$ , we have

$$
n ^ {- 1} \cdot \mathcal {L} (\widehat {\bar {\mathbb {V}}}, \bar {\mathbb {V}}) \leq C _ {3} \kappa_ {0} ^ {6} \frac {r ^ {2} \log n}{L n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}
$$

provided that the network sparsity satisfies

$$
\sqrt {L n p _ {\max}} \geq \left(C _ {1} \sqrt {\bar {K}} + C _ {2} \kappa_ {0} r \log (n)\right) \left(\kappa_ {0} ^ {3} r / \sqrt {m}\right) \log (\kappa_ {0} r) \log (n), \tag {5.3}
$$

From Theorem 3, it follows that the relative clustering error is  $O_p\big(\log^{-3}(n)\big)$  when  $\bar{K}, m, \kappa_0$  are bounded. Therefore, vertices' global memberships can be consistently recovered under near optimal network sparsity conditions.

We now compare our method with some other available ones in the literature.

- A special case when  $m = 1$  was considered by Lei et al. (2019), who showed that their algorithm is able to consistently recover the communities if  $np_{\mathrm{max}}\sqrt{L} \gg \log^{3/2} n$ . On the other hand, our result deals with more general mixture multi-layer model, is computationally more efficient, and requires weaker network sparsity:  $np_{\mathrm{max}}L \gg \log^4(n)$ . The dependence of  $L$  in (5.3) is optimal, if we ignore the logarithmic term. This improvement is due to a sharper concentration inequality of  $\mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$  in terms of tensor incoherent norm.  
- A joint matrix factorization method (Co-reg) was proposed in Paul and Chen (2017) for a special case with  $m = 1$  and different  $B_{j}$ s, in which they proved that their method can consistently recover the vertices memberships if  $Lnp_{\max} \gg \log n$  and the signal strengths of  $B_{j}$ s are similar. Their network sparsity condition is similar to (5.3) above up to the logarithmic factor. On the other hand, our approach differs from Paul and Chen (2017) in several aspects. Our method can perform vertices clustering and network clustering simultaneously when  $m > 1$ . Computationally, Paul and Chen (2017) employed a BFGS algorithm to solve the non-convex programming, which is more computationally intensive than TWIST. More will be discussed later in the paper after Lemma 5.

# 5.3 Network classification

We now show that the standard K-means algorithm on  $\widehat{W}$  can consistently uncover the network classes of  $L$  layers under the near optimal network sparsity condition (5.3). Further under a slightly stronger network sparsity condition (5.4), we can apply Algorithm 2 to exactly recover the layer labels with high probability. This shows that more layers will provide more information about layer structure and be very helpful in exact clustering of networks. Similarly, we denote

$$
\mathcal {L} (\hat {\mathbb {L}}, \mathbb {L}) = \min _ {\tau : \mathrm {p e r m u t a t i o n o f} [ m ]} \sum_ {l = 1} ^ {L} \mathbf {1} (\ell_ {l} \neq \tau (\hat {\ell} _ {l})).
$$

Theorem 4 (Consistency and exact recovery of network classes). Let  $\widetilde{\mathbb{L}} = \{\tilde{\ell}_l\}_{l=1}^L$  be the output of the standard  $K$ -means algorithm applied to  $\widehat{W}$ .

1. Under the same conditions in Theorem 3, we have, with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$

$$
L ^ {- 1} \cdot \mathcal {L} \big (\widetilde {\mathbb {L}}, \mathbb {L} \big) \leq C _ {3} \kappa_ {0} ^ {4} \frac {(r ^ {2} / m) \log n}{L n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}
$$

where  $\mathbb{L} = \{\ell_l\}_{l = 1}^L$

2. We further assume

$$
\sqrt {L} n p _ {\max } \geq C _ {1} m ^ {- 1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {5} r ^ {5 / 2} \log \left(r \kappa_ {0}\right) \log^ {5 / 2} (n). \tag {5.4}
$$

Then with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ , we have

$$
\mathcal {L} (\widehat {\mathbb {L}}, \mathbb {L}) = 0
$$

where  $\widehat{\mathbb{L}} = \{\hat{\ell}_l\}_{l=1}^L$  is the output of Algorithm 2 with parameters  $m$  and  $\varepsilon \in [c_1, c_2] \sqrt{m / L}$ .

By Theorem 4, in the case  $r,m,\kappa_0 = O(1)$ , Algorithm 2 is capable to exactly recover the network classes  $\mathbb{L} = \{\ell_l\}_{l = 1}^L$  with appropriately chosen parameter  $\varepsilon$  if the network sparsity (5.4) satisfies  $\sqrt{L} np_{\mathrm{max}}\gg \log^{5 / 2}n$ . On the other hand, consistent network clustering requires, by (5.3), network sparsity  $Lnp_{\mathrm{max}}\gg \log^4 n$ . Therefore, condition (5.4) is stronger with respect to the number of layers  $L$ .

Remark 1. Clustering of networks is essentially a problem of clustering or classifying high-dimensional data. Without exploring network structures, a simple method for clustering high-dimensional data is by spectral clustering on the left singular vectors of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})$  which is a highly rectangular matrix. A simple fact is that  $\| \mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A})\| = O_p(n\sqrt{p_{\max}})$  implying that a naive spectral clustering on  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})$  requires very strong condition of network sparsity for consistent network clustering. The cause of such a sub-optimality is due to the ignorance of matrix structures of rows of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})$ .

# 5.4 Consistency of local clustering

After obtaining the network classes, it suffices to apply spectral clustering on  $\sum_{l:\hat{\ell}_l = j}A_l$  for all  $j\in [m]$  to recover the local memberships  $\mathbb{V}^j = \{\mathcal{V}_k^j\}_{k = 1}^{K_j}$ . Its consistency can be directly proved by existing results in the literature (see, e.g., Lei and Rinaldo (2015)). We hereby omit the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 5. Suppose that the conditions of Theorem 4 and (5.4) hold. For all  $j \in [m]$ , let  $\widehat{\mathbb{V}}^j = \{\hat{\mathcal{V}}_k^j\}_{k=1}^{K_j}$  denote the output of  $K$ -means algorithm on  $\sum_{l: \hat{\ell}_l = j} A_l$ . If  $\sigma_{\min}(B_j) \geq c_1 p_{\max}$  for some absolute constant  $c_1 > 0$  and  $|\mathcal{V}_k^j| \asymp n / K_j$  for all  $k \in [K_j]$ , then with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ ,

$$
n ^ {- 1} \cdot \mathcal {L} (\widehat {\mathbb {V}} ^ {j}, \mathbb {V} ^ {j}) \leq C _ {1} m ^ {- 1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {4} \cdot \frac {K _ {j} ^ {2} \log n}{L n p _ {\max}}.
$$

# 5.5 Warm initialization for regularized power iteration

An important condition for the success of Algorithm 1 is the existence of warm initialization

$$
\operatorname {E r r} (0) = \max \{\mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(0)}, \bar {U}), \mathrm {d} (\widehat {W} ^ {(0)}, \bar {W}) \} \leq 1 / 4.
$$

In this section, we introduce a spectral method for initializing  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  by summing up all the layers of networks. After that, we initialize  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  by taking the left singular vectors of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})(\widetilde{U}^{(0)} \otimes \widetilde{U}^{(0)})$  where  $\widetilde{U}^{(0)} = \mathcal{P}_{\delta_1}(\widehat{U}^{(0)})$ . The following lemma shows that these initializations are indeed close to the truth under reasonable conditions.

Lemma 5 (Initialization). Let  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  denote the top- $r$  left singular vectors of  $\sum_{l=1}^{L} A_l$  and let  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  be the top- $r$  left singular vectors of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})(\widetilde{U}^{(0)} \otimes \widetilde{U}^{(0)})$  where  $\widetilde{U}^{(0)} = \mathcal{P}_{\delta_1}(\widehat{U}^{(0)})$  with  $\delta_1 = \max_j \|e_j^\top \bar{U}\|$ . Then with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ ,

$$
\mathrm {d} \left(\widehat {U} ^ {(0)}, \bar {U}\right) \leq \min  \left\{\frac {C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}}} \log^ {2} n}{\sigma_ {r} \left(\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {d} _ {L} / L\right) ^ {1 / 2}\right)}, 2 \right\} \tag {5.5}
$$

where  $\mathbf{d}_L = (L_1,\dots ,L_m)$ . If  $\delta_{1} = O(\sqrt{r / n})$  and

$$
\sigma_ {r} \left(\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {d} _ {L} / L\right) ^ {1 / 2}\right) \geq 4 C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max }} \log^ {2} n, \tag {5.6}
$$

then with same probability,

$$
\mathrm {d} (\widehat {W} ^ {(0)}, \bar {W}) \leq \min \Bigl \{\frac {C _ {4} \sqrt {m r n p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r)}{\sigma_ {\min} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}, 2 \Bigr \}.
$$

It is possible to improve  $\log^2 n$  to  $\sqrt{\log n}$  in (5.5) as in Paul and Chen (2016). Comparing the rate of initialization (5.5) and the rate after regularized power iterations in Theorem 2, Algorithm 1 improves the estimation error by a ratio of  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{C}})$  and  $\sigma_r\big(\bar{\mathbf{C}}\times_3(\mathbf{d}_L / L)^{1 / 2}\big)$ . In special cases, such an improvement can be significant. For instance, consider  $m = r = 2$  and  $L_{1} = L_{2}$  and  $\bar{\mathbf{C}}\in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2\times 2}$  with

$$
\bar {C} (:,:, 1) = \left( \begin{array}{c c} 1 + \varepsilon & 0 \\ 0 & 1 + \varepsilon \end{array} \right) \quad \text {a n d} \quad \bar {C} (:,:, 2) = \left( \begin{array}{c c} 0 & 1 - \varepsilon \\ 1 - \varepsilon & 0 \end{array} \right)
$$

for some small number  $\varepsilon \in (0,1)$ . It is easy to check that  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}) = \sigma_2\bigl (\mathcal{M}_3(\bar{\mathbf{C}})\bigr) = \sqrt{2} (1 - \varepsilon)$ . On the other hand,

$$
\sigma_ {2} \big (\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {d} _ {L} / L\right) ^ {1 / 2} \big) = \sqrt {2} \varepsilon .
$$

Moreover, if  $\varepsilon = 0$ , then  $\bar{\mathbf{C}} \times_3 (\mathbf{d}_L / L)^{1/2}$  is rank deficient implying that simply projecting the multi-layer networks into a graph can potentially cause serious information loss. See more details in Ke et al. (2019) and a similar discussion in Lei et al. (2019).

It is worthwhile pointing out that one could use other methods to initialize  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  (the initialization of  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  is easy once it is done for  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$ ). Examples include the HOSVD by extracting the top- $r$  left singular vectors of  $\mathcal{M}_1(\mathbf{A})$ , the joint matrix factorization method in Paul and Chen (2017), and random projection Ke et al. (2019).

# 6 Simulation studies

We conduct several simulations to test the performance of the TWIST on the MMSBM with different choices of network sparsity, "out-in" ratio, number of layers and the size of each layer. We use K-means as the clustering algorithm. The evaluation criterion is the mis-clustering rate. All the experiments are replicated 100 times and the average performance across the repetitions is reported.

We generate the data according to the MMSBM in Section 2 in the following fashion. The underlying class  $\ell_l$  for each layer  $l$  is generated from the multinomial distribution with  $\mathbb{P}(\ell_l = j) = 1 / m$ ,  $j = 1, \dots, m$ . The membership  $z_i^j$  for each node  $i$  in layer type  $j$  is generated from the multinomial distribution with  $\mathbb{P}(z_i^j = s) = 1 / K$ ,  $s = 1, \dots, K$ . We choose the connection matrix as  $B = pI + q(\mathbf{11}^T - I)$ , where  $\mathbf{1}$  is a  $K$ -dimensional vector with all elements being 1. Let  $\alpha = q / p$  be the out-in ratio.

# 7 Real data analysis

In this section, we apply the TWIST to two real data sets: worldwide food trading networks and Malaria parasite genes networks. The two datasets have been studied in the literature before. However, with the TWIST, we are able to make some new, interesting, and sometimes surprising findings, which the earlier methods have failed to do so.

# 7.1 Malaria parasite genes networks

Var gene sequences are characterized by pronounced mosaicism, precluding the use of traditional phylogenetic tools. Larremore et al. # 8 Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we have proposed a novel mixture multi-layer stochastic block model (MMSBM) to capture the intrinsic local as well as global community structures. A tensor-based algorithm, TWIST, was proposed to conduct community detection on multi-layer networks and shown to have near optimal error bounds under weak conditions in the MMSBM framework. In particular, the method allows for very sparse networks in many layers. The proposed method outperforms other state of the art methods both in nodes community detection and layers clustering by extensive simulation studies. We also applied the algorithm to two real dataset and found some interesting results.

A number of future directions are worth exploring. As a natural extension, one can generalize the tensor-based representation to account for adjacency matrices capturing the degree heterogeneity of nodes. The layers of networks could have the spacial and temporal structures of networks in many real applications, one could incorporate these into the model. On a more theoretical level, it is of interest to explore theoretical properties in more sparse scenario. It is also important to develop scalable algorithms which can handle millions nodes with thousands layers in this big data era.

# 9.1 Proof of Lemma 1

Write  $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{B}\times_{1}\bar{Z}\times_{2}\bar{Z}\times_{3}W$  , then

$$

$$
\begin{array}{l} = \bar {Z} \left( \right.\left(\begin{array}{c c c c c}0 _ {K _ {1}}&&&&\\&\ddots&&\\&\underbrace {0 _ {K _ {\ell_ {l} - 1}}} _ {\ell_ {l} - 1}&B _ {\ell_ {l}}&&\\&&0 _ {K _ {\ell_ {l} + 1}}&&\\&&\underbrace {0 _ {K _ {m}}} _ {m - \ell_ {l}}\end{array}\right) \bar {Z} ^ {T} \\ = Z _ {\ell_ {l}} B _ {\ell_ {l}} Z _ {\ell_ {l}} ^ {T} = \mathbb {E} (A _ {l} | \ell_ {l}). \\ \end{array}

# 9.2 Proof of Lemma 2

It immediately implies the claim.

# 9.3 Proof of Lemma 3

By the definition of  $\bar{\mathbf{B}}$ , it is clear that  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}) \geq \sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{B}}) \cdot \sigma_r^2(\bar{D}) \sqrt{L_{\mathrm{min}}}$ . Recall that  $\bar{D}$  consists of the singular values of  $\bar{Z}$ . It is obvious by definition that  $\| \bar{Z} \|_{\mathrm{F}} = nm$ . Therefore,  $\sigma_r^2(\bar{D}) \geq \kappa_0^{-2} r^{-1} \cdot nm$  which concludes the proof.

# 9.4 Proof of Lemma 4

Recall that  $\mathcal{M}_3\big(\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{A}|\mathbb{L})\big) = \bar{W}\mathcal{M}_3(\bar{\mathbf{C}})(\bar{U}\otimes \bar{U})^\top$ . Therefore,

$$
\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \| \sigma_ {\min} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \leq \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) (\bar {U} \otimes \bar {U}) ^ {\top} \| = \| \mathbb {E} A _ {l} | \ell_ {l} \| _ {\mathrm {F}} \leq n p _ {\max}.
$$

By Lemma 3, we conclude that  $\max_l\| e_l^\top \bar{W}\| \leq \kappa_0^2 r / (m\sqrt{L_{\mathrm{min}}})$ . Similarly, recall that  $\bar{Z} = \bar{U}\bar{D}\bar{R}^{\top}$  is the singular value decomposition of  $\bar{Z}$ . Then,

$$
\| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \bar {U} \| = \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \bar {Z} \bar {R} \bar {D} ^ {- 1} \| \leq \frac {1}{\sigma_ {r} (\bar {D})} \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \bar {Z} \| = \frac {\sqrt {m}}{\sigma_ {r} (\bar {D})} \leq \kappa_ {0} \sqrt {\frac {r}{n}},
$$

where the last inequality is due to  $\sigma_r(\bar{D})\geq \kappa_0^{-1}\sqrt{mn / r}$  by the proof of Lemma 3.

# 9.5 Proof of Theorem 2

For  $t \geq 1$ , we begin with the bound for  $\mathrm{d}(\widehat{U}^{(t)},\bar{U})$  where  $\widehat{U}^{(t)}$  is the output of a regularized power iteration with input  $\widehat{U}^{(t - 1)}$  and  $\widehat{W}^{(t - 1)}$ . Without loss of generality, assume that  $\mathrm{d}(\widehat{U}^{(t - 1)},\bar{U}) \vee \mathrm{d}(\widehat{W}^{(t - 1)},\bar{W}) \leq 1 / 4$ . For all integers  $t \geq 0$ , denote

$$
\mathrm {E r r} _ {t} = \max \Big \{\mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {U}), \mathrm {d} (\widehat {W} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {W}) \Big \}.
$$

By Algorithm 1,  $\widehat{U}^{(t)}$  is the top- $r$  left singular vectors of  $\mathcal{M}_1(\mathbf{A})\big(\widetilde{U}^{(t - 1)}\otimes \widetilde{W}^{(t - 1)}\big)$  where we abuse the notation and denote  $\otimes$  the Kronecker product. By Algorithm 1 and property of regularization (see Keshavan et al. (2010) and Ke et al. (2019)),

$$
\mathrm {d} (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {U}) \leq 2 \sqrt {2} \cdot \mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {U}) \quad \text {a n d} \quad \max _ {j} \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \leq \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1} \tag {9.1}
$$

and

$$
\mathrm {d} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {W}\right) \leq 2 \sqrt {2} \cdot \mathrm {d} \left(\widehat {W} ^ {(t - 1)}, \bar {W}\right) \quad \text {a n d} \quad \max  _ {j} \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \leq \sqrt {2} \delta_ {2}. \tag {9.2}
$$

Write

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\mathbf {A}) \big (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \otimes \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \big) = \mathcal {M} _ {1} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) \big (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \otimes \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \big) + \mathcal {M} _ {1} (\mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) \big (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \otimes \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \big).
$$

Observe that the left singular space of  $\mathcal{M}_1(\mathbb{EA})\big(\widetilde{U}^{(t - 1)}\otimes \widetilde{W}^{(t - 1)}\big)$  is the column space of  $\bar{U}$ . In addition,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sigma_ {r} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \otimes \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right)\right) = \sigma_ {r} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \left(\left(\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) \otimes \left(\bar {W} ^ {\top} \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right)\right)\right) \\ \geq \sigma_ {r} (\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})) \cdot \sigma_ {\min } (\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}) \sigma_ {\min } (\bar {W} ^ {\top} \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}) \geq \sigma_ {r} (\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})) / 4, \\ \end{array}
$$

where we used the fact

$$
\| I - (\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}) (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1) \top} U) \| \leq \frac {\sqrt {2}}{2},
$$

implying that  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{U}^{\top}\widetilde{U}^{(t - 1)})\geq 1 / 2$

We next bound the operator norm of  $\mathcal{M}_1(\mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A})\big(\widetilde{U}^{(t - 1)}\otimes \widetilde{W}^{(t - 1)}\big)$ . For notational simplicity, denote  $\pmb{\Delta} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$ . Write

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} (\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \\ \leq \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| + \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\bar {U} \widetilde {O} _ {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \\ + \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {U} \widetilde {O} _ {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\widetilde{O}_U^{(t - 1)} = \mathrm{argmin}_{O\in \mathbb{O}_r}\| \widetilde{U}^{(t - 1)} - \bar{U} O\|$  and  $\widetilde{O}_W^{(t - 1)} = \mathrm{argmin}_{O\in \mathbb{O}_m}\| \widetilde{W}^{(t - 1)} - \bar{W} O\|$ .

By Lemma 6 (from (Xia et al., 2020+, Lemma 6)), we obtain

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \\ \leq \sqrt {\frac {2 r m}{\operatorname* {m a x} \{r , 2 m \}}} \left\| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \right\| \\ = \sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \cdot \left\| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \right\|. \\ \end{array}
$$

The last term can be sharply bounded via the tensor incoherent norm. Indeed, for any

$$
v _ {1} \in \mathbb {R} ^ {n}, v _ {2} \in \mathbb {R} ^ {r}, v _ {3} \in \mathbb {R} ^ {m} \mathrm {w i t h} \| v _ {j} \| _ {\ell_ {2}} \leq 1, j = 1, 2, 3,
$$

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\langle \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} _ {2} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}, v _ {1} \otimes v _ {2} \otimes v _ {3} \right\rangle \\ = \left\langle \boldsymbol {\Delta}, v _ {1} \otimes \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} v _ {2}\right) \otimes \left(\left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) v _ {3}\right) \right\rangle \\ = \| \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \cdot \left\langle \boldsymbol {\Delta}, v _ {1} \otimes (\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} v _ {2}) \otimes \left(\left((\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}) v _ {3}\right) / \| \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \|\right) \right\rangle \\ \leq \left\| \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \right\| \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {2, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} \\ \end{array}
$$

where we use (9.1) and the fact that

$$
| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} v _ {2} | \leq \| e _ {j} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \leq \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}.
$$

Thus,

$$
\left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \| \leq \sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \cdot \| \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {2, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}}. \right. \tag {9.3}
$$

In the same fashion,

$$
\left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {U} \widetilde {O} _ {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \leq \sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \cdot \| \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {U} \widetilde {O} _ {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {3, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {2}}. \tag {9.4}
$$

Putting together (9.3) and (9.4), we obtain

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \left(\widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)}\right) ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \leq \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \bar {U} ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \bar {W} ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \\ + \sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \cdot \left(\| \widetilde {W} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {W} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {2, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} + \| \widetilde {U} ^ {(t - 1)} - \bar {U} \widetilde {O} _ {U} ^ {(t - 1)} \| \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {3, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {2}}\right). \\ \end{array}
$$

It remains to bound  $\| \mathcal{M}_1(\Delta \times_2\bar{U}^\top \times_3\bar{W}^\top)\|$  where  $\bar{U}$  and  $\bar{W}$  are deterministic singular vectors. Towards that end, a matrix Bernstein inequality could yield a sharp bound. Indeed, write

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} (\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \bar {U} ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \bar {W} ^ {\top}) \right\| \leq \left\| \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \left(A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) - \mathbb {E} A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3})\right) e _ {i _ {1}} \left((\bar {U} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {2}}) \otimes (\bar {W} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {3}})\right) ^ {\top} \right\| \\ + \left\| \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {\left(i _ {1}, i _ {2}\right) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \left(A \left(i _ {2}, i _ {1}, i _ {3}\right) - \mathbb {E} A \left(i _ {2}, i _ {1}, i _ {3}\right)\right) e _ {i _ {2}} \left(\left(\bar {U} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {1}}\right) \otimes \left(\bar {W} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {3}}\right)\right) ^ {\top} \right\| \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\mathfrak{I}_n^2 = \{(i_1,i_2),1\leq i_1 < i_2\leq n\}$ . Clearly, it is equivalent to bound the spectral norm of the sum of independent random matrices. The following bounds are obvious.

$$
\big \| \big (A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) - \mathbb {E} A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \big) e _ {i _ {1}} \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {2}}) \otimes (\bar {W} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {3}}) \big) ^ {\top} \big \| \leq \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2}
$$

and

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \operatorname {V a r} \big (A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) - \mathbb {E} A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \big) \cdot \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {2}}) \otimes (\bar {W} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {3}}) \big) \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {2}}) \otimes (\bar {W} ^ {\top} e _ {i _ {3}}) \big) ^ {\top} \right\| \\ \leq n p _ {\max}. \\ \end{array}
$$

Therefore, by matrix Bernstein inequality (Tropp (2012) and Koltchinskii and Xia (2015)), with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$ ,

$$
\left\| \mathcal {M} _ {1} \left(\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {2} \bar {U} ^ {\top} \times_ {3} \bar {W} ^ {\top}\right) \right\| \leq C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\max} \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n
$$

for some absolute constants  $C_1, C_2 > 0$ . By Davis-Kahan Theorem,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(t)}, \bar {U}) \leq 8 \sqrt {2} \cdot \frac {\sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \cdot \left(\mathrm {d} (\widehat {W} ^ {(t - 1)} , \bar {W}) \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {2 , \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} + \mathrm {d} \big (\widehat {U} ^ {(t - 1)} , \bar {U} \big) \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {3 , \sqrt {2} \delta_ {2}}\right)}{\sigma_ {r} \big (\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \big)} \\ + \frac {C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {r} (\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}))}. \\ \end{array}
$$

By Theorem 1, if  $Lnp_{\max} \geq \log n$ , there exist absolute constants  $C_3, C_4 > 0$  such that

$$
\| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {2, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} \leq C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} \log (n) \log (\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n) + C _ {4} \delta_ {1} \sqrt {n ^ {2} p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n)
$$

and

$$
\| \Delta \| _ {3, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {2}} \leq C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \log (n) \log (\delta_ {2} ^ {2} L) + C _ {4} \delta_ {2} \sqrt {n L p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (\delta_ {2} ^ {2} L)
$$

with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$ .

As a result, we get

$$
\mathrm {d} \left(\widehat {U} ^ {(t)}, \bar {U}\right)
$$

$$
\begin{array}{l} \leq \operatorname {E r r} _ {t - 1} \cdot \frac {\sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \left(C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \log (n) + C _ {4} \sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \left(\left(\delta_ {1} \sqrt {n}\right) \vee \left(\delta_ {2} \sqrt {L}\right)\right) \log^ {2} n\right) \log \left(\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n \vee \delta_ {2} ^ {2} L\right)}{\sigma_ {r} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})\right)} \\ + \frac {C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {r} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {1} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})\right)}. \\ \end{array}
$$

Similarly, under the same event (only  $\| \pmb{\Delta}\|_{2,\delta},\| \pmb{\Delta}\|_{3,\delta}$  and  $\| \mathcal{M}_1(\pmb {\Delta})(\bar{U}\otimes \bar{W})\|$  involve probabilities), the bound holds for  $\mathrm{d}(\widehat{W}^{(t)},\bar{W})$  . To this end, we conclude with

$$
\operatorname {E r r} _ {t} \leq \operatorname {E r r} _ {t - 1} \cdot \frac {\sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \left(C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \log (n) + C _ {4} \sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \left((\delta_ {1} \sqrt {n}) \vee (\delta_ {2} \sqrt {L})\right) \log^ {2} n\right) \log \left(\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n \vee \delta_ {2} ^ {2} L\right)}{\sigma_ {\min} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}
$$

$$
+ \frac {C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {\min } (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}
$$

where we denote  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}) = \min \left\{\sigma_r\bigl (\mathcal{M}_1(\bar{\mathbf{C}})\bigr),\sigma_m\bigl (\mathcal{M}_3(\bar{\mathbf{C}})\bigr)\right\}$ . To guarantee a contraction property, we assume that

$$
\sigma_ {\min } (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \geq 2 \sqrt {r \wedge 2 m} \left(C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max }} \log (n) + C _ {4} \sqrt {n p _ {\max }} \left(\left(\delta_ {1} \sqrt {n}\right) \vee \left(\delta_ {2} \sqrt {L}\right)\right) \log^ {2} n\right) \log \left(\left(\delta_ {1} \vee \delta_ {2}\right) ^ {2} n\right) \tag {9.5}
$$

for some large enough absolute constants  $C_3, C_4 > 0$ . If condition (9.5), then for all  $t = 1, 2, \dots, t_{\max}$ ,

$$
\operatorname {E r r} _ {t} \leq \frac {1}{2} \cdot \operatorname {E r r} _ {t - 1} + \frac {C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {\operatorname* {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}
$$

which holds with probability at least  $1 - 2n^{-2}$  and  $C_1, C_2 > 0$  are also some absolute constants. The above contraction inequality implies that after

$$
t _ {\mathrm {m a x}} = O \Big (\log \big (\sigma_ {\mathrm {m i n}} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) / (\sqrt {n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} + \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2}) \big) \vee 1 \Big)
$$

iterations, with probability at least  $1 - 2n^{-2}$

$$
\operatorname {E r r} _ {t _ {\max }} \leq \frac {C _ {1} \sqrt {n p _ {\max } \log n} + C _ {2} \delta_ {1} \delta_ {2} \log n}{\sigma_ {\min } (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}
$$

for some absolute constants  $C_1, C_2 > 0$  which concludes the proof.

# 9.6 Proof of Thoerem 1

Without loss of generality, we only prove  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}\|_{1,\delta}$ . The spirit of proving  $\| \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}\|_{3,\delta}$  is similar. The main ideal of proving sharp concentration inequality for tensor incoherent norms is a combination of the techniques in Yuan and Zhang (2016, 2017) and Ke et al. (2019) (see also Xia et al.  $(2020+)$ ).

Let  $\mathbf{E}$  be an  $n\times n\times L$  random tensor with each entry being a Rademacher random variable such that for  $\forall i_1,i_2\in [n],i_3\in [L]$

$$
\mathbb {P} \left(E \left(i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}\right) = + 1\right) = \mathbb {P} \left(E \left(i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}\right) = - 1\right) = \frac {1}{2}
$$

and  $\mathbf{E}$  is partially symmetric such that

$$
E (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) = E (i _ {2}, i _ {1}, i _ {3}).
$$

By a standard symmetrization argument (e.g., Giné and Zinn (1984)), we get for any  $t > 0$

$$
\mathbb {P} \left\{\| \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} \| _ {1, \delta} \geq 3 t \right\} \leq \max  _ {u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} (\delta)} \mathbb {P} \{\langle \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \rangle \geq t \} + 4 \mathbb {P} \{\| \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A} \| _ {1, \delta} \geq t \}
$$

where  $\odot$  denotes a Hadamard product of matrices.

We begin with the probabilistic upper bound of  $|\langle \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}, u_1 \otimes u_2 \otimes u_3 \rangle|$ . Fix any  $u_1 \otimes u_2 \otimes u_3 \in \mathcal{U}_1(\delta)$ , we write  $\Delta = \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$  and

$$
\left\langle \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \right\rangle = \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \Delta (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} u _ {1 i _ {1} ^ {\prime}} u _ {2 i _ {2} ^ {\prime}} u _ {3 i _ {3}}
$$

where we denote  $\mathfrak{I}_n^2 = \{(i_1,i_2):1\leq i_1\leq i_2\leq n\}$ . The following bounds are clear.

$$
\left| \left| \Delta (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} u _ {1 i _ {1} ^ {\prime}} u _ {2 i _ {2} ^ {\prime}} u _ {3 i _ {3}} \right| \leq 2 \delta \right.
$$

and

$$
\begin{array}{l} \operatorname {V a r} \Big (\sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {i _ {1}, i _ {2} \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \Delta (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} u _ {1 i _ {1} ^ {\prime}} u _ {2 i _ {2} ^ {\prime}} u _ {3 i _ {3}} \Big) \\ = \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {i _ {1}, i _ {2} \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \mathbb {E} \left(\Delta \left(i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}\right)\right) ^ {2} \left(\sum_ {\left(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}\right) \in \left\{\left(i _ {1}, i _ {2}\right), \left(i _ {2}, i _ {1}\right) \right\}} u _ {1 i _ {1} ^ {\prime}} u _ {2 i _ {2} ^ {\prime}} u _ {3 i _ {3}}\right) ^ {2} \\ \leq \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {i _ {1}, i _ {2} \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} p _ {\max } \cdot 2 \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} u _ {1 i _ {1} ^ {\prime}} ^ {2} u _ {2 i _ {2} ^ {\prime}} ^ {2} u _ {3 i _ {3}} ^ {2} \leq 2 p _ {\max }. \\ \end{array}
$$

By Bernstein inequality, we obtain

$$
\max  _ {u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} (\delta)} \mathbb {P} \{\langle \mathbf {A} - \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \rangle \geq t \} \leq \exp \left(\frac {- t ^ {2}}{8 p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}}}\right) + \exp \left(\frac {- 3 t}{8 \delta}\right).
$$

Now, we are to bound  $\mathbb{P}\{\| \mathbf{E} \odot \mathbf{A} \|_{1,\delta} \geq t\}$ . For notational simplicity, we write  $n_1 \coloneqq n, n_2 \coloneqq n, n_3 \coloneqq L$ . Define a discretized version of  $\mathcal{U}_1(\delta)$  as

$$
\mathcal {U} _ {1} ^ {*} (\delta) := \{u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} (\delta): \| u _ {j} \| _ {\ell_ {2}} \leq c _ {j}, u _ {j} \in \{\pm 2 ^ {k / 2} c _ {j} / \sqrt {2 n _ {j}}, k = 0, 1, \dots , m _ {j} \} ^ {n _ {j}}, \forall j = 1, 2, 3 \}
$$

with  $m_j = \lceil \log_2(\delta^2 n_j) - 1\rceil$  for  $j = 1$  and  $m_j = \lceil \log_2(n_j) - 1\rceil$  for  $j = 2,3$ . By choosing  $1/2 \leq c_1 \leq 1$  and  $1/\sqrt{2} \leq c_j \leq 1$  for  $j = 2,3$ , we can guarantee, for  $u_1 \otimes u_2 \otimes u_3 \in \mathcal{U}_1^*(\delta)$ , that

$$
u _ {1} \in \{\pm 2 ^ {- k / 2}, k = 2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil , \dots , 2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {2} n _ {1}) - 1 \rceil \} ^ {n _ {1}}
$$

and such that for  $j = 2,3$

$$
u _ {j} \in \{\pm 2 ^ {- k / 2}, k = 2, \dots , \lceil \log_ {2} (n _ {j}) - 1 \rceil + 2 \} ^ {n _ {j}}.
$$

It is well known that (see, e.g., Yuan and Zhang (2017))

$$
\begin{array}{l} \| \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A} \| _ {1, \delta} = \max _ {u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} (\delta)} \langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \rangle \leq \frac {2 ^ {3}}{\prod_ {j = 1} ^ {3} c _ {j}} \max _ {u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} ^ {*} (\delta)} \langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \rangle \\ \leq 2 ^ {5} \max  _ {u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} ^ {*} (\delta)} \left\langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \right\rangle \\ \end{array}
$$

implying that it suffices to bound  $\max_{u_1\otimes u_2\otimes u_3\in \mathcal{U}_1^* (\delta)}\langle \mathbf{E}\odot \mathbf{A},u_1\otimes u_2\otimes u_3\rangle$

A simple fact of cardinality bound is  $\log |\mathcal{U}_1^* (\delta)|\leq 4(2n\vee L)\leq 8n$  where we assume  $L\leq n$ . For a sharper union bound, define, for  $\forall \mathbf{U}\in \mathcal{U}_1^* (\delta)$ , that

$$
A _ {k} (\mathbf {U}) = \{(i _ {2}, i _ {3}): | u _ {2 i _ {2}} u _ {3 i _ {3}} | = 2 ^ {- k / 2} \}
$$

for  $\forall k = 4,\dots ,\lceil \log_2(n_2) - 1\rceil +\lceil \log_2(n_3) - 1\rceil +4$  and

$$
B _ {k, s} (\mathbf {U}) = \{i _ {1}: (i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \in A _ {k} (U), \Omega (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \neq 0, | u _ {1 i _ {1}} | = 2 ^ {- s / 2} \}
$$

for  $\forall s = 2 + \lceil \log_2(\delta^{-2})\rceil ,\ldots ,2 + \lceil \log_2(\delta^{-2})\rceil +\lceil \log_2(\delta^2 n_1) - 1\rceil$  and where  $\Omega$  denotes the support of  $\mathbf{A}$ , i.e.,  $\Omega (i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}) = \mathbf{1}(\mathbf{A}(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}) > 0)$ .

Moreover, for a positive integer  $k^{\star}$  (whose value is determined later), define

$$
S _ {2, 3, k ^ {\star}} (\mathbf {U}) = \{(i _ {2}, i _ {3}): | u _ {2 i _ {2}} u _ {3 i _ {3}} | \leq 2 ^ {- k ^ {\star} / 2 - 1 / 2} \}.
$$

For notational simplicity, let us omit the dependence of  $A_{k}(\mathbf{U})$ ,  $B_{k,s}(\mathbf{U})$ ,  $S_{2,3,k^{\star}}(\mathbf{U})$  on  $\mathbf{U}$  when no confusion occurs.

For  $\forall \mathbf{U} \in \mathcal{U}_1^*(\delta)$  and  $\forall k^\star \in \mathbb{N}_+$ , the following decomposition holds

$$
\begin{array}{l} \langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3} \rangle = \langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, u _ {1} \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {S _ {2, 3, k ^ {*}}} (U _ {2, 3}) \rangle \\ + \sum_ {4 \leq k \leq k ^ {\star}} \sum_ {s = 2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil} ^ {2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {2} n _ {1}) - 1 \rceil} \left\langle \mathbf {E} \odot \mathbf {A}, \mathcal {P} _ {B _ {k, s}} (u _ {1}) \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}) \right\rangle \\ \end{array}
$$

where we write  $U_{2,3} \coloneqq u_2 \otimes u_3$ . The notation  $\mathcal{P}_C(u)$  is the projection operator which projects  $u$  onto the support of  $C$ .

Let us write  $\mathbf{Y} \coloneqq \mathbf{E} \odot \mathbf{A}$  for brevity in the following discussion.

Bounding for  $\langle \mathbf{Y},\mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}(u_1)\otimes \mathcal{P}_{A_k}(U_{2,3})\rangle$  To take the advantage of the sparsity of  $\mathbf{Y}$ , we define the aspect ratio (see, e.g., Yuan and Zhang (2016) and Xia et al.  $(2020+)$ ) for a tensor  $\mathbf{X}\in \mathbb{R}^{n_1\times \ldots \times n_m}$  such that for  $1\leq j_{1} < \ldots < j_{l}\leq m$  and  $1\leq l\leq m$ ,

$$
\nu_ {j _ {1}, \dots , j _ {l}} (\mathbf {X}) = \max _ {i _ {j _ {1}} \in [ n _ {j _ {1}} ], \dots , i _ {j _ {l}} \in [ n _ {j _ {l}} ]} | \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}, \dots , i _ {m}): X (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, \dots , i _ {m}) \neq 0, i _ {k} \in [ n _ {k} ], k \in [ m ] \backslash \{j _ {1}, \dots , j _ {l} \} \} |.
$$

By Chernoff bound (see, e.g., Yuan and Zhang (2016)), we get

$$
\mathbb {P} \{\nu_ {2, 3} (\mathbf {Y}) \geq 1 3 (n _ {1} p _ {\max} + \log n) \} \leq n ^ {- 2}
$$

and

$$
\mathbb {P} \{\nu_ {3} (\mathbf {Y}) \geq 1 3 (n _ {1} n _ {2} p _ {\max} + \log n) \} \leq n ^ {- 2}
$$

Write  $\nu_{2,3}^{\star} = 26\max \{n_1p_{\mathrm{max}},\log n\}$  and  $\nu_{3}^{\star} = 26\max \{n_{1}n_{2}p_{\mathrm{max}},\log n\}$ . Denote the event  $\mathcal{E}_{2,3} = \{\nu_{2,3}(\mathbf{Y})\leq \nu_{2,3}^{\star}\}$  and  $\mathcal{E}_3 = \{\nu_3(\mathbf{Y})\leq \nu_3^\star \}$ . We discuss two cases.

(1) If  $n_1p_{\max} \geq \log n$  (recall that  $n_1 = n$ ), then  $\nu_{2,3}^{\star} = 26n_1p_{\max}$ . Under the event  $\mathcal{E}_{2,3}$ , define

$$
\mathfrak {B} _ {1} (k, l) := \left\{\mathbf {V} = \mathcal {P} _ {B} (u _ {1}) \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}): | A _ {k} | \leq 2 ^ {k - l}, | B | \leq \nu_ {2, 3} ^ {\star} 2 ^ {k - l}, u _ {1} \otimes U _ {2, 3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} ^ {*} (\delta) \right\}
$$

for  $\forall k\in \mathbb{N}$  and  $\forall 0\leq l\leq k$

Then, under  $\mathcal{E}_{2,3}$ , for any  $\mathbf{U} \in \mathcal{U}_1^*(\delta)$ , we have  $\mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}(u_1) \otimes \mathcal{P}_{A_k}(U_{2,3}) \in \mathfrak{B}_1(k,l)$  for some

$l$ . Therefore, we write

$$
\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {4 \leq k \leq k ^ {*}} ^ {2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {2} n _ {1}) - 1 \rceil} \sum_ {s = 2 + \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {- 2}) \rceil} \langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathcal {P} _ {B _ {k, s}} (u _ {1}) \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}) \rangle \\ \leq k^{\star}\lceil \log_{2}(\delta^{2}n_{1})\rceil \max_{\substack{4\leq k\leq k^{\star}\\ 2 + \lceil \log_{2}(\delta^{-2})\rceil \leq s\leq 2 + \lceil \log_{2}(\delta^{-2})\rceil +\lceil \log_{2}(\delta^{2}n) - 1\rceil}}\langle \mathbf{Y},\mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}(u_{1})\otimes \mathcal{P}_{A_{k}}(U_{2,3})\rangle \\ \leq k ^ {\star} \lceil \log_ {2} (\delta^ {2} n _ {1}) \rceil \max  _ {4 \leq k \leq k ^ {\star}} \max  _ {0 \leq l \leq k} \max  _ {\mathbf {V} \in \mathfrak {B} _ {1} (k, l)} \langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathbf {V} \rangle \\ \end{array}
$$

As shown by Yuan and Zhang (2016) Yuan and Zhang (2017),

$$
\log | \mathfrak {B} _ {1} (k, l) | \leq 3 \cdot 2 ^ {(k - l) / 2} \sqrt {4 \nu_ {2 , 3} ^ {\star} (\bigvee_ {j} n _ {j}) \log (\bigvee_ {j} n _ {j})} = 3 \cdot 2 ^ {(k - l) / 2} \sqrt {4 \nu_ {2 , 3} ^ {\star} n \log n}.
$$

Now, we bound  $\sup_{\mathbf{V} \in \mathfrak{B}_1(k,l)} \langle \mathbf{Y}, \mathbf{V} \rangle$ . For  $\forall \mathbf{V} \in \mathfrak{B}_1(k,l)$ ,

$$
\langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathbf {V} \rangle = \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} Y (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} V (i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}, i _ {3}).
$$

Then,

$$
\left|Y(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3})\sum_{(i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime})\in \{(i_{1},i_{2}),(i_{2},i_{1})\}}V(i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},i_{3})\right|\leq 2\| V\|_{\max}\leq 2^{-k / 2 + 1}\delta
$$

and

$$
\begin{array}{l} \operatorname {V a r} \Big (\sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} Y (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} V (i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}, i _ {3}) \Big) \\ = \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} \mathbb {E} \left(A (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) ^ {2} E (i _ {1}, i _ {2}, i _ {3}) ^ {2}\right) \left(\sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} V (i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}, i _ {3})\right) ^ {2} \\ \leq 2 \sum_ {i _ {3} = 1} ^ {L} \sum_ {(i _ {1}, i _ {2}) \in \mathfrak {I} _ {n} ^ {2}} p _ {\max } \sum_ {(i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}) \in \{(i _ {1}, i _ {2}), (i _ {2}, i _ {1}) \}} V (i _ {1} ^ {\prime}, i _ {2} ^ {\prime}, i _ {3}) ^ {2} \leq 2 p _ {\max } \| \mathbf {V} \| _ {F} ^ {2} \leq 2 ^ {- l + 1} p _ {\max } \\ \end{array}
$$

where we use the fact  $\| \mathbf{V}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2} = \left\| \mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}(u_{1})\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\| \mathcal{P}_{A_{k}}(U_{2,3})\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 2^{k - l}\cdot (2^{-k / 2})^{2}\leq 2^{-l}$

By Bernstein's inequality, for each fixed  $\mathbf{V} \in \mathfrak{B}_1(k,l)$ ,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {P} \left\{\langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathbf {V} \rangle \geq \frac {2 ^ {- 5} t}{2 k ^ {\star}} \right\} \leq \exp \left(\frac {- t ^ {2}}{4 \cdot 2 ^ {1 2} k ^ {\star^ {2}} \cdot 2 ^ {- l + 1} p _ {\max}}\right) + \exp \left(\frac {- 3 t}{4 \cdot 2 ^ {6} k ^ {\star} \cdot 2 ^ {- k / 2 + 1} \delta}\right) \\ \leq \exp \left(\frac {- t ^ {2}}{2 ^ {1 5} \cdot 2 ^ {- l} k ^ {\star^ {2}} p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}\right) + \exp \left(\frac {- 3 t}{2 ^ {9} \cdot 2 ^ {- k / 2} k ^ {\star} \delta}\right). \\ \end{array}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathbb {P} \left\{\max  _ {\mathbf {V} \in \mathfrak {B} _ {1} (k, l)} \langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathbf {V} \rangle \geq \frac {2 ^ {- 5} t}{2 k ^ {\star} \log (\delta^ {2} n)} \right\} \\ \leq \exp \left(8 n - \frac {t ^ {2}}{2 ^ {1 5} \cdot 2 ^ {- l} k ^ {\star 2} p _ {\max} \log^ {2} (\delta^ {2} n)}\right) + \exp \left(3 \cdot 2 ^ {(k - l) / 2} \sqrt {4 \nu_ {2 , 3} ^ {\star} n \log n} - \frac {3 t}{2 ^ {9} \cdot 2 ^ {- k / 2} \delta k ^ {\star} \log (\delta^ {2} n)}\right). \\ \end{array}
$$

It turns out that for

$$
t \geq \max \left\{2 ^ {9} k ^ {\star} \sqrt {p _ {\mathrm {m a x}} n} \log (\delta^ {2} n), 2 ^ {1 1} k ^ {\star} \delta \sqrt {\nu_ {2 , 3} ^ {\star} n \log n} \log (\delta^ {2} n) \right\},
$$

we get

$$
\mathbb {P} \left\{\max _ {\mathbf {V} \in \mathfrak {B} _ {1} (k, l)} \langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathbf {V} \rangle \geq \frac {2 ^ {- 5} t}{2 k ^ {\star} \log (\delta^ {2} n)} \right\} \leq \exp \left(- \frac {t ^ {2}}{2 ^ {1 6} k ^ {\star^ {2}} p _ {\max} \log^ {2} (\delta^ {2} n)}\right) + \exp \left(- \frac {3 t}{2 ^ {9} k ^ {\star} \delta \log (\delta^ {2} n)}\right).
$$

Recall that  $\nu_{2,3}^{\star} = 26n_{1}p_{\mathrm{max}}$ . In this case, the condition becomes

$$
t \geq \max  \left\{2 ^ {9} k ^ {\star} \sqrt {p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} n} \log (\delta^ {2} n), 2 ^ {1 1} \sqrt {2 6} k ^ {\star} \delta \sqrt {n ^ {2} p _ {\operatorname* {m a x}} \log n} \log (\delta^ {2} n) \right\}.
$$

(2) If  $n_1p_{\max} < \log n$ , then  $\nu_{2,3}^{\star} = 26\log n$ . In this case, the network can be extremely sparse. Then, we need a sharper analysis for  $\max_{\mathbf{U}\in \mathcal{U}_1^* (\delta)}\langle \mathbf{Y},\mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}(u_1)\otimes \mathcal{P}_{A_k}(U_{2,3})\rangle$  for any positive integers  $k\leq k^{\star}$  and  $s\leq 2 + \log (\delta^{-2}) + \log (\delta^{2}n)$ . Now, define

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathfrak {B} _ {1} (\Omega , k, s, l) := \left\{V = \mathcal {P} _ {B _ {k, s}} (u _ {1}) \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}): | A _ {k} | \leq 2 ^ {k - l}, | B _ {k, s} | \leq \nu_ {2, 3} (\Omega) 2 ^ {k - l} \wedge n \right. \\ , u _ {1} \otimes U _ {2, 3} \in \mathcal {U} _ {1} ^ {*} (\delta) \} \\ \end{array}
$$

for which we need a sharper analysis of the cardinality of  $\mathfrak{B}_1(\Omega, k, s, l)$  with respect to the sparsity of  $\Omega$ .

We start with

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathcal {U} _ {2, 3} (k, l) := \left\{\mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} \left(U _ {2, 3}\right): U _ {2, 3} = u _ {2} \otimes u _ {3}, \left| A _ {k} \right| \leq 2 ^ {k - l} \right. \\ , u _ {j} \in \{\pm 2 ^ {- k / 2}, k = 2, \dots , \lceil \log (n _ {j}) - 1 \rceil + 2 \} ^ {n _ {j}}, j = 2, 3 \}. \\ \end{array}
$$

To investigate the respective sparsity on the fibers of  $\mathcal{P}_{A_k}(U_{2,3})$ , recall the inner product

$$
\left\langle \mathbf {Y}, \mathcal {P} _ {B _ {k, s}} (u _ {1}) \otimes \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}) \right\rangle = \left\langle \mathbf {Y} \times_ {1} \mathcal {P} _ {B _ {k, s}} ^ {\top} (u _ {1}), \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}) \right\rangle
$$

where  $\mathbf{Y} \times_1 \mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}^\top(u_1)$  is a  $n \times L$  matrix. It is easy to check that the sparsity of fibers (row and column vector) of  $\mathbf{Y} \times_1 \mathcal{P}_{B_{k,s}}^\top(u_1)$  is bounded by  $\nu_3(\Omega)$ . As a result, it suffices to restrict to  $\mathcal{P}_{A_k}(U_{2,3})$  whose fiber sparsity is bounded by  $\nu_3(\mathbf{A})$ . See, for instance, (Yuan and Zhang, 2016, Lemma 11).

To this end, define

$$
\nu (\mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3})) := \max _ {i = 1, 2} \nu_ {i} (\mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3})) \leq \nu_ {3} (\Omega)
$$

and

$$
\mathfrak {B} _ {1} ^ {(2, 3)} (\Omega , k, l) := \{V = \mathcal {P} _ {A _ {k}} (U _ {2, 3}): V \in \mathcal {U} _ {2, 3} (k, l), \nu (V) \leq \nu_ {3} (\Omega) \}.
$$

# 9.7 Proof of Thoerem 3

Under the conditions of Corollary 1, we get with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$  that,

$$
\| \widehat {U} - \bar {U} \widehat {O} \| \leq R ^ {\dagger}
$$

with  $\widehat{O} = \operatorname*{argmin}_{O\in \mathbb{O}_r}\| \widehat{U} -\bar{U} O\|$  and  $R^{\dagger} = C_{3}\kappa_{0}^{2}(r / \sqrt{m})\cdot \sqrt{\log n} /\sqrt{Lnp_{\max}}$  for some absolute constant  $C_3 > 0$

Since the rank of  $\widehat{U} -\bar{U}\widehat{O}$  is at most  $2r$  , we have

$$
\left\| \widehat {U} - \bar {U} \widehat {O} \right\| _ {\mathrm {F}} \leq \sqrt {2 r} \left\| \widehat {U} - \bar {U} \widehat {O} \right\| \leq \sqrt {2 r} R ^ {\dagger}.
$$

Write  $\widehat{U} = [\hat{u}_1,\dots ,\hat{u}_n]^\top$  and  $\bar{U} = [\bar{u}_1,\dots ,\bar{u}_n]^\top$ , where  $\hat{u}_j^\top$  and  $\bar{u}_j^\top$  denote the  $j$ -th row of  $\widehat{U}$  and  $\bar{U}$  respectively. Hence

$$
\sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \| \hat {u} _ {i} - \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {u} _ {i} \| ^ {2} = \| \widehat {U} - \bar {U} \widehat {\mathcal {O}} \| _ {\mathrm {F}} ^ {2} \leq 2 r R ^ {\dagger 2}.
$$

We claim that  $\bar{U}$  has  $\bar{K}$  distinct rows. To see this, define a  $\bar{K} \times \bar{K}$  matrix  $Z^{*} = [z_{1}^{*},\dots ,z_{\bar{K}}^{*}]^{\top}$  where  $z_{k}^{*\top} = \bar{Z}(j_{k},:)$  for some  $j_{k} \in \bar{\mathcal{V}}_{k}$ ,  $k \in [\bar{K}]$ . Then by the definition of  $Z^{*}$ , we have

$$
\bar {u} _ {j} = \bar {U} (j,:) = \bar {Z} (j,:) \bar {R} \bar {D} ^ {- 1} = z _ {k} ^ {* \top} \bar {R} \bar {D} ^ {- 1} =: \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*}, j \in \mathcal {V} _ {k}.
$$

It implies that the rows of  $\bar{U}$  in the same global community take the same value. Therefore,  $\{\bar{u}_i\}_{i=1}^n$  can only take  $\bar{K}$  distinct values from  $\{\bar{v}_k^*\}_{k=1}^{\bar{K}}$ .

To investigate the performance of k-means, we first consider putting the  $K$  clustering centers at  $\{\widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_k^*\}_{k = 1}^{\bar{K}}$ . The objective value (within-cluster sum of squares) of K-means algorithm, denoted by  $\mathrm{WCSS}^*$ , is

$$
\mathrm {W C S S} ^ {*} = \sum_ {k = 1} ^ {\bar {K}} \sum_ {j \in \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k}} \| \hat {u} _ {j} - \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} \| ^ {2} = \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {n} \| \hat {u} _ {i} - \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {u} _ {i} \| ^ {2} \leq 2 r R ^ {\dagger 2}. \tag {9.11}
$$

Define the following index set

$$
J = \left\{i \in [ n ]: \| \hat {u} _ {i} - \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {u} _ {i} \| \leq \alpha \sqrt {r} / 3 \right\}
$$

where  $\alpha = \kappa_0^{-1} / \sqrt{nm}$ . Clearly,  $|J^c| (\alpha \sqrt{r} / 3)^2 \leq \sum_{i \in J^c} \| \hat{u}_i - \widehat{O}^\top \bar{u}_i \|^2 \leq 2rR^{\dagger 2}$  and hence

$$
\left| J ^ {c} \right| \leq \frac {1 8 R ^ {\dagger 2}}{\alpha^ {2}}. \tag {9.12}
$$

We now denote the objective value of K-means algorithm on  $\widehat{U}$  by  $\widehat{\mathrm{WCSS}}$ . We make the following claim:

Claim 1. For each  $k \in [\bar{K}]$ , there exists a unique clustering center within a distance of  $\alpha \sqrt{r}$  to  $\widehat{O}^{\top} \bar{v}_k^*$ .

To prove Claim 1, we first prove the existence of such a clustering center. Otherwise, assume for some  $k \in [\bar{K}]$ , K-means algorithm assigns no center within a distance of  $\alpha \sqrt{r}$  to  $\hat{O}^\top \bar{v}_k^*$ . For any  $j \in \bar{\mathcal{V}}_k \cap J$ , denote the closest center to  $\hat{u}_j$  by  $\hat{c}_j$ ,

$$
\| \hat {u} _ {j} - \hat {c} _ {j} \| \geq \| \hat {c} _ {j} - \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {u} _ {j} \| - \| \hat {u} _ {j} - \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {u} _ {j} \| \geq \alpha \sqrt {r} - \frac {\alpha \sqrt {r}}{3} = \frac {2 \alpha \sqrt {r}}{3}.
$$

By the conditions of Theorem 3,  $|\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k| \gtrsim n / \bar{K}$ . By (9.12), we get  $|\bar{\mathcal{V}}_k\backslash J|\leq 18R^{\dagger 2} / \alpha^2$ . Then,

$$
\left| \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k} \cap J \right| = \left| \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k} \right| - \left| \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k} \backslash J \right| \gtrsim \frac {n}{\bar {K}} - \frac {1 8 R ^ {\dagger 2}}{\alpha^ {2}} = O \left(\frac {n}{\bar {K}}\right)
$$

where we use condition (5.3). Hence,

$$
\widehat {\mathrm {W C S S}} \geq \left| \bar {\mathcal {V}} _ {k} \cap J \right| \cdot \| \hat {u} _ {j} - \hat {c} _ {j} \| ^ {2} \gtrsim \frac {n}{\bar {K}} \cdot \left(\frac {2 \alpha \sqrt {r}}{3}\right) ^ {2} \gtrsim \frac {n r \alpha^ {2}}{\bar {K}} = \frac {C n r}{\bar {K}} \cdot \frac {1}{\kappa_ {0} ^ {2} m n}.
$$

However, (9.11) implies that  $\mathrm{WCSS}^* \leq 2rR^{\dagger 2} = o(r / (\kappa_0^2 m\bar{K}))$  under condition (5.3), which is a contradiction. This proves the existence of such clustering centers.

Next for any  $k \neq l$ , by Lemma 2,

$$
\| \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} - \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {l} ^ {*} \| = \| \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} - \bar {v} _ {l} ^ {*} \| \geq \frac {1}{\sigma_ {1} (\bar {D})}
$$

and recall that

$$
\sigma_ {1} (\bar {D}) \leq \kappa_ {0} \sigma_ {r} (\bar {D}) \leq \frac {\kappa_ {0} \| \bar {Z} \| _ {\mathrm {F}}}{\sqrt {r}} \leq \kappa_ {0} \sqrt {\frac {n m}{r}}
$$

implying that  $\| \widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_k^* -\widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_l^*\| \geq 3\alpha \sqrt{r}$ . Then one clustering center cannot be within a distance of  $\alpha \sqrt{r}$  to two different clusters  $\widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_k^*$  and  $\widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_l^*$  at the same time. Therefore, for each  $k\in [\bar{K} ]$ , the clustering center within a distance of  $\alpha \sqrt{r}$  to  $\widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_k^*$  is unique. This completes the proof for Claim 1.

Now denote the clustering centers (in Claim 1) that minimize the K-means objective by  $\{\hat{v}_k\}_{k=1}^{[\bar{K}]}.$  Then for  $\forall j \in \bar{\mathcal{V}}_k \bigcap J$

$$
\| \hat {u} _ {j} - \hat {v} _ {k} \| \leq \| \hat {u} _ {j} - \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} \| + \| \widehat {\mathcal {O}} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} - \hat {v} _ {k} \| \leq \frac {\alpha \sqrt {r}}{3} + \alpha \sqrt {r} \leq \frac {4 \alpha \sqrt {r}}{3}.
$$

For any  $l \neq k$ ,  $\| \widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_{k}^{*} - \hat{v}_{l}\| \geq \| \bar{v}_{k}^{\star} - \bar{v}_{l}^{\star}\| - \| \hat{v}_{l} - \widehat{O}^{\top}\bar{v}_{l}^{*}\|\geq 3\alpha \sqrt{r} -\alpha \sqrt{r} = 2\alpha \sqrt{r}$ , then

$$
\| \hat {u} _ {j} - \hat {v} _ {l} \| \geq \| \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} - \hat {v} _ {l} \| - \| \widehat {O} ^ {\top} \bar {v} _ {k} ^ {*} - \hat {u} _ {j} \| \geq 2 \alpha \sqrt {r} - \frac {\alpha \sqrt {r}}{3} \geq \frac {5 \alpha \sqrt {r}}{3}.
$$

Then  $\hat{u}_j$  can only be assigned to the center  $\hat{v}_k$ , which indicates that nodes in  $J$  are all correctly clustered and those wrongly clustered can only happen in  $J^c$ . Therefore we conclude by (9.12) that

$$
| J ^ {\mathrm {c}} | \leq \frac {1 8 R ^ {\dagger 2}}{\alpha^ {2}} = C _ {1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} (n m) \cdot R ^ {\dagger 2} = C _ {1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {6} \frac {r ^ {2} n \log n}{L n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}
$$

for some absolute constant  $C_1 > 0$ . Therefore,

$$
n ^ {- 1} \cdot \mathcal {L} (\widehat {\bar {\mathbb {V}}}, \bar {\mathbb {V}}) \leq n ^ {- 1} | J ^ {\mathrm {c}} | \leq \frac {C _ {1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {6} r ^ {2} \log n}{L n p _ {\max}}.
$$

# 9.8 Proof of Thoerem 4

The proof of the first claim is identical to the proof of Theorem 3 by observing that

$$
\left\| \left(e _ {i _ {1}} - e _ {i _ {2}}\right) ^ {\top} \bar {W} \right\| \geq c _ {1} \sqrt {\frac {m}{L}} \quad \text {i f} \quad \ell_ {i _ {1}} \neq \ell_ {i _ {2}} \tag {9.13}
$$

for some absolute constants  $c_{1}\in (0,1)$ . We only prove the second claim.

Without loss of generality, denote  $\widehat{W}$  the left singular vectors of

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbf {A}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) = \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U})
$$

where  $\pmb{\Delta} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$  and  $\max_j\| e_j^\top \widetilde{U}\| \leq \sqrt{2}\delta_1$  and (by Corollary 1) with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$ ,

$$
\mathrm {d} (\widetilde {U}, \bar {U}) \leq C _ {3} \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} \cdot \frac {(r / \sqrt {m}) \sqrt {\log n}}{\sqrt {L n p _ {\max}}}
$$

where  $C_3 > 0$  is some absolute constant. Write

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) = \bar {W} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \otimes (\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \big).
$$

By the fact  $\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\bar{U}^{\top}\widetilde{U})\geq 1 / \sqrt{2}$  (under the lower bound condition of  $\sqrt{Lnp_{\mathrm{max}}}$ ), we have

$$
\sigma_ {m} \Big (\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \otimes (\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \big) \Big) \geq \sigma_ {m} \big (\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \big) / 2 \geq \frac {n \sqrt {L m} p _ {\max}}{2 r \kappa_ {0} ^ {2}}
$$

where the last inequality is due to Lemma 3. Denote the thin singular value decomposition of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbb{EA})(\widetilde{U}\otimes \widetilde{U})$  by

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) = \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) \big ((\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \otimes (\bar {U} ^ {\top} \widetilde {U}) \big) = \bar {W} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {K} ^ {\top}
$$

where  $\widetilde{D}$  is an  $m\times m$  diagonal matrix and  $\widetilde{K}\in \mathbb{O}_{(2r)\times m}$ . Meanwhile, denote the thin SVD of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\mathbf{A})(\widetilde{U}\otimes \widetilde{U})$  by

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbf {A}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) = \widehat {W} \widehat {D} \widehat {K} ^ {\top}
$$

where  $\widehat{D}$  is an  $m\times m$  diagonal matrix and  $\widehat{K}\in \mathbb{O}_{(2r)\times m}$ . Therefore,

$$
\widehat {W} \widehat {D} \widehat {K} ^ {\top} = \bar {W} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {K} ^ {\top} + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}).
$$

Recall that  $\max_j\| e_j^\top \widetilde{U}\| \leq \sqrt{2}\delta_1\leq \kappa_0\sqrt{2r / n}$ . By Theorem 1 and Lemma 6, with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ ,  $\left\| \mathcal{M}_3(\pmb {\Delta})(\widetilde{U}\otimes \widetilde{U})\right\| \leq \sqrt{r\wedge 2m}\| \pmb {\Delta}\|_{1,\sqrt{2}\delta_1}\leq C_2\kappa_0\sqrt{mrnp_{\mathrm{max}}}\log^2 (n)\log (r\kappa_0)$

for some absolute constant  $C_2 > 0$ . On the same event, by Davis-Kahan theorem, there exists an orthonormal matrix  $\widetilde{O}_1, \widetilde{O}_2 \in \mathbb{O}_r$  so that

$$
\max \left\{\| \widehat {K} - \widetilde {K} \widetilde {O} _ {2} \|, \| \widehat {W} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1} \| \right\} \leq C _ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {3} r ^ {3 / 2} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0}) / \sqrt {L n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}.
$$

and as a result

$$
\| \widehat {D} - \widetilde {O} _ {1} ^ {\top} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {O} _ {2} \| \leq C _ {3} \kappa_ {0} \sqrt {m r n p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0}).
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \widehat {W} = \bar {W} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {K} ^ {\top} \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} \\ = \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1} (\widetilde {O} _ {1} ^ {\top} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {O} _ {2}) (\widetilde {K} \widetilde {O} _ {2}) ^ {\top} \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} \\ \end{array}
$$

implying that

$$
\widehat {W} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1} = \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1} \big ((\widetilde {O} _ {1} ^ {\top} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {O} _ {2}) (\widetilde {K} \widetilde {O} _ {2}) ^ {\top} \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} - I _ {m} \big) + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\pmb {\Delta}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1}.
$$

Therefore, for any  $l\in [L]$

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} (\widehat {W} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1}) \right\| \\ \leq \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \right\| \cdot \left\| \left(\widetilde {O} _ {1} ^ {\top} \widetilde {D} \widetilde {O} _ {2}\right) \left(\widetilde {K} \widetilde {O} _ {2}\right) ^ {\top} \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} - I _ {m} \right\| + \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta}) \left(\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}\right) \widehat {K} \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} \right\| \\ \leq \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \| \cdot \frac {C _ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {3} r ^ {3 / 2} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0})}{\sqrt {L n p _ {\max}}} + \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\pmb {\Delta}) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \| \cdot \| \widehat {D} ^ {- 1} \|. \\ \end{array}
$$

To bound the last term, write

$$
\begin{array}{l} \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \right\| \leq \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\bar {U} \otimes \bar {U}) \right\| + \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) \big (\bar {U} \otimes (\widetilde {U} - \bar {U} \hat {O}) \big) \right\| \\ + \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) \big ((\widetilde {U} - \bar {U} \widehat {O}) \otimes \widetilde {U} \big) \right\| \\ \end{array}
$$

where  $\widehat{O} = \operatorname*{argmin}_{O\in \mathbb{O}_r}\| \widehat{U} -\bar{U} O\|$ . Since  $\max_l\| e_l^\top \bar{U}\| \leq \delta_1$  and  $\max_l\| e_l^\top \widehat{U}\| \leq \sqrt{2}\delta_1$ ,

$$
\begin{array}{l} \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\widetilde {U} \otimes \widetilde {U}) \| \leq \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\bar {U} \otimes \bar {U}) \| + 2 \sqrt {2 m} \| \Delta \| _ {1, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} \cdot \mathrm {d} (\widehat {U}, \bar {U}) \\ \leq \left\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\Delta) (\bar {U} \otimes \bar {U}) \right\| + C _ {3} \kappa_ {0} ^ {3} r ^ {3 / 2} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0}) \sqrt {\log (n) / L} \\ \end{array}
$$

where the last inequality is due to Corollary 1 and Theorem 1. By Bernstein inequality, it is easy to get that

$$
\max _ {l} \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\pmb {\Delta}) (\bar {U} \otimes \bar {U}) \| \leq C _ {3} r \sqrt {p _ {\max} \log n} + C _ {4} \delta_ {1} ^ {2} \log n \leq C _ {3} ^ {\prime} r \sqrt {p _ {\max} \log n}
$$

which holds with probability at least  $1 - n^{-2}$  and where the last inequality holds for  $\delta_1 = O(\kappa_0\sqrt{r / n})$  and  $Lnp_{\mathrm{max}} \geq \log n$ .

As a result, under the condition (5.1), for all  $l \in [L]$

$$
\begin{array}{l} \big \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} (\widehat {W} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1}) \big \| \leq \| e _ {l} ^ {\top} \bar {W} \| \cdot \frac {C _ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {3} r ^ {3 / 2} \log^ {2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0})}{\sqrt {L n p _ {\max}}} + \frac {C _ {3} r ^ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} \sqrt {\log n}}{n \sqrt {L m p _ {\max}}} \\ + \frac {C _ {4} \kappa_ {0} ^ {5} r ^ {5 / 2} \log^ {5 / 2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0})}{L n \sqrt {m} p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} \\ \leq c _ {1} / 6 \cdot \sqrt {\frac {m}{L}} + \frac {C _ {3} r ^ {2} \kappa_ {0} ^ {2} \sqrt {\log n}}{n \sqrt {L m p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}}} + \frac {C _ {4} \kappa_ {0} ^ {5} r ^ {5 / 2} \log^ {5 / 2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0})}{L n \sqrt {m} p _ {\mathrm {m a x}}} \\ \end{array}
$$

where the constant  $c_{1}$  is the same constant in (9.13). Therefore, if condition (5.1) holds and for some large enough constant  $C_{1} > 0$

$$
\sqrt {L} n p _ {\mathrm {m a x}} \geq C _ {1} m ^ {- 1} \kappa_ {0} ^ {5} r ^ {5 / 2} \log^ {5 / 2} (n) \log (r \kappa_ {0}),
$$

then for all  $l\in [L]$

$$
\| e _ {l} ^ {\top} (\widehat {W} - \bar {W} \widetilde {O} _ {1}) \| <   c _ {1} / 5 \cdot \sqrt {m / L}
$$

with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ . On this event, if  $\ell_{i_1} = \ell_{i_2}$  for  $i_1 \neq i_2 \in [L]$ , then  $\|(e_{i_1} - e_{i_2})^\top \widehat{W}\| < 2c_1 / 5 \cdot \sqrt{m / L}$ . On the other hand, if  $\ell_{i_1} \neq \ell_{i_2}$ , then  $\|(e_{i_1} - e_{i_2})^\top \widehat{W}\| > 3c_1 / 5 \cdot \sqrt{m / L}$ . It suggests that if  $\varepsilon \in [0.4c_1\sqrt{m / L}, 0.6c_2\sqrt{m / L}]$ , then Algorithm 2 with parameter  $\varepsilon$  and  $m$  can exactly recover the network classes.

# 9.9 Proof of Lemma 5

We begin with  $\mathrm{d}(\widehat{U}^{(0)},\bar{U})$ . By definition,  $\widehat{U}^{(0)}$  are the top- $r$  left singular vectors of

$$
\mathbf {A} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} = \mathbb {E} \mathbf {A} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} + \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top}
$$

where  $\pmb{\Delta} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbb{E}\mathbf{A}$ . Recall the decomposition (2.5),  $\mathbf{A} = \bar{\mathbf{C}}\times_{1}\bar{U}\times_{2}\bar{U}\times_{3}\bar{W}$  and then

$$
\mathbf {A} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} = \bar {U} \big (\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} \bar {W}\right) \big) \bar {U} ^ {\top} + \mathbf {\Delta} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top}.
$$

By definition of  $\bar{W}$ , it is clear that  $\mathbf{1}_L^\top \bar{W} = \sqrt{\mathbf{d}_L}$  where  $\mathbf{d}_L = (L_1, \dots, L_m)$ . Therefore,

$$
\sigma_ {r} \big (\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} \bar {W}\right) \big) = \sigma_ {r} (\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \sqrt {\mathbf {d} _ {L}}).
$$

By definition, it is obvious that  $\| \pmb{\Delta} \times_3 \mathbf{1}_L^\top \| \leq \sqrt{L} \cdot \| \pmb{\Delta} \|_{3,1/\sqrt{L}}$ . By Theorem 1, with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ ,

$$
\left\| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {3} \mathbf {1} _ {L} ^ {\top} \right\| \leq \sqrt {L} \cdot \left\| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \right\| _ {3, 1 / \sqrt {L}} \leq C _ {2} \sqrt {L n p _ {\max }} \log^ {2} n.
$$

Therefore, by Davis-Kahan theorem,

$$
\mathrm {d} (\widehat {U} ^ {(0)}, \bar {U}) \leq \min  \left\{C _ {3} \frac {\sqrt {n p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} n}{\sigma_ {r} (\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} (\mathbf {d} _ {L} / L) ^ {1 / 2})}, 2 \right\}
$$

which holds with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ .

Next, we investigate  $\mathrm{d}(\widehat{W}^{(0)},\bar{W})$ . As shown in Theorem 2, as long as

$$
\sigma_ {r} \left(\bar {\mathbf {C}} \times_ {3} \left(\mathbf {d} _ {L} / L\right) ^ {1 / 2}\right) \geq 4 C _ {3} \sqrt {n p _ {\max }} \log^ {2} n, \tag {9.14}
$$

then the regularization can guarantee  $\mathrm{d}(\widetilde{U}^{(0)}, U) \leq \sqrt{2} \mathrm{d}(\widehat{U}^{(0)}, \bar{U})$  and  $\max_j \| e_j^\top \widetilde{U}^{(0)} \| \leq \sqrt{2} \delta_1$ . Recall that  $\widehat{W}^{(0)}$  are the top- $m$  left singular vectors of

$$
\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbf {A}) \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}\right) = \mathcal {M} _ {3} \left(\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}\right) \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}\right) + \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta}) \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}\right).
$$

As shown in the proof of Theorem 2, under Condition (9.14),

$$
\sigma_ {m} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\mathbb {E} \mathbf {A}) \left(\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}\right)\right) \geq \sigma_ {m} \left(\mathcal {M} _ {3} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})\right) / 4 \geq \sigma_ {\min } (\bar {\mathbf {C}}) / 4.
$$

To bound the operator norm of  $\mathcal{M}_3(\Delta)(\widetilde{U}^{(0)}\otimes \widetilde{U}^{(0)})$ , we use the following lemma ((Xia et al., 2020+, Lemma 6)):

Lemma 6. For a tensor  $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1 \times n_2 \times n_3}$  with multilinear ranks  $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ , the following fact holds for  $j = 1, 2, 3$ :

$$
\| \mathcal {M} _ {j} (\mathbf {A}) \| \leq \| \mathbf {A} \| \sqrt {\frac {(r _ {1} r _ {2} r _ {3}) / r _ {j}}{\max _ {j ^ {\prime} \neq j} r _ {j}}}
$$

By Lemma 6,

$$
\left\| \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta}) (\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}) \right\| = \left\| \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {1} \widetilde {U} ^ {(0) \top} \times_ {2} \widetilde {U} ^ {(0) \top}) \right\| \leq \sqrt {m} \cdot \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \times_ {1} \widetilde {U} ^ {(0) \top} \times_ {2} \widetilde {U} ^ {(0) \top} \|.
$$

By the incoherence property of  $\widetilde{U}^{(0)}$ ,

$$
\left\| \mathcal {M} _ {3} (\boldsymbol {\Delta}) (\widetilde {U} ^ {(0)} \otimes \widetilde {U} ^ {(0)}) \right\| \leq \sqrt {m} \| \boldsymbol {\Delta} \| _ {1, \sqrt {2} \delta_ {1}} \leq C _ {1} \delta_ {1} \sqrt {m n ^ {2} p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n)
$$

where the last inequality, due to Theorem 1, holds with probability at least  $1 - 3n^{-2}$ . By Davis-Kahan theorem (Davis and Kahan (1970)), we obtain

$$
\mathrm {d} \big (\widehat {W} ^ {(0)}, \bar {W} \big) \leq \min \left\{C _ {4} \frac {\delta_ {1} \sqrt {m n ^ {2} p _ {\max}} \log^ {2} (n) \log (\delta_ {1} ^ {2} n)}{\sigma_ {\min} (\bar {\mathbf {C}})}, 2 \right\}
$$

which completes the proof.
