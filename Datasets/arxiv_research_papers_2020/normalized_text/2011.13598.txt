# beamforming design for multiuser urllc with finite blocklength transmission

shiwen he, zhenyu an, jianyue zhu, jian zhang, yongming huang, and yaoxue zhang

# abstract

driven by the explosive growth of internet of things (iot) devices with stringent requirements on latency and reliability, ultra-reliability and low latency communication (urllc) has become one of the three key communication scenarios for the fifth generation (5g) and beyond 5g communication systems. in this paper, we focus on the beamforming design problem for the downlink multiuser urllc systems. since the strict demand on the reliability and latency, in general, short packet transmission is a favorable form for urllc systems, which indicates the literature shannon's capacity formula is no longer applicable. with the finite blocklength transmission, the achievable delivery rate is greatly influenced by the reliability and latency. using the developed achievable delivery rate formula for finite blocklength transmission, we respectively formulate the problems of interest as the weighted sum rate maximization, energy efficiency maximization, and user fairness optimization by considering the maximum allowable transmission power and minimum rate requirement. it's worthy pointing out that this is the first work to design the beamforming vectors for the downlink multiuser urllc systems. to address these nonconvex problems, some important insights have been discovered by analyzing the function of achievable delivery rate. for example, the minimum rate requirement can be realized by low bounded the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio. based on the discovered results, we provide algorithms to optimize the beamforming vectors and power allocation, which are guaranteed to converge to a local optimum

# i. introduction

driven by the explosive growth of internet of thing (iot) devices with stringent requirements on latency and reliability, ultra-reliability and low latency communication (urllc) has become a part of the emerging 5th generation (5g) and beyond 5g communication systems. urllc is one of the most innovative technical schemes for the 5g mobile network. practically, the reliability requirement of the factory automation and tele-surgery is  $1 - 10^{-9}$  with end-to-end latency being less than 1 ms [1]. other services, e.g., smart grids, intelligent transportation systems, and process automation, have more relaxed reliability requirements of  $(1 - 10^{-3})\sim (1 - 10^{-6})$  at latencies between 1 ms to 100 ms. the 3rd generation partnership project (3gpp) proposed the general urllc requirement of  $99.999\%$  (block error rate (bler) of  $10^{-5}$ ) for one transmission of a packet, which is with 32 bytes and 1 ms latency [2].

the existing study on coordinated multiuser communication is carried based on the classical shannon capacity [3], [4], which assumes having an arbitrarily low decoding error probability for very long transmission blocklengths. however, in practical urllc systems, a large number of iot devices have to communicate using short bursts of data. in other words, finite blocklength (short-packet) transmission is the typical form for urllc systems, e.g., measurements and control commands for industrial manufacturing and control systems are 10 to 30 bytes [5]. as a result, the literature shannon's capacity formula is no longer applicable in urllc systems due to the finite blocklength transmission [6]. for urllc systems, the decoding error probability is non-negligible due to the impact of the finite blocklength transmission. in a word, three key performance indicators of reliability, latency, and throughput have a fundamental correlation for urllc systems. specifically, in urllc systems, the performance improvement can be realized from three dimensions, i.e., directly reducing latency, directly increasing reliability, and improving the throughput via reducing latency and increasing reliability [7]. the authors in [8]

provided an overview of channel coding techniques for urllc systems and compared them in terms of performance and complexity.

in recent years, the short-packet communication was widely investigated. the achievable rate of finite blocklength transmission is investigated in [9], which is smaller than the shannon rate, with taking into account the decoding error probability, the number of transmitting bits, and the finite blocklength (channels used). the authors in [10] discussed the application of the asymptotic uniform expansion approach for evaluating the achievable bounds in the finite blocklength regime. the authors in [11] introduced the downlink non-orthogonal multiple access (noma) into short-packet communications, where the effective throughput of the user with a higher channel gain was maximized. a wireless-powered iots network with short-packet communication was studied in [12]. the authors in [13] analyzed the impact of finite blocklength secrecy coding on the design of secure transmissions in slow fading channel. the instantaneous and average block error was investigated for the simultaneous information and power transmission urllc relay system [14].

note that the achievable rate for urllc systems is a complicated function and hence the resource allocation problem for urllc systems is tricky. in the literature, significant attention has been devoted to studying and developing resource allocation algorithms enabling urllc, e.g., [15]–[18]. in [15], in a downlink multiuser network operating with finite blocklength codes, the authors proposed optimal power allocation algorithms to maximize the normalized sum throughput under qos constraints. the uplink-downlink bandwidth configuration and delay components were jointly optimized to minimize the total bandwidth [16]. in [17], the authors studied the resource allocation problem for ultra-reliable low-latency edge computing, where the users' power consumption was minimized. in [18], the authors studied the resource allocation for downlink orthogonal frequency division multiple access (ofdma) systems with the objective of maximizing weighted system sum rate for urllc systems. in [19], the authors discussed the principles of wireless access for urllc systems, where massive mimo and multi-connectivity were considered. in [20], the authors studied the problem of dynamic channel allocation for urllc systems while assuming the absence of instantaneous channel state information (csi) at the transmitter. the authors in [21] formulated an energy-efficient packet scheduling problem by adopting the channel capacity formula for finite blocklength.

though a large number of works on the urllc systems have been carried on in the last few years, to the best of our knowledge, there are no existing works investigating the beamforming design problem for urllc multi-antenna multiuser systems. nevertheless, the application of

multi-antenna in urllc systems is necessary for further improving the quality of experiences of user and enhancing the delivery data rate. in this paper, we focus on the beamforming design for multiuser urllc systems and the contributions are listed as follows.

- we first analysis the property of the achievable rate for urllc systems to obtain some key insights. for example, the function of the achievable rate is a non-convex and non-concave function with respect to the signal-to-interference-pus-noise ratio (sinr).  
- the feasibility of the considered problems is studied. practically, if the rate of one of the users approaches zero, the delivery latency is infinite for the user, which is undesirable for urllc systems and leads to the infeasibility of the formulated problems. to avoid the infinite delivery latency, we obtain an analytical condition under which the rate of any user can satisfy its minimum rate requirement and hence the feasibility of the problem is guaranteed.  
- we investigate the beamforming design respectively for weighted sum rate maximization, energy efficiency maximization, and user fairness optimization in urllc systems by considering the maximum allowable transmission power and minimum rate requirement.  
- algorithmic solutions to the formulated problems are provided by using some basic mathematical operations, successive convex approximation method, and the uplink-downlink duality theory.  
- we further discuss the initialization of the provided algorithms by solving the formulated power minimization problem, which greatly improves the efficiency of beamforming design.  
- finally, the proposed solution is evaluated via simulation. it's found that our proposed beamforming design outperforms the classical zero-forcing beamforming (zfbf) algorithm with equal power allocation.

the rest of the paper is organized as follows. section ii describes the urllc system model and the formulated problem. section iii analyzes the properties of the achievable rate of urllc systems. section iv studies the beamforming design respectively for sum rate maximization and energy efficiency maximization. the performance of the proposed beamforming solution is shown in section v via simulation. finally, section vi concludes this paper.

# ii. system model and problem formulation

in this section, we present the system model of the downlink multiuser urllc system, including a multi-antenna base station (bs) and  $k$  single antenna users. the bs is assumed to be equipped with  $n_{\mathrm{t}}$  transmitting antennas and  $k \leq n_{\mathrm{t}}$ . for simplicity, let  $\mathcal{k} = \{1,2,\dots ,k\}$  be the set of users. consequently, the received baseband signal at the  $k$ -th user is given by:

$$
y _ {k} = \sum_ {l \in \mathcal {k}} \sqrt {p _ {l}} \mathbf {h} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {l} s _ {l} + n _ {k}, \tag {1}
$$

where  $\mathbf{w}_k \in \mathbb{c}^{n_t \times 1}$  and  $p_k$  denote the normalized beamforming vector and transmitting power used by the bs for the  $k$ -th user, respectively.  $\mathbf{h}_k \in \mathbb{c}^{n_t \times 1}$  represents the channel coefficient between the bs and the  $k$ -th user.  $s_k$  represents the baseband signal for the  $k$ -th user and  $n_k$  denotes the additive white gaussian noise with  $\mathcal{cn}(0, \sigma_k^2)$  at the  $k$ -th user. thus, the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (sinr) of the  $k$ -th user is calculated as (2) with  $\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k = \frac{\mathbf{h}_k}{\sigma_k}$ .

$$
\gamma_ {k} = \frac {p _ {k} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2}}{\sum_ {l \neq k} p _ {l} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {l} \right| ^ {2} + 1}. \tag {2}
$$

generally speaking, shannon's formula adopted by the existing literature is based on the assumption of the blocklength approaching infinity and the decoding error probability going to zero [3]. namely, it has no ability to capture the relation among the achievable rate, decoding error probability, and transmission latency for the communication system with a finite blocklength

$n$  [9]. here, the blocklength denotes the codeword length or the number of channels used. therefore, in the finite blocklength regime which is restricted to a finite number of channels used, we need to look for a new method to characterize this relation. fortunately, as can be seen in [10, fig. 1] and [22, e4.277], the relation among the achievable rate, decoding error probability, transmission latency, and finite blocklength  $n$  can be described as

$$
r \left(\gamma_ {k}\right) = c \left(\gamma_ {k}\right) - \vartheta \sqrt {v \left(\gamma_ {k}\right)}, \tag {3}
$$

where  $c(\gamma) = \ln (1 + \gamma)$ ,  $\vartheta = \frac{q^{-1}(\epsilon)}{\sqrt{n}}$  with  $\epsilon$  being a desirable decoding error probability,  $q^{-1}(\cdot)$  being the inverse of gaussian q-function  $q(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{x}^{\infty}\exp \left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)dt$ , and  $v(\gamma)$  is defined as

$$
v (\gamma) = 1 - \frac {1}{(1 + \gamma) ^ {2}}. \tag {4}
$$

different from shannon capacity, the achievable rate defined in (3) takes the error probability into account, i.e., the second item in the right-hands of (3).

generally specking, the spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and user fairness are three key indices of performance metrics for wireless communication systems. meanwhile, a general urllc reliability requirement for the transmission of a packet is  $10^{-5}$  for 32 bytes (i.e. the number of transmitting data bits is  $d = 256$  bits) with a user plane latency of 1 ms [7]. motivated by these observations, in this paper, we focus on investigating these three problems for multiuser urllc communication systems by considering the maximum allowable transmission power and minimum rate requirement. firstly, the weighted sum rate maximization (srmax) problem is formulated as

$$
\max  _ {\left\{\mathbf {w} _ {k}, p _ {k} \right\}} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} r \left(\gamma_ {k}\right), \tag {5a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} p _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} ^ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {5b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq r (\gamma_ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {5c}
$$

where  $\alpha_{k}$  denotes the prior of the  $k$ -th user. secondly, the problem of energy efficiency maximization (eemax) is formulated as

$$
\max  _ {\left\{\mathbf {w} _ {k}, p _ {k} \right\}} \frac {\sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} r \left(\gamma_ {k}\right)}{\eta \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} p _ {k} + n _ {\mathrm {t}} p _ {\mathrm {c}} + p _ {0}}, \tag {6a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} p _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} ^ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {6b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq r (\gamma_ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {6c}
$$

where  $\eta \geq 1$  is a constant which accounts for the inefficiency of the power amplifier.  $p_{\mathrm{c}}$  is the constant circuit power consumption per-antenna including power dissipations in the transmit filter, mixer, frequency synthesizer, and digital-to-analog converter, which are independent of the actual transmitting power.  $p_{0}$  is the basic power consumed at the bs and is independent of the number of transmitting antennas [23]. another focus of the urllc system is to ensure that the worst user rate is maximized. namely, the fairness optimization (maxmin) problem is formulated as follows

$$
\max  _ {\left\{\mathbf {w} _ {k}, p _ {k} \right\}} \min  _ {k \in \mathcal {k}} r \left(\gamma_ {k}\right), \tag {7a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} p _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} ^ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {7b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq r (\gamma_ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {7c}
$$

note that in this paper, by properly designing the beamforming vectors, the number of bits received by the  $k$ -th user with decoding error probability  $\epsilon_{k}$  can be determined. it is well known that the weighted srmax, eemax, and fairness (maxmin) problems, i.e., (5), (6), and (7), are non-convex and hard to obtain their solutions for interference channel communication systems. furthermore, compared to shannon formula, the rate expression defined in (3) is more complex, therefore, problems (5), (6), and (7) are also non-convex and difficult to directly obtain their solutions.

# iii. property analysis of function  $r(\gamma)$

in this section, we focus on analyzing function  $r(\gamma) = c(\gamma) - \vartheta \sqrt{v(\gamma)}$  in the range  $\gamma \geq 0$  to obtain some key insights. note that if the achievable rate of one of the users approaches zero, then the delivery latency is infinite for the user, i.e., the system latency of multiuser communication system is also infinite. however, this is undesirable for the urllc system. on the other hand, the user rate is limited by the maximum allowable transmission power  $p$ . based on this observation, we assume that  $0 < \gamma_k \leq \tilde{\gamma}_k = \frac{p\|\mathbf{h}_k^h\|^2}{\sigma_k^2}$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ , where  $\tilde{\gamma}_k$  denotes the sinr of the  $k$ -th user which is obtained via maximum ratio transmission with full power transmission and without suffering any interference. generally speaking, the desirable decoding error probability  $\epsilon$  should be at least smaller than 0.5. in particular, in urllc scenarios, the

desirable decoding error probability  $\epsilon$  should be less than  $10^{-6}$  [7]. note that  $q(0) = 0.5$ , i.e.,  $q^{-1}(\epsilon = 0.5) = 0$ , and  $q(x)$  is a decreasing function with respect to  $x$ . therefore, without loss of generality, we assume that  $q^{-1}(\epsilon) > 0$ .

theorem 1. given  $\vartheta >0$ , function  $r(\gamma)$  is a monotonic decreasing function for  $0\leq \gamma < \nu_{0}$  and is a monotonic increasing function for  $\gamma >\nu_0$ , where  $\nu_{0}$  is given by

$$
\nu_ {0} = \sqrt {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 + 4 \vartheta^ {2}}}{2}} - 1, \tag {8}
$$

and  $r(\nu_0) \leq 0$ .

proof: the first and second order derivation of function  $r(\gamma)$  are calculated as follows:

$$
r ^ {\prime} (\gamma) = \frac {1}{1 + \gamma} \left(1 - \vartheta \frac {1}{(1 + \gamma) \sqrt {(1 + \gamma) ^ {2} - 1}}\right), \tag {9a}
$$

$$
r ^ {\prime \prime} (\gamma) = - \frac {1}{(1 + \gamma) ^ {2}} \left[ 1 - \frac {\vartheta (3 (1 + \gamma) ^ {2} - 2)}{(1 + \gamma) ((1 + \gamma) ^ {2} - 1) ^ {\frac {3}{2}}} \right]. \tag {9b}
$$

let  $r^{\prime}(\gamma) = 0$ , we have

$$
(1 + \gamma) \sqrt {(1 + \gamma) ^ {2} - 1} = \vartheta , \tag {10}
$$

after some basic mathematic operation, the solution to (10) is given as:

$$
\nu_ {0} = \sqrt {\frac {1 + \sqrt {1 + 4 \vartheta^ {2}}}{2}} - 1, \tag {11}
$$

in addition, note that  $r''(\nu_0) > 0$ . this implies that  $\nu_0$  is an extreme small value point of function  $r(\gamma)$ . note that function  $(1 + \gamma)\sqrt{(1 + \gamma)^2 - 1}$  is a monotonic increasing function with respect to  $\gamma > 0$ . therefore, we have

1) when  $0 \leq \gamma < \nu_0$ , we have  $r'(\gamma) < 0$ , i.e., function  $r(\gamma)$  is a monotonic decreasing function.  
2) when  $\nu_0 < \gamma$ , we have  $r'(\gamma) > 0$ , i.e., function  $r(\gamma)$  is a monotonic increasing function. recalling  $r(0) = 0$ , we have  $r(\nu_0) \leq 0$ .

remark 1. from theorem 1, it is not difficult to find that  $r(\gamma) = 0$  with  $\vartheta > 0$  has two solutions. one of the two solutions is  $\nu_{1} = 0$ . note that  $\gamma$  should be larger than the positive solution of  $r(\gamma) = 0$ , which avoids the rate of one of users is less than or equal to zero. but, it is very hard to find the non-zero analytical solution of  $r(\gamma)$  due to its complex expression. in

the sequel, we investigate the solution of  $r(\gamma) = \alpha$  with  $\alpha \geq 0$  and  $\vartheta > 0$  in the range  $\gamma > 0$  to obtain the non-zero solution  $\nu_{2}$  of  $r(\gamma) = 0$ .

theorem 2. when  $\vartheta >0$  is fixed, the solution of  $r(\gamma) = \alpha$  with  $\alpha \geq 0$  in the range  $\gamma >0$  is  $\gamma^{*} = e^{\alpha +\frac{\kappa^{*}}{2}} - 1$ , where  $\kappa^{*} = \mathcal{w}\left(2^{\vartheta , - 2\vartheta}; - 4e^{-2\alpha}\vartheta^{2}\right)$  with  $\mathcal{w}(\iota_1,\iota_2;\mu)$  being given by

$$
\mathcal {w} \left(^ {\iota_ {1}, \iota_ {2}}; \mu\right) = \iota_ {1} - \sum_ {m = 1} ^ {\infty} \frac {1}{m * m !} \left(\frac {\mu m e ^ {- \iota_ {1}}}{\iota_ {2} - \iota_ {1}}\right) ^ {m} \mathcal {b} _ {m - 1} \left(\frac {- 2}{\iota_ {2} - \iota_ {1}}\right), \tag {12}
$$

with  $\mathcal{b}_m(z)$  being defined as

$$
\mathcal {b} _ {m - 1} (z) = \sum_ {k = 0} ^ {m} \frac {(m + k) !}{k ! (m - k) !} \left(\frac {z}{2}\right) ^ {k}. \tag {13}
$$

proof: rewriting  $r(\gamma) = \alpha$  as follows

$$
\ln \left(e ^ {- \alpha} (1 + \gamma)\right) - \vartheta \sqrt {v (\gamma)} = 0. \tag {14}
$$

let  $e^{-\alpha} = \beta$  and  $\ln (\beta (1 + \gamma)) = \eta$ , then, we have

$$
(1 + \gamma) ^ {- 2} = \beta^ {2} e ^ {- 2 \eta}. \tag {15}
$$

in addition, according to the range  $\gamma > 0$  and (14), it is not difficult to find that  $0 < \eta \leq \vartheta$ . furthermore, we can rewrite (15) as follows

$$
\eta = \vartheta \sqrt {1 - \beta^ {2} e ^ {- 2 \eta}}. \tag {16}
$$

after some basic mathematical operations, (16) can be rewritten as

$$
- \frac {1}{\beta^ {2} \vartheta^ {2}} (\eta - \vartheta) (\eta + \vartheta) = e ^ {- 2 \eta}. \tag {17}
$$

belonging to the class of transcendent equation (generalized lambert  $w$  function) [24]-[26]. let  $\kappa = 2\eta$ ,  $0 < \kappa \leq 2\vartheta$ , we can reformulate (17) as follows

$$
e ^ {\kappa} (\kappa - 2 \vartheta) (\kappa + 2 \vartheta) = - 4 \beta^ {2} \vartheta^ {2}. \tag {18}
$$

note that  $-4\beta^{2}\vartheta^{2} < 0$ , recalling  $0 < \kappa \leq 2\vartheta$ , therefore, the solution of (18) belongs to  $0 < \kappa \leq 2\vartheta$ . using the lagrange inversion theorem, the solution of (18) is given by

$$
\kappa^ {*} = \mathcal {w} \left(^ {2 \vartheta , - 2 \vartheta}; - 4 \beta^ {2} \vartheta^ {2}\right), \tag {19}
$$

where  $\mathcal{w}(\iota_1,\iota_2;\mu)$  is defined as follows

$$
\mathcal {w} \left(^ {\iota_ {1}, \iota_ {2}}; \mu\right) = \iota_ {1} - \sum_ {m = 1} ^ {\infty} \frac {1}{m * m !} \left(\frac {\mu m e ^ {- \iota_ {1}}}{\iota_ {2} - \iota_ {1}}\right) ^ {m} \mathcal {b} _ {m - 1} \left(\frac {- 2}{m (\iota_ {2} - \iota_ {1})}\right), \tag {20}
$$

with  $\mathcal{b}_m(z)$  being defined as

$$
\mathcal {b} _ {m} (z) = \sum_ {k = 0} ^ {m} \frac {(m + k) !}{k ! (m - k) !} \left(\frac {z}{2}\right) ^ {k}. \tag {21}
$$

according to (19), it is not difficult to see that the summation term in equation (20) is a nonnegative number, i.e.,  $0 < \kappa^{*} \leq 2\vartheta$ . thus, the solution of  $r(\gamma) = \alpha$  is given by

$$
\gamma^ {*} = e ^ {\alpha + \eta^ {*}} - 1 = e ^ {\alpha + \frac {\kappa^ {*}}{2}} - 1. \tag {22}
$$

the proof is completed.

remark 2. note that  $r(\gamma) = 0$  and  $r(\gamma) = \frac{d}{n} \ln(2)$  are two special cases of  $r(\gamma) = \alpha$  with  $\alpha = 0$  and  $\alpha = \frac{d}{n} \ln(2)$ , respectively. this implies that their solutions can be easily obtained by using the conclusion obtained in theorem 2. in particular, another solution  $\nu_{2}$  of  $r(\gamma) = 0$  is given by

$$
\nu_ {2} = e ^ {\frac {\kappa^ {*}}{2}} - 1. \tag {23}
$$

while, the solution of  $r(\gamma) = \frac{d}{n} \ln (2)$  is calculated as

$$
\nu_ {3} = e ^ {\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) + \frac {\kappa^ {*}}{2}} - 1. \tag {24}
$$

therefore, to guarantee the feasibility of problems (5), (6), and (7), the sinr  $\gamma_{k}$  has to be larger than  $\nu_{3}$ , for  $\forall k\in \mathcal{k}$ , i.e., we have the following corollary.

theorem 3. given  $\vartheta >0$ , function  $r(\gamma)$  has an inflection point  $\nu_{4}$ , i.e., the concave-convex property of function  $r(\gamma)$  changes in  $0 < \gamma < \infty$ .

# iv. beamforming design

in this section, we focus on designing an efficient optimization algorithm to address problems (5), (6), and (7) via some basic mathematical operations and successive convex approximation method [27]. it is well known that the uplink-downlink duality theory has shown that the uplink and downlink communications have the same sinr region for the downlink multiuser communication systems subject to the total power constraint [28], [29]. in the sequel, we revisit to use the uplink-downlink duality theory to address problems (5), (6), and (7) instead of directly solving them. for ease of notation, we let  $q_{k}$  be the transmitting power of the uplink  $k$ -th user and firstly give out the following lemmas.

lemma 1. given the optimal solution  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, q_k^*\}$  for the uplink srmax problem, i.e.,

$$
\max  _ {\left\{\mathbf {w} _ {k}, q _ {k} \right\}} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} \overleftarrow {r} (\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}), \tag {27a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {27b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq \overleftarrow {r} (\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {27c}
$$

where  $\overleftarrow{r} (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k) = c (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k) - \vartheta \sqrt{v (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k)}$  and  $\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k$  is given by

$$
\leftarrow \gamma_ {k} = \frac {q _ {k} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2}}{\sum_ {l \in \mathcal {k} , l \neq k} q _ {l} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {l} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2} + 1}. \tag {28}
$$

then,  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, p_k^*\}$  is the optimal solution to the downlink srmax problem (5), where  $p_k^*$  is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\mathbf{p}^* = [p_1^*, \dots, p_k^*]^t$ , which is calculated as:

$$
\mathbf {p} ^ {*} = \boldsymbol {\psi} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {1}, \tag {29}
$$

with matrix  $\psi$  satisfying

$$
[ \psi ] _ {k, l} = \left\{ \begin{array}{l} \frac {\left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} ^ {*} \right| ^ {2}}{\bar {\gamma} _ {k} ^ {*}}, k = l \\ - \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {l} ^ {*} \right| ^ {2}, k \neq l, \end{array} \right. \tag {30}
$$

where  $\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k^*$  is calculated with  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, q_k^*\}$  via (28) and 1 is an all one vector.

lemma 2. given the optimal solution  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, q_k^*\}$  for the uplink eemax problem, i.e.,

$$
\max  _ {\left\{\mathbf {w} _ {k}, q _ {k} \right\}} \frac {\sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} \overleftarrow {r} \left(\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}\right)}{\eta \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} + n _ {\mathrm {t}} p _ {\mathrm {c}} + p _ {0}}, \tag {31a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {31b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq \overleftarrow {r} (\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {31c}
$$

then,  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, p_k^*\}$  is the optimal solution of the downlink eemax problem (6), where  $p_k^*$  is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\mathbf{p}^* = [p_1^*, \dots, p_k^*]^t$ , which is calculated as (29).

lemma 3. given the optimal solution  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, q_k^*\}$  for the uplink fairness problem, i.e.,

$$
\max  _ {\left. \mathbf {w} _ {k}, q _ {k} \right\}} \min  _ {k \in \mathcal {k}} r \left(\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}\right), \tag {32a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p, \| \mathbf {w} _ {k} \| _ {2} ^ {2} = 1, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {32b}
$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq r (\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {32c}
$$

then,  $\{\mathbf{w}_k^*, p_k^*\}$  is the optimal solution of the downlink maxmin problem (7), where  $p_k^*$  is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\mathbf{p}^* = [p_1^*, \dots, p_k^*]^t$ , which is calculated as (29).

from the expression of the uplink sinr  $\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k$  in (28) and the descriptions of problems (27), (31), and (32), it is not difficult to find that, given the uplink transmitting power  $\{q_k\}$ , solving problems (27), (31), and (32) can resort to maximize  $\overleftarrow{r} (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k)$  with respect to  $\{\mathbf{w}_k\}$ . in the sequel, we focus on addressing problems (27), (31), and (32) rather than directly solving problems (5), (6), and (7) via two-step. in particular, we adopt the block coordinate descent method to design an iterative optimization algorithm to address them. given the uplink transmitting power  $\{q_k\}$ , the optimal solution of  $\{\mathbf{w}_k\}$  for maximizing  $\overleftarrow{r} (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k)$  is the minimum mean square error receiver, i.e.,

$$
\mathbf {w} _ {k} ^ {*} = \frac {\left(\mathbf {i} _ {n _ {\mathrm {t}}} + \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h}\right) ^ {- 1} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k}}{\left\| \left(\mathbf {i} _ {n _ {\mathrm {t}}} + \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h}\right) ^ {- 1} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} \right\|} \tag {33}
$$

where  $\mathbf{i}_{n_{\mathrm{t}}}$  denotes  $n_{\mathrm{t}}$  -by-  $n_{\mathrm{t}}$  identity matrix.

# a. for sr maximization

recalling the conclusions obtained in theorems 1 and 2, i.e., the monotonic increasing property of function  $\widetilde{r} (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k)$  with respect to  $\overleftarrow{\gamma}_k$  in the range  $\nu_{3}\leq \overleftarrow{\gamma}_{k}\leq \tilde{\gamma}_{k},\frac{d}{n}\ln (2)\leq \overleftarrow{r} (\overleftarrow{\gamma}_{k})$  can be rewritten as  $\nu_{3}\leq \overleftarrow{\gamma}_{k},\forall k\in \mathcal{k}$ . accordingly, given the beamforming vector  $\{\mathbf{w}_k\}$ , problem (27) is reformulated as

$$
\max  _ {\left\{q _ {k} \right\}} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} \overleftarrow {r} (\overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}), \tag {34a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p, \nu_ {3} \leq \overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {34b}
$$

before proceeding, to obtain a tractable form of problem (34), we equivalently rewrite it as problem (35) by introducing auxiliary variables  $\varphi_{k},\psi_{k},\phi_{k}$ , and  $\theta_{k},\forall k\in \mathcal{k}$ .

$$
\max  \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} \widetilde {r} \left(\varphi_ {k}, \theta_ {k}\right), \tag {35a}

$$

$$

$$

$$
\leftarrow \gamma_ {k} \leq \phi_ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {35e}
$$

$$
\widetilde {v} \left(\phi_ {k}\right) \leq \psi_ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {35f}
$$

$$
\sqrt {\psi_ {k}} \leq \theta_ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {35g}
$$

$$
\widetilde {v} (\nu_ {3}) \leq \psi_ {k} \leq \widetilde {v} (\widetilde {\gamma} _ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {35h}
$$

$$
\sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p. \tag {35i}
$$

in problem (35), the optimization variables are  $q_{k}$ ,  $\varphi_{k}$ ,  $\psi_{k}$ ,  $\phi_{k}$ , and  $\theta_{k}$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ .  $\widetilde{r}(\varphi_{k},\theta_{k}) = \ln(1 + \varphi_{k}) - \vartheta \theta_{k}$  and  $\widetilde{v}(\phi_{k})$  is given by

$$

at the optimal point of problem (35), inequality constraints (35c) and (35e)-(35g) are activated. it is not difficult to see that problem (35) is non-convex due to the non-convex constraints (35c) and (35e)-(35g). constraints (35c) and (35e) can be equivalently rewritten as

$$
\sum_ {l \neq k} a _ {k, l} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {l} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2} - q _ {k} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2} + \varphi_ {k} \leq 0, \tag {37a}
$$

$$
q _ {k} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2} - \sum_ {l \neq k} b _ {k, l} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {l} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} \right| ^ {2} - \phi_ {k} \leq 0, \tag {37b}
$$

where  $a_{k,l} = \varphi_k q_l$ ,  $b_{k,l} = \phi_k q_l$ ,  $\forall k, l \in \mathcal{k}$ , and  $k \neq l$ . constraint (35f) can be equivalently rewritten as:

$$
\left(1 + \phi_ {k}\right) ^ {2} - \frac {1}{1 - \psi_ {k}} \leq 0. \tag {38}
$$

exploiting the convexity of function  $\frac{1}{1 - \psi_k}$ , the concavity of function  $\sqrt{\psi_k}$ , and the first-order taylor series expansion, we can obtain their low boundary approximations as follows

$$
\frac {1}{1 - \psi_ {k}} \geq \bar {\psi} (\psi_ {k}) \triangleq \frac {1 - 2 \psi_ {k} ^ {(t)}}{\left(1 - \psi_ {k} ^ {(t)}\right) ^ {2}} + \frac {\psi_ {k}}{\left(1 - \psi_ {k} ^ {(t)}\right) ^ {2}}, \tag {39a}
$$

$$
\sqrt {\psi_ {k}} \leq \mu (\psi_ {k}) \triangleq \frac {1}{2} \sqrt {\psi_ {k} ^ {(t)}} + \frac {\psi_ {k}}{2 \sqrt {\psi_ {k} ^ {(t)}}}. \tag {39b}
$$

in (39),  $t$  denotes the  $t$ -th iteration,  $\varphi_k^{(t)}$  and  $\psi_k^{(t)}$  represent the solutions obtained at the  $t$ -th iteration for variables  $\varphi_k$  and  $\psi_k$ , respectively. thus, problem (35) can be reformulated as

$$
\max  \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} \widetilde {r} \left(\varphi_ {k}, \theta_ {k}\right), \tag {40a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} (3 5 \mathrm {b}), (3 5 \mathrm {d}), (3 5 \mathrm {h}), (3 5 \mathrm {i}), (3 7), \tag {40b}
$$

$$
\left(1 + \phi_ {k}\right) ^ {2} - \bar {\psi} (\psi_ {k}) \leq 0, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {40c}
$$

$$
\mu (\psi_ {k}) \leq \theta_ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {40d}
$$

$$
a _ {k, l} = \varphi_ {k} q _ {l}, b _ {k, l} = \phi_ {k} q _ {l}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l. \tag {40e}
$$

in problem (40), the optimization variables are  $q_{k}, \varphi_{k}, \psi_{k}, \phi_{k}, \theta_{k}, a_{k,l}$ , and  $b_{k,l}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal{k}, k \neq l$ . note that the bilinear form constraints in (40e) are non-convex. to overcome these challenges, we rewrite problem (40) into (41) via using mccormick envelopes [30], where  $\tilde{q}_{k}$  be the minimum power for achieving  $\nu_{3}$ .

$$
\max  \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \alpha_ {k} (\ln (1 + \varphi_ {k}) - \vartheta \theta_ {k}), \tag {41a}
$$

$$
s. t. (4 0 b), (4 0 c), (4 0 d), \tag {41b}
$$

$$
\nu_ {3} q _ {l} + \tilde {q} _ {l} \varphi_ {k} - a _ {k, l} \leq \nu_ {3} \tilde {q} _ {l}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41c}
$$

$$
\tilde {\gamma} _ {k} q _ {l} + p \varphi_ {k} - a _ {k, l} \leq p \tilde {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41d}
$$

$$
a _ {k, l} - \tilde {\gamma} _ {k} q _ {l} - \tilde {q} _ {l} \varphi_ {k} \leq - \tilde {\gamma} _ {k} \tilde {q} _ {l}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41e}
$$

$$
a _ {k, l} - p \varphi_ {k} - \nu_ {3} q _ {l} \leq - p \nu_ {3}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41f}
$$

$$
\nu_ {3} q _ {l} + \tilde {q} _ {l} \phi_ {k} - b _ {k, l} \leq \tilde {q} _ {l} \nu_ {3}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41g}
$$

$$
\tilde {\gamma} _ {k} q _ {l} + p \phi_ {k} - b _ {k, l} \leq p \tilde {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41h}
$$

$$
b _ {k, l} - \tilde {\gamma} _ {k} q _ {l} - \tilde {q} _ {l} \phi_ {k} \leq - \tilde {q} _ {l} \tilde {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41i}
$$

$$
b _ {k, l} - p \phi_ {k} - \nu_ {3} q _ {l} \leq - p \nu_ {3}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal {k}, k \neq l, \tag {41j}
$$

in problem (41), the optimization variables are  $q_{k}, \varphi_{k}, \psi_{k}, \phi_{k}, \theta_{k}, a_{k,l}$ , and  $b_{k,l}, \forall k, l \in \mathcal{k}, k \neq l$ . problem (41) is convex and can be easily solved using the classical optimization methods, e.g., primal-dual interior-point methods, with complexity  $\mathcal{o}\left((k^2 + 3k)^{3.5} + n_{\mathrm{t}}^{2.7}\right)$  [31], [32]. the detailed steps of solving problem (21) is outlined in algorithm 1 where  $\xi^{(\tau)}$  and  $\varsigma^{(t)}$  denote the objective values of problem (27) and problem (41) at the  $\tau$ -th and  $t$ -th iterations, respectively.

# algorithm 1 solution of problem (27)

1: initialize beamforming vector  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(0)}$  and  $p_k^{(0)}$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ , such that constraint (5b) and (5c) are satisfied.  
2: compute  $\gamma_{k}$  with  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(0)}$  and  $p_k^{(0)}$  to obtain  $\bar{\gamma}_{k}$ . compute  $\tilde{q}_k$  that is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\tilde{\mathbf{q}} = [\tilde{q}_1, \dots, \tilde{q}_k]^t$ , which is calculated as:

$$
\tilde {\mathbf {q}} = \boldsymbol {\phi} ^ {- 1} \mathbf {1}. \tag {42}
$$

where matrix  $\phi$  is given by

$$
[ \boldsymbol {\phi} ] _ {k, l} = \left\{ \begin{array}{l} \frac {\left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} ^ {(0)} \right| ^ {2}}{\bar {\gamma} _ {k}}, k = l \\ - \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {l} ^ {h} \mathbf {w} _ {k} ^ {(0)} \right| ^ {2}, k \neq l. \end{array} \right. \tag {43}
$$

3: let  $\tau = t = 0$ ,  $\varphi_k^{(0)} = \phi_k^{(0)} = \bar{\gamma}_k$  and  $\psi_k^{(0)} = \widetilde{v}\left(\phi_k^{(0)}\right)$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ . calculate the objective value  $\xi^{(\tau)}$  and  $\varsigma^{(t)}$ .  
4: let  $t \gets t + 1$ . solve problem (41) to obtain  $\xi^{(t)}, q_k^{(t)}, \varphi_k^{(t)}, \psi_k^{(t)}, \phi_k^{(t)}, \theta_k^{(t)}, a_{k,l}^{(t)}$ , and  $b_{k,l}^{(t)}$ ,  $\forall k, l \in \mathcal{k}, k \neq l$ , with  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(l)}$ .  
5: if  $\left|\frac{\varsigma^{(t)} - \varsigma^{(t-1)}}{\varsigma^{(t-1)}}\right| \leq \varepsilon$ , stop iteration. otherwise, go to step 4.  
6: let  $\tau = \tau + 1$ , update  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(\tau)}$  with  $q_k^{(t)}$  and (33), and calculate the objective value  $\xi^{(\tau)}$ . if  $\left|\frac{\xi^{(\tau)} - \xi^{(\tau - 1)}}{\xi^{(\tau - 1)}}\right| \leq \varepsilon$ , stop iteration. otherwise, go to step 4.

s. t. (3 5 b) - (3 5 i). \tag {45b}

3: compute  $\gamma_{k}$  with  $\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(0)}$  and  $p_k^{(0)}$  to obtain  $\bar{\gamma}_k$ . compute  $\tilde{q}_k$  that is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\tilde{\mathbf{q}} = [\tilde{q}_1,\dots ,\tilde{q}_k]^t$  with (42) and (43).  
4: let  $\tau = t = 0$ ,  $\varphi_k^{(0)} = \phi_k^{(0)} = \bar{\gamma}_k$  and  $\psi_k^{(0)} = \widetilde{v}\left(\phi_k^{(0)}\right)$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ . calculate the objective value  $\tilde{\xi}^{(t)}$  and  $\tilde{\varsigma}^{(\tau)}$ .  
5: let  $t \gets t + 1$ . solve problem (46) to obtain  $\tilde{\xi}^{(t)}, q_k^{(t)}, \varphi_k^{(t)}, \psi_k^{(t)}, \phi_k^{(t)}, \theta_k^{(t)}, a_{k,l}^{(t)}$ , and  $b_{k,l}^{(t)}$ ,  $\forall k, l \in \mathcal{k}, k \neq l$ , with  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(l)}$ .  
6: if  $\left|\frac{\tilde{\varsigma}^{(t)} - \tilde{\varsigma}^{(t - 1)}}{\tilde{\varsigma}^{(t - 1)}}\right| \leq \varepsilon$ , stop iteration. otherwise, go to step 5.  
7: if  $\left|\sum_{k\in \mathcal{k}}\alpha_k\tilde{r}\left(\varphi_k^{(t)},\theta_k^{(t)}\right) - \lambda \left[\eta \sum_{k\in \mathcal{k}}q_k^{(t)} + n_tp_c + p_0\right]\right| < \varepsilon$ , then stop iteration. otherwise, let  $\lambda = \frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{k}}\alpha_k\tilde{r}\left(\varphi_k^{(t)},\theta_k^{(t)}\right)}{\eta\sum_{k\in\mathcal{k}}q_k^{(t)} + n_tp_c + p_0}$ , go to step 5.  
8: let  $\tau = \tau + 1$ , update  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(\tau)}$  with  $q_k^{(t)}$  and (33), and calculate the objective value  $\tilde{\xi}^{(\tau)}$ . if  $\left|\frac{\tilde{\xi}^{(\tau)} - \tilde{\xi}^{(\tau - 1)}}{\tilde{\xi}^{(\tau - 1)}}\right| \leq \varepsilon$ , stop iteration. otherwise, go to step 5.

further, problem (47) can be equivalently rewritten as follows

$$
\max  _ {\left\{q _ {k}, \mu \right\}} \mu , \tag {48a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} q _ {k} \leq p, \tag {48b}
$$

$$
\nu_ {3} \leq \overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}, \tag {48c}
$$

$$
\mu \leq \overleftarrow {\gamma} _ {k}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {48d}
$$

it is not difficult to find that problem (48) can be reformulated as a standard geometric programming (gp) problem by jointly using the logarithmic change of the variable and a logarithmic transformation of the objective function and constraints, respectively [35]. in other words, problem (48) can be easily solved by using the powerful gp optimization tool packets [36]. the detailed algorithm for solving problem (32) is summarized as algorithm 3, where  $\bar{\xi}^{(t)}$  denotes the objective value of problem (32) at the  $t$ -th iteration. the convergence of algorithm 3 can

be guaranteed using the monotonic boundary theorem [33]. this is because that a monotonic nondecreasing sequence is generated with the running of algorithm 3.

# algorithm 3 solution of problem (32)

1: initialize beamforming vector  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(0)}$  and  $p_k^{(0)}, \forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ , such that constraints (47b) and (47c) are satisfied.  
2: compute  $\gamma_{k}$  with  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(0)}$  and  $p_k^{(0)}$  to obtain  $\bar{\gamma}_{k}$ . compute  $\tilde{q}_k$  that is the  $k$ -th element of vector  $\tilde{\mathbf{q}} = [\tilde{q}_1,\dots ,\tilde{q}_k]^t$  with (42) and (43).  
3: let  $t = 0$ . calculate the objective value  $\bar{\xi}^{(t)}$ .  
4: let  $t \gets t + 1$ . solve problem (48) to obtain  $\mu^{(t)}, q_k^{(t)}, \forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ , with  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(t)}$ .  
5: update  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(t)}$  with  $q_k^{(t)}$  and (33), and calculate the objective value  $\bar{\xi}^{(t)}$ . if  $\left|\frac{\bar{\xi}^{(t)} - \bar{\xi}^{(t-1)}}{\bar{\xi}^{(t-1)}}\right| \leq \varepsilon$ , stop iteration. otherwise, go to step 4.

# d. feasibility analysis and initialization

to effectively solve problems (5) and (6), another thing that needs to be addressed is to investigate their feasibility. according to the conclusion obtained in theorem 2, the value of sinr  $\gamma_{k}$  must be greater than  $\nu_{3}, \forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ . in what follows, we resort to address the power minimization optimization formulated in (49) to discuss their feasibility.

$$
\min  _ {\{\tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k} \}} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \| \tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k} \| ^ {2} \tag {49a}
$$

$$
\text {s . t .} \nu_ {3} \leq \frac {\left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k} \right| ^ {2}}{\sum_ {l \neq k} \left| \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {l} \right| ^ {2} + 1}, \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {49b}
$$

as the phases of the beamforming vectors  $\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k\}$  do not change the objective nor the constraints. therefore, without loss of generality, we assume that the beamforming vectors  $\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k\}$  satisfy  $\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k^h\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k > 0$  [37]. let  $\mathbf{o} = \left[\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k^h\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_1,\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k^h\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_2,\dots ,\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k^h\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_{k - 1},\overline{\mathbf{h}}_k^h\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k,1\right]$ . thus, problem (49) can be rewritten as follows:

$$
\min  _ {\{\tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k} \}} \sum_ {k \in \mathcal {k}} \| \tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k} \| ^ {2}, \text {s . t .} \| \mathbf {o} \| \leq \sqrt {1 + \frac {1}{\nu_ {3}}} \overline {{\mathbf {h}}} _ {k} ^ {h} \tilde {\mathbf {w}} _ {k}. \tag {50a}
$$

problem (50) can be easily solved by the classical convex optimization tools, such as cvx [38]. let  $\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k^{(*)}\right\}$  and  $p^{(*)}$  be the optimum solution and target value of problem (49), respectively. if

$p^{(*)} \leq p$ , problems (5), (6), and (7) are feasible. otherwise, the two problems are not feasible. furthermore, the initialization of  $\{p_k\}$  and  $\{\mathbf{w}_k\}$  can be achieved by letting  $p_k^{(0)} = \left\| \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k^{(*)} \right\|^2$  and  $\mathbf{w}_k^{(0)} = \frac{\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k^{(*)}}{\|\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k^{(*)}\|}$ ,  $\forall k \in \mathcal{k}$ .

# v. numerical results

in this section, we present numerical results to evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithms for the downlink multiuser urllc communication system with finite blocklength transmission. our simulation model consists of a single multi-antenna bs and  $k$  single antenna users. the channel coefficient  $\mathbf{h}_k$  from the bs to the  $k$ -th user is modeled as  $\mathbf{h}_k = \sqrt{\varrho_k}\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_k$ , where the channel power  $\varrho_{k}$  is given as  $\varrho_{k} = 1 / (1 + (d_{k} / d_{0})^{\varrho})$  with  $d_{k}$  being the distance between the bs and the  $k$ -th user,  $d_0$  and  $\varrho$  respectively denoting the reference distance and the fading exponent. the elements of  $\widetilde{\mathbf{h}}_k$  are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) with  $\mathcal{cn}(0,1)$ . all users have the same noise variance, i.e.,  $\sigma_k^2 = \sigma^2$ ,  $\forall k\in \mathcal{k}$ . for easy of notation, we define the snr as  $\mathrm{snr} = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{p}{\sigma^2}\right)$  in db. other simulation parameters are listed in detail in table ii.

$$

$$
\frac {d}{n} \ln (2) \leq c (\gamma_ {k}), \forall k \in \mathcal {k}. \tag {52c}
$$

note that the problems (51) and (52) can be easily solved with similar methods as described in algorithm 1 and algorithm 3. in addition, the initializations of problems (51) and (52) can be realized via solving (49) with replacing  $\nu_{3}$  with  $\tilde{\nu}_{3} = 2^{\frac{d}{n}} - 1$ .

# d. fairness optimization experiment

experiment v-d discusses performance of algorithm 3 under different qos requirements. traditional processing method using shannon capacity, as the problem studied in (52) is also compared with  $k = 6$ ,  $n = 128$ , and  $\mathrm{snr} = 20$  db. again, channel coefficients under the worst condition ( $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ ) are first generated before the above two algorithms are implemented. the minimum rate for algorithm 3 (mr_alg3) and traditional method using shannon capacity (mr_trad), and an error term mr_error are respectively defined as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {m r} _ {-} \mathrm {a l g} 3 = \log (1 + \gamma) - \frac {q ^ {- 1} (\epsilon)}{\sqrt {n}} \sqrt {\left(1 - \frac {1}{(1 + \gamma)}\right) ^ {2}} \\ \mathrm {m r} _ {-} \operatorname {t r a d} = \log (1 + \gamma) \\ \mathrm {m r} _ {-} \text {e r r o r} = \frac {q ^ {- 1} (\varepsilon)}{\sqrt {n}} \sqrt {\left(1 - \frac {1}{(1 + \gamma)}\right) ^ {2}} \\ \end{array}
$$

in the experiment, mr_alg3 and mr_trad are directly calculated using the obtained beamforming via running algorithm 3 and solving problem (52), respectively. mr_error is calculated using  $\gamma$  which is obtained by using the obtained beamforming via running algorithm 3. fig. 6 illustrates results of mr_alg3, mr_trad and mr_error. mr_alg3 decreases with an decreasing  $\epsilon$ , which indicates that more strict qos requirement reduces transmission capacity. on the contrary, mr_trad remains unchanged since conventional method ignores the error term which could not be eliminated under finite blocklength circumstance. therefore, mr_trad is not an accessible result in practice compared to mr_alg3. relationship among mr_alg3, mr_error and mr_trad, namely, summation of mr_alg3 and mr_error is close to mr_trad in theory, can also be experimentally confirmed in fig. 6.

# vi. conclusions

in this paper, for the downlink urllc multiuser multi-antenna system, we focused on the beamforming design respectively for the weighted sum rate maximization, energy efficiency maximization, and fairness optimization by considering the maximum allowable transmission power and minimum rate requirement. we also analyzed the feasibility of the formulated optimization problem and the feasibility condition avoiding the situation where the rate of one of the users is zero. using some basic mathematical operations, successive convex approximation method, and the uplink-downlink duality theory, we provided effective and efficient optimization algorithms

to solve the formulated problems. simulation results show that our proposed results outperforms zfbf with equal power allocation, especially for the case with small transmission power.
